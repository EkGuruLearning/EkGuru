German Nouns, Gender and Articles

Why every German noun is der, die or das, how to guess the gender, and the plural patterns that cover most words.

Full course. Authored German content, part of the free EkGuru German course. Audio is your browser’s computer voice, not a native recording.

Three genders, no escape

Every German noun has a gender — masculine (der), feminine (die) or neuter (das) — and the gender is a property of the word, not the thing. A girl is das Mädchen (neuter), a turnip is die Rübe (feminine). There is no way around memorising gender with each noun: learn der Tisch (the table), not just Tisch. Dictionaries always list it.

Guessing rules that usually work

Some endings are reliable clues. Words ending in -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion, -tät are almost always feminine (die Zeitung, the newspaper). Words ending in -chen and -lein (diminutives) are always neuter (das Mädchen, the girl). Most words ending in -er that describe people doing something are masculine (der Lehrer, the teacher). Days, months, seasons, compass directions and most car makes are masculine; most flowers, trees and numbers are feminine.

Definite and indefinite articles

The definite article (the) is der / die / das; the indefinite article (a, an) is ein (masculine and neuter) and eine (feminine): ein Mann (a man), eine Frau (a woman), ein Kind (a child). German has no indefinite article in the plural — Kinder alone means "children" or "some children" depending on context. Professions and nationalities after sein (to be) also drop the article: Ich bin Lehrer (I am a teacher).

Plurals: five patterns

German plurals look chaotic but follow five main patterns: add -e (der Tag → die Tage, days), add -er often with an umlaut (das Kind → die Kinder), add -(e)n — the commonest feminine pattern (die Frau → die Frauen), add -s — mostly foreign words (das Auto → die Autos), or change nothing (der Lehrer → die Lehrer). All plurals take die as their definite article, whatever the singular gender.

ArticleUse
derthe — masculine singular (der Mann)
diethe — feminine singular AND all plurals
dasthe — neuter singular (das Kind)
eina — masculine and neuter (ein Tag, ein Auto)
einea — feminine (eine Frau)
kein / keineno, not a — the negative article
