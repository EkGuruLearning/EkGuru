O alfabeto, os acentos e o seu nome

Portuguese writes almost what it says, and the exceptions are the accents. The til makes a vowel nasal (ã, õ), the cedilla keeps a c soft (ç), the acute marks the stressed vowel (é, á) and the circumflex marks a closed one (ê, ô). When someone asks como se escreve?, they want letters, not sounds — double l, c with cedilla, no accent.

Asking and giving a spelling

Como se escreve? asks how a word is written, and the answer is given letter by letter or by naming the change: com acento, sem acento, com dois l, com ç. The customer-service form é escrito com… is also ordinary. Names keep their own spelling, so ask instead of guessing.

Watch out:
  • Como escreve seu nome? → Como se escreve o seu nome? The question needs se: como se escreve.

Family and TER (age)

Meet the family: MÃE, PAI, IRMÃO, IRMÃ. Age uses TER (to have): TENHO 10 ANOS = I have 10 years = I am 10. Ask: QUANTOS ANOS VOCÊ TEM? (How many years do you have?)

A casa: moro em, tem, fica

Three verbs describe a home and only one of them is about you. Eu moro em… puts you there, o apartamento tem… describes what is inside, and o prédio fica… places the building in the street. The same three do the work for a job or a school: trabalho em, a empresa tem, fica no centro.

Describing where you live

Moro em takes the city or the neighbourhood, not the street: moro em Pinheiros, moro em São Paulo. Tem is used with no subject when you list what exists: tem uma cozinha grande. Fica places the building: o prédio fica na esquina. All three stay in the present for a permanent arrangement.

Weather is said two ways and both are correct: está quente (it is hot, with an adjective) and faz calor (it is hot, with a noun). Choose by what follows — faz calor pairs with nouns (calor, frio, sol, vento) and está pairs with adjectives (quente, frio, nublado). Chover and nevar are impersonal: chove muito em janeiro.

Talking about the weather

Use está + adjective for a moment (hoje está frio), and faz + noun for what the day is like (faz frio no inverno). Verbs of weather have no subject: chove, neva, venta. The seasons in Brazil run the other way round from Europe: o verão is December to March, and o inverno is short in the North and real in the South.

Portuguese A1 beyond the drills — culture, reading, listening, idioms

Culture note

Portuguese is the official language of Brazil and of seven other countries, and Brazil alone has more speakers than all the others together — which is why this course teaches the Brazilian variety. Three sounds do most of the work: the nasal vowels written with a til (pão, irmã, não), nh (like the ny in canyon: manhã) and lh (like the lli in million: filho). The everyday word for you is você, and it takes the same verb form as ele, which is why beginners hear fala where Spanish would say hablas.

Source (named, not linked): en.wikipedia.org, the article “Portuguese language”.

The 3 mistakes this level makes

  • Eu sou vinte anos. → Eu tenho vinte anos. Age uses ter (to have), never ser.
  • Eu estou bom hoje. → Eu estou bem hoje. bem is the adverb of how you are; bom is an adjective for a thing or a person.
  • Obrigado! (said by a woman) → Obrigada! (said by a woman) The word agrees with the speaker: a man says obrigado, a woman obrigada.
