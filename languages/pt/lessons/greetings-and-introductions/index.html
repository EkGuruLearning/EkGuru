Portuguese Greetings and Introductions

The greetings that cover most situations

Portuguese greets by time of day: bom dia (good morning), boa tarde (good afternoon) and boa noite (good evening AND good night — it covers both arriving and leaving). The all-purpose hello is olá, with the breezier oi among friends. In Portugal you will also hear bom dia stretched until lunch and adeus as goodbye; in Brazil tchau (from Italian ciao) is the everyday farewell.

Introducing yourself

To give your name, say Meu nome é… (my name is…) or Eu sou… (I am…). Both are natural: Meu nome é Ana and Eu sou a Ana (note the article before names in Portugal — a Ana — usually dropped in Brazil). To ask someone's name: Como você se chama? (formal/neutral) or Como você se chama? everywhere in Brazil. Origin: Eu sou da Índia (I am from India).

Tu vs você — the register choice

For "you", Brazil uses você almost everywhere (conjugated like he/she), while Portugal uses tu with friends and family and você or titles with others. A learner is safe with você in Brazil and polite-but-distant with it in Portugal — where tu marks real familiarity. When unsure in Portugal, dodge the pronoun entirely: Como se chama? works without any "you" at all.

Please, thanks and sorry

Obrigado (said by men) and obrigada (said by women) both mean thank you — the ending matches the SPEAKER, not the listener. Por favor is please; se faz favor is the Portuguese equivalent you hear constantly in Lisbon. Desculpe excuses (sorry, excuse me) and com licença asks permission to pass or interrupt. The reply to thanks is de nada (it's nothing).
