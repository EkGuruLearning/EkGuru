Past and desires

Past: 먹었어요/였어요

Past power! 먹어요→먹었어요 (ate), 가요→갔어요 (went). 입니다→이었어요/였어요: 학생이었어요 (was a student). Time words: 어제 (yesterday), 오늘 (today), 내일 (tomorrow)!

먹→먹었어요, 가→갔어요, 하→했어요. 학생이었어요 (was a student), 아니었어요 (wasn't). 이미 + past = already: 이미 먹었어요!

약속을 잡아요

Making an appointment moves between two question shapes: -(으)ㄹ까요? invites, 어때요? asks how something suits. The future -(으)ㄹ 거예요 states what will happen once the time is agreed.

WordSay itMeaning
약속yaksokappointment, promise
토요일toyoilSaturday
언제eonjewhen
-(으)ㄹ 거예요-(eu)l geoyeyowill (future)
만나다mannadato meet
Proposing and agreeing

어때요? asks whether a suggestion suits; -(으)ㄹ까요? asks the other person to decide with you. The answer can be as short as 좋아요, and the agreed time takes 에: 두 시에 만나요.

토요일 어때요? · 몇 시에 만날까요? · 두 시에 만나요 · 좋아요
WordSay itMeaning
토요일 어때요?toyoil eottaeyo?How is Saturday?
몇 시에 만날까요?myeot si-e mannalkkayo?What time shall we meet?
두 시에 만나요.du si-e mannayo.Let us meet at two.
Watch out:
  • 토요일에 만나요 어때요? → 토요일 어때요? 어때요 is a whole question; do not put another verb in front of it.
  • 일요일에 시간이 없어요. 일요일이 어때요? → 일요일에 시간이 없어요. 일요일은 어때요? The contrastive 은/는 marks the alternative day you are proposing.

Asking for a price

The conditional -면 turns quantity into a bargain: 두 개 사면 얼마예요? asks what two would cost. The request 싸게 해 주세요 works on the price with 하다 — 만들다 would mean making an object, not lowering a number.

Korean A2 beyond the drills — culture, reading, listening, idioms

These sections are written for Korean A2 specifically. The reading text is EkGuru's own; the culture note cites where its facts come from; the idioms and the mistakes list are the ones this level actually trips over.

Culture note

A Korean meal arrives all at once. 반찬, the small side dishes, are refilled without asking at most restaurants, and the rice and soup are yours alone. Because 반찬 and 물 are shared, the polite habit is to fill a neighbour's glass before your own and to leave the last piece alone until somebody offers it. 수저 come in a rolled set, and using the spoon for rice and the chopsticks for 반찬 is the quiet default.

Source (named, not linked): en.wikipedia.org, the article “Korean cuisine”.

The 3 mistakes this level makes

  • 어제 밥을 먹어요. → 어제 밥을 먹었어요. 어제 is past, so the verb carries -았/었-; the present form belongs to 오늘.
  • 물을 마시고 싶어요 있어요. → 물을 마시고 싶어요. 싶어요 ends the sentence — a second verb cannot follow it.
  • 집에 가요 싶어요. → 집에 가고 싶어요. A wish is made on the stem with -고 싶어요, never on the finished form 가요.
