Negation and ability

-지 않다 / -지 못하다

Formal negation! 먹지 않아요 (I don't eat — formal), 가지 않아요. CAN'T: 먹지 못해요 (I can't eat / unable). 안 = casual, -지 않다 = formal writing and speeches. Reason: 왜냐하면…그래서!

WordSay itMeaning

하지 않아요haji an-ayodon't do (formal)
먹지 못해요meokji motaeyocan't eat
가지 못해요gaji motaeyocan't go

-지 않다 (won't) · -지 못하다 (can't)

안 먹어요 = don't eat (casual), 먹지 않아요 = don't eat (formal). 먹지 못해요 = CAN'T eat (unable). 못 = unable, 안 = unwilling!

  • Using -지 않다 with friends — friends: 안!

Can and plan: -(으)ㄹ 수 / -(으)려고

Ability! -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 (can): 할 수 있어요, 먹을 수 있어요. Can't: 수 없어요. Plans: -(으)려고 해요 (I plan to): 가려고 해요! Ability + plans = B1 power!

약속을 지키지 못했을 때

Missing an appointment needs one sentence, not two: -지 못해서 carries the failure into the apology. To ask for a new time, use -(으)ㄹ 수 있을까요? with a reason attached.

WordSay itMeaning
사정sajeongcircumstances, a situation
늦다neutdato be late
지키다jikidato keep (a promise)
미루다mirudato postpone
-(으)ㄹ 수 있을까요?-(eu)l su isseulkkayo?could you / would it be possible to
Apology and repair

-지 못하다 is cannot, -지 않다 is do not; an apology explains the failure with 못해서, not with 않아서. The request for a new time is softened by -(으)ㄹ까요?, and the reason precedes it.

WordSay itMeaning
약속을 지키지 못해서 죄송합니다.yaksok-eul jikiji motaeseo joesonghamnida.I am sorry I could not keep the appointment.

Watch out:
  • 약속을 지키지 않았습니다 미안합니다. → 약속을 지키지 못해서 미안합니다. -지 못해서 joins the failure to the apology in one sentence.

Korean B1 beyond the drills — culture, reading, listening, idioms

These sections are written for Korean B1 specifically. The reading text is EkGuru's own; the culture note cites where its facts come from; the idioms and the mistakes list are the ones this level actually trips over.

Culture note

Korean has six speech levels, and the two that matter first are 해요체 (-아요/어요) and 합니다체 (-습니다/입니다). Neither is more polite than the other in the abstract: 해요체 is warm and standard in conversation, 합니다체 is the register of announcements, news and first meetings. Age and workplace rank decide which one, and -(으)시- marks the person you are talking about rather than the person you are talking to.

Source (named, not linked): en.wikipedia.org, the article “Korean honorifics”.

Reading — read this aloud, slowly

우리 회사는 작지만 일이 많습니다. 저는 매일 아침 회의 자료를 만들고 오후에는 고객과 통화합니다. 요즘 새로운 일을 배우느라 바쁘지만 재미있습니다. 지난주에는 팀장님께 제 의견을 말씀드렸습니다. 처음에는 긴장했지만 자료를 준비한 덕분에 잘 설명했습니다. 팀장님은 좋은 생각이라고 하셨습니다. 다음 달에는 제가 발표를 맡을 것입니다.

What it says: Our company is small, but there is a lot of work. Every morning I prepare the material for the meeting and in the afternoon I talk with customers. These days I am busy learning something new, but it is interesting. Last week I gave my team leader my opinion. At first I was nervous, but thanks to the preparation I explained it well. The team leader said it was a good idea. Next month I will give the presentation.

The 3 mistakes this level makes

  • 저는 매운 음식을 먹지 않아요 못 해요. → 저는 매운 음식을 먹지 못해요. Choose one negation: -지 않다 means do not, -지 못하다 means cannot.
  • 할아버지가 밥을 먹었어요. → 할아버지께서 밥을 드셨어요. An elder takes 께서 and the honorific verb 드시다, and the verb carries -시-.
  • 몇 살이에요? 저는 스물 살이에요. → 몇 살이에요? 저는 스무 살이에요. 스물 shortens to 스무 before the counter 살.

Your task

Give and defend one opinion Write a short opinion in 합니다체: state it with -고 생각합니다, support it with two reasons, and add one sentence about what would change your mind. Use at least one -지 않다 and one -(으)ㄹ 수 있다 form, and address one sentence to a superior with the -시- honorific.
