French Numbers 1 to 20

The first twenty numbers, how to pronounce the tricky ones, and how to ask prices.

Full course. Authored French content, part of the free EkGuru French course. Audio is your browser’s computer voice, not a native recording.

1 to 10

un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq, six, sept, huit, neuf, dix. Watch the silent endings: deux, trois, six, dix all end in a silent consonant when alone.

11 to 16

onze, douze, treize, quatorze, quinze, seize. These are single words with no pattern — memorise them. Note the z sound in onze and quinze.

17 to 20

From 17 the pattern appears: dix-sept (17, "ten-seven"), dix-huit (18), dix-neuf (19), then vingt (20). The hyphen joins the parts, and vingt keeps its final -t silent.

Asking and saying prices

C'est combien ? and Ça coûte combien ? both mean "how much is it?". The answer is just the number with euros: C'est dix euros (it's ten euros), Vingt euros (twenty euros).
