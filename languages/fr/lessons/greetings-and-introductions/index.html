French Greetings and Introductions

The greetings that cover most situations

French greetings are smaller in number than English ones. Bonjour (hello / good morning) works from dawn to dusk, in shops, offices and on the phone. After about 6pm people switch to bonsoir (good evening). Salut is the casual hi for friends, and bonne nuit (good night) is only said when someone is going to bed.

Introducing yourself

To give your name, say Je m'appelle (literally "I call myself") or Je suis (I am): Je m'appelle Marie / Je suis Marie. To ask someone's name: Comment tu t'appelles ? (informal) or Comment vous appelez-vous ? (formal).

Tu vs vous — the register choice

French has two words for "you": tu for friends, family, children and close peers, and vous for strangers, older people, and formal situations — vous is also the plural "you all". When unsure, vous is the safer, more respectful choice, and French speakers often wait to be invited to use tu.

Ça va ? — asking how someone is

Ça va ? and Comment ça va ? mean "how's it going?" (informal); the formal version is Comment allez-vous ?. Everyday answers: Ça va bien, merci (fine, thanks), Très bien (very well), or Pas mal (not bad).
