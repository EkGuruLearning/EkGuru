Être and Avoir — The Two Verbs You Can't Live Without

The two most common French verbs, how to conjugate them, and why French says "I have 20 years" instead of "I am 20".

Full course. Authored French content, part of the free EkGuru French course. Audio is your browser’s computer voice, not a native recording.

Why start here

Être (to be) and avoir (to have) are the two most frequent verbs in French. You need them for descriptions, possession, and — surprisingly — for age, hunger and thirst, where French uses avoir where English uses "to be".

Être — to be

je suis, tu es, il/elle est, nous sommes, vous êtes, ils/elles sont. Examples: Je suis de l'Inde (I am from India), Je suis étudiant (I am a student). Nationality, origin and profession all use être.

Avoir — to have

j'ai, tu as, il/elle a, nous avons, vous avez, ils/elles ont. Notice je ai becomes j'ai — the vowel runs together. Examples: J'ai un frère (I have a brother), Nous avons une voiture (we have a car).

Avoir for age and feelings

French literally "has" its age and feelings: J'ai vingt ans (I am 20 years old), J'ai faim (I am hungry), J'ai soif (I am thirsty), J'ai chaud (I am hot), J'ai froid (I am cold). Never say je suis faim — that is a classic learner error.
