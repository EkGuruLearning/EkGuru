A1, unit by unit

First words

Bonjour! Greetings and politeness

BONJOUR works all day until evening, then BONSOIR. SALUT is hello/bye for friends. Magic trio: S'IL VOUS PLAÎT (please), MERCI (thanks), PARDON (sorry). AU REVOIR closes every meeting.

WordSay itMeaning
bonjourboh(n)-ZHOORhello / good morning
bonsoirboh(n)-SWAHRgood evening
salutsah-LEWhi / bye (friends)
au revoiroh ruh-VWAHRgoodbye
s'il vous plaîtseel voo PLEHplease (formal)
mercimair-SEEthanks

Every noun is LE or LA (un/une)

Masculine nouns take LE/UN, feminine take LA/UNE. Learn every noun WITH its article: LE livre, LA maison. No shortcuts — gender is half the word!

un/le + masculine · une/la + feminine
WordSay itMeaning
le livreluh LEEVRthe book
la maisonlah meh-ZOH(n)the house
un caféuhn kah-FEHa coffee
Watch out:
  • Saying LA livre — livre is masculine: LE livre.
  • Skipping articles (saying just Maison) — always LE/LA/UN/UNE.

Numbers and introductions

Numbers 1–10 open prices and ages. Introduce with ÊTRE (to be): JE SUIS = I am. Je suis Léa. Ask names: COMMENT TU T'APPELLES? (What are you called?). Answer: JE M'APPELLE …

unuhnone

cinqsa(n)kfive

septsetseven

dixdeesten
ÊTRE: je suis, tu es, il/elle est

JE SUIS = I am, TU ES = you are, IL/ELLE EST = he/she is. French always keeps the pronoun (JE, TU, IL) — never drop it like Spanish!

je suis · tu es · il/elle est
WordSay itMeaning
Je suis Léa.zhuh swee leh-AH.I am Léa.
Tu es Théo?tew eh teh-OH?Are you Théo?
Elle est française.ell eh frah(n)-SEZ.She is French.
Watch out:
  • Dropping JE (saying SUIS Léa) — French needs JE SUIS.
  • Saying IL for a woman — she is ELLE.

Daily life

Family and AVOIR (age)

Meet the family: MÈRE, PÈRE, FRÈRE, SŒUR. Age uses AVOIR (to have): J'AI DIX ANS = I have 10 years = I am 10. Ask: TU AS QUEL ÂGE? (You have what age?)

WordSay itMeaning
la mèrelah mairmother
le pèreluh pairfather
le frèreluh frairbrother
la sœurlah surrsister
le filsluh feesson
la fillelah feedaughter
la famillelah fah-MEEfamily
l'ami / l'amielah-MEEfriend (m/f)
AVOIR for age: j'ai… ans

J'AI = I have, TU AS = you have, IL/ELLE A = he/she has. Age: J'AI + number + ANS. TU AS QUEL ÂGE? = How old are you?

j'ai/tu as/il a + number + ans
WordSay itMeaning
J'ai dix ans.zheh dees ah(n).I am 10 years old.
Tu as quel âge?tew ah kel AHZH?How old are you?
Ma sœur a sept ans.mah surr ah set ah(n).My sister is 7.
Watch out:
  • Saying JE SUIS dix ans — age always uses AVOIR.
  • Saying AN without S: ANS sounds ahn — keep the S silent!

Food and -ER verbs

Food first! EAU, PAIN, LAIT, POMME. Order with JE VOUDRAIS (I would like): JE VOUDRAIS UN CAFÉ. Most verbs end in -ER: PARLER → JE PARLE, TU PARLES, IL PARLE (all sound the same: parl!).

WordSay itMeaning
l'eaulohwater
le painluh pa(n)bread
le laitluh lehmilk
la pommelah pohmapple
le rizluh reerice
le pouletluh poo-LEHchicken
le caféluh kah-FEHcoffee
le théluh tehtea
Present -ER: je parle, tu parles, il parle

Drop -ER, add -E, -ES, -E. All three sound the same (parl)! Spelling differs, sound doesn't. PARLER, MANGER, AIMER — hundreds of verbs, one music.

stem + e/es/e (all sound the same)
WordSay itMeaning

Tu parles anglais?tew parl ah(n)-GLEH?Do you speak English?
Elle chante bien.ell shah(n)t bya(n).She sings well.
Watch out:
  • Pronouncing the -S in TU PARLES — silent! All sound parl.
  • Saying PARLE without JE — French keeps its pronouns.

Telling time: il est… heures

IL EST UNE HEURE (1:00), IL EST DEUX HEURES (2:00). Half: ET DEMIE. Quarter: ET QUART. Always HEURE(S) — never drop it!

il est + number + heure(s) (+ et demie)
WordSay itMeaning
Il est une heure.eel eh ewn urr.It is one o'clock.
Il est trois heures.eel eh trwah zurr.It is three o'clock.
Il est trois heures et demie.eel eh trwah zurr eh duh-MEE.It is half past three.
Watch out:
  • Saying IL EST TROIS (no HEURES) — always add HEURES.
  • Capitalising days (Lundi) — French days are lowercase.
