First words

Bonjour! Greetings and politeness

BONJOUR works all day until evening, then BONSOIR. SALUT is hello/bye for friends. Magic trio: S'IL VOUS PLAÎT (please), MERCI (thanks), PARDON (sorry). AU REVOIR closes every meeting.

Every noun is LE or LA (un/une)

Masculine nouns take LE/UN, feminine take LA/UNE. Learn every noun WITH its article: LE livre, LA maison. No shortcuts — gender is half the word!

ÊTRE: je suis, tu es, il/elle est

JE SUIS = I am, TU ES = you are, IL/ELLE EST = he/she is. French always keeps the pronoun (JE, TU, IL) — never drop it like Spanish!

je suis · tu es · il/elle est
WordSay itMeaning
Je suis Léa.zhuh swee leh-AH.I am Léa.
Tu es Théo?tew eh teh-OH?Are you Théo?
Elle est française.ell eh frah(n)-SEZ.She is French.
Watch out:
  • Dropping JE (saying SUIS Léa) — French needs JE SUIS.
  • Saying IL for a woman — she is ELLE.

Tu ou vous : choisir sur la première phrase

French makes one decision before it says anything else: tu for friends and family, vous for strangers, shops and anyone older. « Bonjour » opens both doors; the pronoun picks the room.

WordSay itMeaning
vousvooyou (polite)
tutüyou (familiar)
madamemah-DAHMmadam
monsieurmuh-SYUHsir
onoh(n)one, we (spoken)
The two yous

Bonjour madame, comment allez-vous ? Bonjour Théo, comment vas-tu ? Vous works for one person when politeness is needed; on means we in speech and takes the verb of il.

vous + verb (2nd person plural) · tu + verb (2nd person singular) · on + 3rd person singular
WordSay itMeaning
Bonjour madame, comment allez-vous ?boh(n)-ZHOOR mah-DAHM, koh-mah(n) tah-lay-VOO ?Hello madam, how are you?
Salut Théo, comment vas-tu ?sah-LÜ tay-OH, koh-mah(n) vah-TÜ ?Hi Théo, how are you?
On va au café ?oh(n) vah oh kah-FAY ?Shall we go to the café?
Watch out:
  • Bonjour madame, comment vas-tu ? → Bonjour madame, comment allez-vous ? The pronoun and the verb form must agree in politeness.
  • Vous est mon ami. → Tu es mon ami. vous is never used for a friend you call by first name.

Daily life

Family and AVOIR (age)

Meet the family: MÈRE, PÈRE, FRÈRE, SŒUR. Age uses AVOIR (to have): J'AI DIX ANS = I have 10 years = I am 10. Ask: TU AS QUEL ÂGE? (You have what age?)

AVOIR for age: j'ai… ans

J'AI = I have, TU AS = you have, IL/ELLE A = he/she has. Age: J'AI + number + ANS. TU AS QUEL ÂGE? = How old are you?

  • Saying JE SUIS dix ans — age always uses AVOIR.
  • Saying AN without S: ANS sounds ahn — keep the S silent!

Le temps et les saisons

Weather is impersonal and short: IL FAIT BEAU, IL PLEUT, IL FAIT FROID. Seasons take EN for the ones you live through and À for the ones you name in a date.

Weather frame

Il fait beau en été, il fait froid en hiver. En automne, il pleut souvent. Notice which seasons take en (été, hiver, automne) and which takes au (printemps).

French A1 beyond the drills — culture, reading, listening, idioms

These sections are written for French A1 specifically. The reading text is EkGuru's own; the culture note cites where its facts come from; the idioms and the mistakes list are the ones this level actually trips over.

Culture note

Bonjour is not a word, it is a door: French opens every exchange with it and closes with au revoir. The tu/vous choice is made on the first sentence — vous for a stranger, a shopkeeper or anyone older, tu for family, friends and classmates who have offered it.

Source (named, not linked): en.wikipedia.org, the article “French language”.

Reading — read this aloud, slowly

Léa arrive au café le matin. « Bonjour, un café, s'il vous plaît. » Le serveur répond : « Bonjour ! Et avec ça ? » Elle prend un croissant et demande l'heure. « Il est huit heures et quart. » Elle dit merci, au revoir, et va au travail à pied, parce que le bureau est tout près.

What it says: Léa arrives at the café in the morning. 'Hello, a coffee, please.' The waiter answers: 'Hello! And with that?' She takes a croissant and asks the time. 'It is a quarter past eight.' She says thank you, goodbye, and walks to work, because the office is very close.

The 3 mistakes this level makes

  • Bonjour, comment allez-tu ? → Bonjour, comment allez-vous ? / Bonjour, comment vas-tu ? The vous form and the tu form cannot mix inside one question.
  • Merci vous. → Merci. Merci takes no object; to insist, use merci beaucoup.
  • Je m'appelle je suis Léa. → Je m'appelle Léa. / Je suis Léa. Two introductions; use one.

Your task

Meet someone twice Write the same introduction twice: once at a party to someone your age (tu, first name) and once at a doctor's reception desk (vous, surname). Keep both to four lines, read them aloud, and notice that the politeness lives in the pronoun, not in the adjectives.
