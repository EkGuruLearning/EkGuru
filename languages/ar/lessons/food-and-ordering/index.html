Arabic Food and Ordering

Order confidently in any Arab restaurant: the phrases, hospitality rules, and bread culture.

Full course. Authored Arabic content, part of the free EkGuru Arabic course. Audio is your browser’s computer voice, not a native recording.

Ordering politely

The ordering verbs are أريد… (urīdu… — I want…) or softer ممكن… (mumkin… — is it possible…): أريد شايا، من فضلك (I want tea, please). Staff ask تفضل، ماذا تريد؟ (go ahead — what do you want?). To get attention: لو سمحت! (law samaḥt — if you permit!). Menus: القائمة (al-qā'ima). Pointing plus هذا، من فضلك (this one, please) works everywhere.

Hospitality is sacred

Guests are a trust from God — hosts press food relentlessly, and refusing outright offends. Accept at least tea: شاي (shāy) comes sweet and endless in the Gulf; mint tea (أتاي) rules North Africa. Coffee (قهوة) is served in tiny cups — wiggle your cup to decline more. Never refuse with the left hand; eat, give and receive with the right.

Bread, mains and paying

Bread (خبز — khubz) is the utensil — tear, scoop, never cut with a knife. Core words: ماء (water), أرز (rice), لحم (meat), دجاج (chicken), سلطة (salad). The bill: الحساب لو سمحت (al-ḥisāb law samaḥt). Tipping varies (10–15% in Levant/Egypt restaurants; service charges common in Gulf hotels). Delicious: لذيذ! (ladhīdh!).

Before and after eating

Before eating, say بسم الله (bismillāh — in God's name); after, الحمد لله (al-ḥamdu lillāh — praise God). Hosts urge كل! كل! (kul! — eat! eat!). Compliment the cook with تسلم إيديك (dialect — bless your hands). Hunger: أنا جائع (man) / أنا جائعة (woman); thirst: أنا عطشان/عطشانة.
