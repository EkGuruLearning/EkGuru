Greetings: السلام عليكم

The famous greeting! السلام عليكم (peace upon you) → answer وعليكم السلام (and upon you peace). Morning: صباح الخير → answer صباح النور! Greetings always come in PAIRS.

Names and how are you

اسمي ليلى = My name is Layla. Ask: ما اسمك؟ (m) / ما اسمكِ؟ (f) — -KA for men, -KI for women! How are you: كيف حالك؟ → بخير، الحمد لله (fine, praise God)!

Thank you, please, sorry: the polite three

Three words carry almost every small exchange: شكراً (thank you), عفواً (you're welcome — and also 'excuse me'), and من فضلك (please). They change with the person you speak to: من فضلكِ and تفضّلي to a woman, آسفة if you are the woman apologising.

WordSay itMeaning
شكراًshukranthank you
عفواًʿafwanyou're welcome; excuse me
من فضلكmin faḍlikplease (to a man)
آسفāsifsorry (said by a man)
تفضّلtafaḍḍalgo ahead; here you are
Politeness follows gender

Arabic marks the person you are addressing, not your own politeness. To a man: من فضلك، تفضّل. To a woman: من فضلكِ، تفضّلي. If you are a woman apologising, you say آسفة.

min faḍlik → min faḍlik(i) · āsif → āsifa · tafaḍḍal → tafaḍḍalī
WordSay itMeaning
شكراً جزيلاً!shukran jazīlan!Thank you very much!
من فضلكِ، أين المحطة؟min faḍlik, ayna al-maḥaṭṭa?Please, where is the station?
آسفة، أنا متأخرة.āsifa, anā mutaʾakhkhira.Sorry, I am late. (woman speaking)
Watch out:
  • شكراً يا سيدتي! → شكراً يا سيّدتي! The word شكراً ends in an alif; it takes no extra ending for the person addressed.
  • من فضلكي، أين المحطة؟ → من فضلكِ، أين المحطة؟ The feminine ending is a kasra, not a ي: فضلكِ.

Gender and plurals

Gender: هذا / هذه

Two genders! هذا = this (m), هذه = this (f). Feminine usually ends in ة (ta marbuta): مدرسة (school-f), سيارة (car-f). No ة? Usually masculine: كتاب (book-m), بيت (house-m)!

WordSay itMeaning
هذاhādhāthis (m)
هذهhādhihīthis (f)
رجلrajulman
امرأةimraʾawoman
ولدwaladboy
بنتbintgirl

هذا (m) / هذه (f) + ة

هذا كتاب (this is a book-m), هذه مدرسة (this is a school-f). Adjectives match: رجل كبير (big man), امرأة كبيرة (big woman) — feminine adds ة!

Numbers and the noun

Asking: ما، من، أين، هل

Four question makers cover most of A1: ما (what), من (who), أين (where) and هل for any yes/no question. They stand at the front, and the rest of the sentence does not move.

Questions keep the sentence order

Arabic does not reorder for questions. ما اسمك؟ is literally 'what your-name?'. هل turns a statement into a yes/no question with no other change: أنت طالب → هل أنت طالب؟

Watch out:
  • هل ما اسمك؟ → ما اسمك؟ ما is already a question; هل is only for yes/no questions.
  • أنت من أين؟ → من أين أنت؟ The question word stays at the front: من أين أنت؟

Arabic A1 beyond the drills — culture, reading, listening, idioms

Culture note

Greetings in the Arab world come in pairs and carry religion, warmth and time: السلام عليكم is answered وعليكم السلام, and a visitor is offered coffee or tea before business. Refusing the cup is read as refusing the welcome, so a small sip is the polite minimum. The same page teaches fuṣḥā, the shared written Arabic; on the street you will also hear a local dialect — both are Arabic, not one right and one wrong.

Source (named, not linked): en.wikipedia.org, the article “Arabic”.

اسمي منى. أنا من عمّان، وأسكن في القاهرة. صباح الخير! كيف حالك؟ أنا بخير، الحمد لله. هذا صديقي، اسمه كريم. هو طالب في الجامعة. عندي درس الآن، مع السلامة!

What it says: My name is Mona. I am from Amman and I live in Cairo. Good morning! How are you? I am fine, praise God. This is my friend, his name is Karim. He is a student at the university. I have a class now — goodbye!

The 3 mistakes this level makes

  • أنا اسمي منى أنا. → اسمي منى. Arabic marks the subject in the verb or pronoun; doubling أنا sounds like a stutter.
  • مرحبا عليكم! → السلام عليكم! مرحبا is fine alone, but the paired greeting uses السلام عليكم.
  • هذا طالبة. → هذه طالبة. طالبة is feminine, so the demonstrative must be هذه.
