A1, unit by unit

Foundations and People

Greetings and Respect

Learn greetings used across Tamil-speaking communities. Tamil is read left to right. Use நீங்கள் (nīṅkaḷ) with elders and new people; spoken forms may differ from formal written forms.

WordSay itMeaning
வணக்கம்vaṇakkamHello (respectful, universal)
நீங்கள் எப்படி இருக்கிறீர்கள்?nīṅkaḷ eppaḍi irukkiṟīrkaḷ?How are you? (formal)
நீ எப்படி இருக்கிறாய்?nī eppaḍi irukkiṟāy?How are you? (familiar)
நான் நன்றாக இருக்கிறேன்nāṉ naṉṟāka irukkiṟēṉI'm fine
நன்றிnaṉṟiThank you
ஆம் / இல்லைām / illaiYes / No
தயவுசெய்துtayavuceytuPlease
மன்னிக்கவும்maṉṉikkavumExcuse me / Sorry
Respectful identity sentences

Tamil commonly omits an overt “is” in simple identity sentences. Pronouns and respectful forms carry social meaning.

People and Pronouns

Identify people and choose singular, plural, and respectful pronouns. Learn complete forms with romanisation before analysing suffixes.

WordSay itMeaning
நான்nāṉI
நாங்கள்nāṅkaḷwe (excluding you)
நீங்கள்nīṅkaḷyou (formal / plural)
நீnīyou (intimate)
அவன்avaṉhe
அவள்avaḷshe
அதுatuit
அவர்கள்avarkaḷthey (people)
Pronouns and zero copula

A present identity statement often places the description after the person without a separate word for “is.”

person + description
WordSay itMeaning
நான்நான்I
நீநீyou (intimate)
நீங்கள்நீங்கள்you (formal)
Watch out:
  • Do not force English “am/is/are” into every Tamil identity sentence.

Everyday Needs

Home, School, and Objects

Name familiar places and objects, then locate them. Tamil attaches case endings to nouns rather than placing separate prepositions before them.

WordSay itMeaning
புத்தகம்puttakambook
வீடுvīḍuhouse / home
வேலைvēlaiwork
பள்ளிpaḷḷischool
ஆசிரியர்āciriyarteacher
மாணவர்māṇavarstudent
கார்kārcar
சாலைsālairoad
Location with -இல் / -ல

The formal locative suffix -இல் (-il), often -ல (-la) in speech, means “in/at.”

place + locative suffix + இருக்கிறது
WordSay itMeaning
வீட்டில்vīṭṭilat home
எனக்கு கொடுங்கள்enakku koḍuṅkaḷgive (to) me
சென்னையிலிருந்துceṉṉaiyiliruntufrom Chennai
Watch out:
  • Do not put a Tamil location marker before the noun as English does.
