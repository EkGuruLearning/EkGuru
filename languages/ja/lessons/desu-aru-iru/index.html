です, ある and いる

Identity with です, existence split by animacy with ある/いる, possession, and polite negation.

Full course. Authored Japanese content, part of the free EkGuru Japanese course. Audio is your browser’s computer voice, not a native recording.

です — identity and adjectives

です (desu) links identity: 私は先生です (I am a teacher). Adjectives split into two tribes: い-adjectives conjugate themselves (高いです — is expensive; 高くないです — isn't), while な-adjectives need です (静かです — is quiet; 静かじゃありません — isn't). Past: でした (学生でした — was a student). Polite negative: じゃありません / ではありません.

ある vs いる — alive or not?

Existence splits by animacy: いる (iru) for living things, ある (aru) for the rest. 猫がいます (there is a cat), 本があります (there is a book). Location first: 机の上に本があります (on the desk there is a book). Negatives: いません / ありません. Robots and company departments take いる — if it moves with purpose, it's alive.

"I have" through existence

Possession is expressed as existence-at-me: 私は車があります (as for me, a car exists — I have a car). Siblings: 兄弟がいます (I have siblings). Time and events use ある: 時間がありますか (do you have time?), 会議があります (there is a meeting). No verb "to have" exists — existence covers it.

Politeness is grammar

です/ます is the polite baseline — plain forms (だ・である, dictionary verbs) wait for friends and writing. Above polite sits keigo (honorific language): humble verbs for your own acts (参ります for go), exalted verbs for others' (いらっしゃいます). Beginners: master です/ます everywhere — over-polite beats under-polite with every stranger.
