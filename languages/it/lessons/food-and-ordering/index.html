Italian Food and Ordering

Order confidently in any Italian bar or restaurant: the phrases, the coffee rules, and how the courses work.

Ordering: vorrei and per me

The polite ordering verb is vorrei (I would like): Vorrei un caffè, per favore. Equally common is pointing at the menu and saying per me… (for me…) — Per me una pizza margherita. Staff ask Prego? or Mi dica (tell me) — answer with your order directly. To get attention: Scusi! (informal) or Mi scusi! (formal).

Coffee rules — obey them

Italian coffee culture has laws. A caffè is an espresso — small, fast, often drunk standing at the bar (al banco, cheaper than sitting). A cappuccino is breakfast only — ordering one after lunch marks you instantly as a foreigner. A caffè latte is also morning-only; latte macchiato is mostly milk. Never ask for a "latte" alone — you will receive a glass of milk.

Courses and paying

A full Italian meal runs antipasto (starter), primo (pasta/rice), secondo (meat/fish) with contorno (side), then dolce (dessert) and caffè. Nobody expects tourists to eat all five — ordering any subset is normal. The bill is il conto: Il conto, per favore. Watch for coperto (cover charge, €1–3 per person) on the bill — legal in most regions, banned in Lazio and Rome.

Hunger, taste and water

Hunger and thirst use avere: Ho fame (I am hungry), Ho sete (I am thirsty). Core words: il pane (bread), il formaggio (cheese), l'acqua (water — naturale still, frizzante sparkling), il vino (wine), la birra (beer). Before eating, say Buon appetito! Delicious is buonissimo or the heartfelt che buono! (how good!).
