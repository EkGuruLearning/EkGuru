A2, unit by unit

Getting around

Around the city (a Roma, in Italia)

Cities take A, countries take IN: VADO A ROMA (I go to Rome), VADO IN ITALIA (I go to Italy). Location uses STARE: STO A CASA (I am at home). Sinistra (left), destra (right), sempre dritto (straight)!

la bancalah BAHN-kahbank

il negozioeel neh-GOH-tsyohshop

la destralah DEH-strahright
A + city · IN + country

VADO A ROMA (city), VADO IN ITALIA (country). STO A CASA (at home). Big places take IN, dots on the map take A!

a + city · in + country · sto/stai/sta + place
WordSay itMeaning

Vado in Italia.VAH-doh een ee-TAH-lyah.I go to Italy.
Sto a casa.stoh ah KAH-zah.I am at home.
Watch out:
  • Saying VADO IN ROMA — cities take A: A ROMA.
  • Saying VADO A ITALIA — countries take IN: IN ITALIA.

Travel and passato prossimo

Past trips: HO VISITATO (I visited). Passato prossimo = AVERE (ho, hai, ha) + past verb (visitATO, mangiATO, vedUTO). -ARE verbs just add ATO!

WordSay itMeaning
il viaggioeel VYAHD-johtrip
l'aereolah-EH-reh-ohplane
l'hotelLOH-telhotel
la valigialah vah-LEE-jahsuitcase
il bigliettoeel bee-LYEHT-tohticket
la spiaggialah SPYAHD-jahbeach
la montagnalah mohn-TAHN-yahmountain
la fotolah FOH-tohphoto
Passato prossimo: ho visitato

HO + visitATO = I visited. HAI + mangiATO = you ate. HA + vedUTO = he/she saw. Most past verbs end ATO (from -ARE), UTO (-ERE), ITO (-IRE).

ho/hai/ha + past verb (ato/uto/ito)
WordSay itMeaning
Ho viaggiato in Spagna.oh vyahd-JAH-toh een SPAHN-yah.I travelled in Spain.
Hai comprato i biglietti?EH-ee kohm-PRAH-toh ee bee-LYEHT-tee?Did you buy tickets?
È arrivata ieri.eh ahr-ree-VAH-tah YEH-ree.She arrived yesterday. (essere verb!)
Watch out:
  • Saying HO VIAGGIARE — past needs VIAGGIATO, not the infinitive.
  • Forgetting movement verbs use ESSERE: È ARRIVATA (she arrived).

Life and plans

Daily routines (reflexive verbs)

Routines bounce back: MI ALZO = I get (myself) up. MI = myself, TI = yourself, SI = himself/herself. Mornings run on MI/TI/SI: alzarsi, lavarsi, vestirsi!

WordSay itMeaning
la mattinalah maht-TEE-nahmorning
il pomeriggioeel poh-meh-REED-johafternoon
la seralah SEH-rahevening
il lavoroeel lah-VOH-rohwork / job
la scuolalah SKWOH-lahschool
la doccialah DOHT-chahshower
la colazionelah koh-lah-TSYOH-nehbreakfast
la cenalah CHEH-nahdinner
Reflexive MI/TI/SI: mi alzo

ALZARSI = to get up. MI ALZO, TI ALZI, SI ALZA. The little word points back at the person — never drop it!

mi/ti/si + verb
WordSay itMeaning
Mi alzo alle sette.mee AHLT-soh AHL-leh SEHT-teh.I get up at seven.
Ti lavi la mattina?tee LAH-vee lah maht-TEE-nah?Do you wash in the morning?
Si veste tardi.see VEH-steh TAHR-dee.He/She gets dressed late.
Watch out:
  • Saying ALZO without MI — it means 'I lift', not 'I get up'!
  • Mixing TI and SI: TI = you, SI = he/she.

Health and future plans

At the doctor: MI FA MALE LA TESTA = my head hurts. For plans, Italians use the PRESENT + time word: PARTO DOMANI = I leave tomorrow (I am leaving tomorrow). Simple and natural!

Near future: present + time word

PARTO DOMANI = I leave tomorrow. STUDI STASERA? = Are you studying tonight? Present tense + domani/stasera/lunedì = instant future. (Real futuro comes at B1!)

present verb + domani/stasera/…
WordSay itMeaning
Parto domani.PAHR-toh doh-MAH-nee.I leave tomorrow.
Studi stasera?STOO-dee stah-SEH-rah?Are you studying tonight?
Piove domani.PYOH-veh doh-MAH-nee.It rains tomorrow.
Watch out:
  • Skipping the time word: PARTO alone = I leave (now?).
  • Using future endings too early — present + domani first!
