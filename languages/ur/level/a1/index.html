Script and Social Basics

Greetings and Courtesy

Begin with respectful Urdu greetings and survival courtesy. Read from right to left, use the romanisation as a bridge, and listen before repeating. آپ (ap) is the safe respectful form for a new person or an elder.

Respectful introductions

Use آپ (ap) for respectful “you.” A name is linked with ہے (hai), while a self-description with میں (main) commonly ends in ہوں (hun).

person + name/description + ہے/ہوں
WordSay itMeaning
میرا نام علی ہے۔mera nam Ali hai.My name is Ali.
میں ٹھیک ہوں۔main thik hun.I am fine.
آپ کیسے ہیں؟ap kaise hain?How are you?
Watch out:
  • Do not use informal تم (tum) for an elder or a stranger.

Urdu A1 beyond the drills — culture, reading, listening, idioms

These sections are written for Urdu A1 specifically. The reading text is EkGuru's own; the culture note cites where its facts come from; the idioms and the mistakes list are the ones this level actually trips over.

Culture note

Urdu is written from right to left in a connected Perso-Arabic script. Letter shapes can change with position, while short vowels are often not written in ordinary text. Learners benefit from reading complete, familiar words rather than treating each joined shape as a separate letter. Spoken forms and conventions vary across regions and communities.

Source (named, not linked): unicode.org.

The 3 mistakes this level makes

  • آپ کہاں ہے؟ → آپ کہاں ہیں؟ Respectful آپ normally takes the honorific/plural verb ہیں.
  • میں ٹھیک ہے۔ → میں ٹھیک ہوں۔ The first-person copula is ہوں, not ہے.
  • مجھے اردو سیکھتا ہوں۔ → میں اردو سیکھتا/سیکھتی ہوں۔ The learner is the subject; match the verb to the speaker.
