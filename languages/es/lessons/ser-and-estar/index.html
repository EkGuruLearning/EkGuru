Ser and Estar — The Two Ways to Say "To Be"

Why Spanish has two verbs for "to be", the rule that tells them apart, and the exceptions every learner hits.

Beta course. Authored Spanish content, part of the free EkGuru Spanish course in beta. Audio is your browser’s computer voice, not a native recording.

The core rule

Spanish has two verbs for "to be": ser and estar. The rule of thumb: ser is for what something is (permanent identity, origin, profession, characteristics), and estar is for how something is right now (location, mood, temporary states).

Ser — identity and essence

Soy de India (I am from India), Soy estudiante (I am a student), Ella es alta (she is tall). These are stable facts. Origin, nationality, profession and personality all use ser.

Estar — state and location

Estoy cansado (I am tired), Estoy en casa (I am at home), El café está caliente (the coffee is hot). Mood, position and location always use estar — even for places, which are locations, not identities.

The one that trips everyone

Some adjectives change meaning with the verb. Estoy listo means "I am ready"; Soy listo means "I am clever". Está aburrido means "he is bored"; Es aburrido means "he is boring". Learn these as pairs, not rules.
