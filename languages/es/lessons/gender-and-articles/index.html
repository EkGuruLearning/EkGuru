Gender and Articles in Spanish

How to guess a noun's gender from its ending, and the four words for "the" — el, la, los, las.

Full course. Authored Spanish content, part of the free EkGuru Spanish course. Audio is your browser’s computer voice, not a native recording.

Every noun is masculine or feminine

Every Spanish noun has a gender, and it shows in the article. El is "the" for masculine singular, la for feminine singular, los and las for their plurals.

The -o and -a rule (with exceptions)

Most nouns ending in -o are masculine (el libro, the book) and most ending in -a are feminine (la casa, the house). The famous exceptions are worth memorising: el día (the day), el mapa (the map), la mano (the hand), la foto (the photo).

Other endings

Nouns ending in -ción, -sión and -dad are almost always feminine: la canción, la televisión, la ciudad. Nouns ending in -ma of Greek origin are masculine: el problema, el tema, el sistema.

Agreement with adjectives

Adjectives agree with the noun: el libro rojo (the red book) but la casa roja (the red house). Adjectives ending in -e or a consonant are the same for both genders: el libro grande, la casa grande.

elthe — masculine singular
lathe — feminine singular
