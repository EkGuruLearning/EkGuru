School and studies

Talk about classes, exams and homework using going-to future for near plans.

WordSay itMeaning
examexam
subjectschool subject

going to for plans

Use be + going to + verb for a plan already decided.

I/you/we are going to + verb
WordSay itMeaning
I'm going to revise tonight.plan for tonight
She's going to miss the lecture.future result
Are you going to finish the homework?yes/no plan
Watch out:
  • Don't say 'I going to' — you need am/is/are.
  • Don't use going to for a spontaneous offer (use will).

LeoYes, history. I'm going to revise after class.plan

LeoYes — the deadline for notes is tonight.accept

After enjoy, like, mind, keep, use the -ing form.

WordSay itMeaning

Have to expresses an external rule, not a personal wish.

have/has to + verb
WordSay itMeaning
You have to show your passport at the gate.rule
We have to change at Leeds.travel need
He doesn't have to book today.no necessity
Watch out:

  • Don't say 'he have to'.

would like vs want

Would like is the polite order form in restaurants.

I'd like + noun / I'd like to + verb
WordSay itMeaning
I'd like the soup, please.order
Would you like it spicy?offer
I'd like to see the menu.request
Watch out:
  • Want is fine with friends, blunt with waiters.
  • Don't say 'I would like see'.

Shall we / Why don't we

These two frames make a suggestion, not an order.

Shall we + verb? / Why don't we + verb?
WordSay itMeaning
Shall we meet at seven?suggestion
Why don't we catch up next week?softer suggest
Let's bring some food.inclusive let's
Watch out:
  • Shall we is a question, not 'shall I you'.
  • Don't refuse with only 'no' — give a reason.

Dialogue. Read it aloud twice — once for each speaker.

WhoLineSay itEnglish
RiyaThere's a party on Friday. Shall we go?invite
OmarI'm busy Friday. Why don't we catch up next week?refuse+alt
RiyaSunday? I'm free after six.new time
OmarPerfect. I'll bring dessert.accept

first conditional

If + present, will + verb: a real future possibility.

If + present, will + verb
WordSay itMeaning
If it rains, we'll cancel the picnic.real if
If the forecast is clear, we'll walk.plan

  • Don't say 'If it will rain'.

present simple vs going to

Present simple is the timetable; going to is your decision.

I usually + verb / I'm going to + verb
WordSay itMeaning
I usually commute at seven.habit
I'm going to save next month.decision
My shift starts at nine.timetable
Watch out:
  • Don't use present continuous for a permanent habit.
  • Don't use going to for a printed timetable.
