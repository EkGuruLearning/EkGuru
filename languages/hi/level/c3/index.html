Media and the Public Voice

Headline Grammar: Nouns Carry the Verb

Hindi headlines drop the verb and pack the action into verbal nouns: बढ़ोतरी, गिरावट, तैनाती, रवाना. The reader restores the tense from context. At this level you read a headline, rebuild the full sentence in your head, and notice what the headline chose NOT to say.

Verb-less headlines and their full forms

A headline keeps the subject and a verbal noun and deletes है/हुआ/किया: पेट्रोल के दामों में बढ़ोतरी = पेट्रोल के दाम बढ़ गए हैं। Agents hide behind nouns: 'तैनाती' does not say who deployed. Postpositions survive (में, पर, से) because they carry the relationship. To read critically, rebuild the sentence and ask: who did it, when, and says who?

NOUN + में/पर + VERBAL NOUN → full: NOUN + verb (past/present) + agent restored

Three Lexical Layers: Sanskrit, Persian, English

Standard Hindi draws on three word-stocks at once. The same idea can be प्रतीक्षा (Sanskritic), इंतज़ार (Perso-Arabic) or वेट (English). Each choice signals a register and an audience. Experts switch deliberately: Sanskritic for officialdom and ceremony, Perso-Arabic for warmth and the courtroom, English for tech and the office.

Choosing a layer on purpose

Rule of thumb: match the layer to the setting, then stay inside it for a sentence. An invitation card says विवाह; a friend says शादी; a wedding planner's WhatsApp says 'wedding'. Mixing layers is normal in speech but in writing a sudden tatsam word inside a chatty sentence sounds like a joke — which is exactly how satirists use it. Verbs stay Hindi; only the noun/adjective stock moves.

setting → layer: ceremony/office notice = तत्सम · conversation/courts/poetry = फ़ारसी-अरबी · tech/corporate = English loan

Euphemism and Political Metaphor

Public Hindi softens, inflates and hides. Prices are not raised, they are 'rationalised' (युक्तिसंगत); a politician is not sacked, he 'steps aside' (पद छोड़ा); a defeat is a 'setback' (झटका). Expert readers translate the euphemism back into the plain event and notice who benefits from the softening.

Decoding softened language

Three moves: (1) nominalise the harsh verb (कीमतें बढ़ीं → मूल्य-समायोजन), (2) swap the agent for a force of nature (हालात ऐसे बने), (3) borrow a body or object metaphor (कुर्सी, हाशिया, पाला). To decode, ask: what literally happened, who did it, and what plainer verb fits? Then you can quote the euphemism in inverted commas and gloss it.

Humour, Satire and Wordplay

Vyangya: Saying the Opposite with a Straight Face

व्यंग्य (satire) in Hindi rarely shouts. It praises what it attacks — ‘वाह, क्या व्यवस्था है!’ said about a broken system — and relies on particles (तो, ही, भी, ज़रूर), exaggerated respect, and a deadpan tone. You learn to hear the gap between the words and the voice, and to produce it without tipping into insult.

Irony markers: particles and inflated praise

Three signals mark Hindi irony: (1) तो/ही/भी placed for contrast — ‘आप तो समय पर आ ही गए’ to someone two hours late; (2) over-formal praise in a trivial setting — ‘महान कार्य’ for fixing a bulb after a month; (3) agreement words used as disagreement — बेशक, ज़रूर, क्यों नहीं. The verb stays polite; the attack is in the mismatch. In writing, no exclamation mark is needed; the flatter, the sharper.

Proverbs and Puns in Argument

A well-placed कहावत ends an argument in Hindi; a pun (श्लेष) wins the room. At expert level you must know the proverb, its literal image, when it is apt, and how speakers twist it. Half a proverb is enough among natives: ‘अब पछताए होत क्या…’ — everyone completes it.

Deploying a proverb: full, half, twisted

Full form for writing: ‘अब पछताए होत क्या, जब चिड़िया चुग गई खेत’. Half form in speech: ‘अब क्या — चिड़िया चुग गई खेत’. Twisted form for humour: swap one noun to fit the situation — ‘जिसकी सत्ता उसकी भैंस’. Proverbs keep archaic verb forms (होत, छलकत = Awadhi/Braj present); do not modernise them. How English verbs enter Hindi

English verbs come in as noun + करना/होना/मारना: मैनेज करना, अपडेट होना, चिल मारना. Hindi grammar wins: tense, gender and ने belong to the Hindi light verb (उसने फ़ाइल सेव की — feminine agreeing with फ़ाइल). Bambaiya adds its own pronouns (अपुन, तेरेको, मेरेको) and a flat -ता/-ती for all persons (अपुन जाता है). Use it in dialogue, memes and jokes; never in a letter to a client.

Regional and Social Texture

Dialect Colour: Awadhi, Braj and Bhojpuri Traces

Standard Hindi sits on a family of regional speeches. Film songs borrow Braj (मोहे, तोहे), village scenes use Awadhi (हमका, का हो), and Bhojpuri gives Hindi comedy its rhythm (का हो, बा, रउआ). You do not need to speak them, but an expert recognises them, knows the region, and never mocks the speaker.

Recognising a dialect in three cues

Cue 1 — pronoun: हमका/हमें (Awadhi), मोहे/तोहे (Braj), रउआ/तोहरा (Bhojpuri). Cue 2 — the copula: बा/बाड़े (Bhojpuri) vs है. Cue 3 — the question word: का for क्या across the eastern belt. In standard prose these appear only in quoted speech, songs, or deliberate colour. When you quote them, keep the spelling the speaker would use and do not ‘correct’ it.

Respectful and Inclusive Wording

Hindi carries social hierarchy in its words. The expert chooses terms that name people as they wish to be named: दिव्यांग rather than विकलांग, दलित rather than older slurs, घरेलू सहायिका rather than नौकरानी, and gender-neutral frames where the gender is unknown. This is not politeness theatre; it is accuracy about people.

Gender-neutral and person-first phrasing

When the person's gender is unknown, avoid forcing masculine: use plural (आवेदक अपना नाम लिखें), the noun व्यक्ति (कोई व्यक्ति), or recast as a noun phrase (आवेदन करने वाले). For groups, name the role not the body: सफ़ाई कर्मचारी, घरेलू सहायिका. For disability, lead with the person: दिव्यांग व्यक्ति / दृष्टिबाधित छात्र. Honorific plural (वे, हैं) applies to any adult whose status you respect, regardless of job.

Reading Tone: Sarcasm, Understatement, Silence

The last expert skill is hearing what is not said. ‘ठीक है’ can mean fine, furious or finished. ‘देखते हैं’ is often a polite no. A long ‘अच्छा…’ is doubt. Understatement (‘थोड़ी-सी दिक़्क़त है’ about a disaster) and strategic silence carry meaning. You learn the cues and how to respond without forcing the speaker to be blunt.

Cues of indirect refusal and understatement

Indirect no: देखते हैं / कोशिश करूँगा / अभी थोड़ा मुश्किल है. Withdrawn consent: जैसी आपकी मर्ज़ी (I disagree but will not fight). Understatement: थोड़ा-सा, ज़रा-सा attached to serious nouns; the smaller the word, the bigger the problem. Closing: a flat ठीक है with no follow-up question. Respond to the meaning, not the words: offer an exit (‘अगर मुश्किल हो तो बाद में बात करते हैं’) rather than pressing.

Hindi C3 beyond the drills — culture, reading, listening, idioms

These sections are written for Hindi C3 specifically. The reading text is EkGuru's own; the culture note cites where its facts come from; the idioms and the mistakes list are the ones this level actually trips over.

Culture note

Hindi public life runs on three registers at once: the Sanskritic Hindi of notices and ceremonies, the Hindustani of conversation, courts and poetry, and the English-mixed Hindi of offices and screens. Newspapers compress events into verb-less headlines; satirists in the tradition of Harishankar Parsai attack systems with deadpan praise; film songs reach for Braj and Awadhi because those dialects carry centuries of devotional song. An expert reader moves between these layers without judging any of them, hears the irony in a flat ‘ठीक है’, and names people — workers, elders, people with disabilities — in the words they would choose for themselves.

Source (named, not linked): en.wikipedia.org, the article “Hindi”.

नगर निगम ने इस बार बजट की सुर्खी बड़ी सँभालकर लिखी — ‘जल-आपूर्ति शुल्क का युक्तिसंगत पुनर्निर्धारण’। मोहल्ले के पुराने पत्रकार दीनानाथ जी ने अख़बार मेज़ पर रखा और हँसे, ‘बोले तो पानी महँगा।’ उनकी पोती ने पूछा कि अगर दाम बढ़ाने ही थे तो सीधा क्यों नहीं लिखा। उन्होंने कहा कि सीधा लिखने से सवाल उठते हैं, और घुमाकर लिखने से बस भौंहें। फिर उन्होंने अगले पन्ने पर व्यंग्य-स्तंभ दिखाया, जिसमें लेखक ने निगम को ‘महान जल-संरक्षक’ कहा था, क्योंकि जिस पानी के दाम बढ़े, वह आता ही कब है। पोती ने कहा कि यह तो ऊँट के मुँह में जीरा वाली शिकायत नहीं, सीधे ऊँट ग़ायब वाली बात है। दीनानाथ जी ने चाय की चुस्की ली और कहा, ‘देखते हैं, शायद अगले बजट में ऊँट भी लौट आए।’ पोती समझ गई कि दादा जी को उम्मीद कम है।

The 4 mistakes this level makes

  • सूत्रों के मुताबिक फ़ैसला हो गया है, इसलिए अब यह तय है। → सूत्रों के मुताबिक फ़ैसला हो गया है; आधिकारिक पुष्टि बाकी है। Attribution is not confirmation. Repeat the source, not the certainty.
  • कृपया अपना आसन ग्रहण करें और चाय का रसास्वादन करें। (to a friend at home) → बैठो, चाय पियो। Stacking tatsam words in a casual setting is register clash — comic unless intended.
  • मोटे अफ़सर ने फ़ाइल दबा दी। (as satire) → दफ़्तर ने फ़ाइल छह महीने ‘विचाराधीन’ रखी। Satire targets systems and power, never a person's body or identity.
  • वह विकलांग बेचारा पीछे बैठेगा। → दिव्यांग अतिथि के लिए आगे की पंक्ति में सुगम स्थान है। Person-first, role-based, no pity word; accessibility is arranged, not assumed.
