Subjunctive and hypotheticals

Subjunctive: जाऊँ/जाए

Wishes and maybes! मैं जाऊँ (that I go), वह जाए (that he go). चाहता हूँ कि… (I want that…), शायद… (maybe…), ताकि… (so that…)! Root + ऊँ/ए — short and sweet!

शायद, हो सकता है, काश — the ladder of possibility

Hindi grades possibility rather than stating it flatly. At the top: ज़रूर होगा (it must be so). Below it: हो सकता है (it may be). Lower: शायद (maybe). And below the line, the regret that changes nothing: काश वह आता! — if only he came. Each rung takes a different verb form, and that is what makes the ladder worth learning.

WordSay itMeaning
शायदśāyadmaybe, perhaps
हो सकता हैho saktā haiit is possible
ज़रूरzarūrcertainly; must be
काशkāśif only (with regret)
मुमकिनmumkinpossible
उम्मीदummedhope
अंदाज़ाandāzāguess, estimate
चांसcānschance (colloquial, borrowed from English)
एक ही बात, चार दर्जे की निश्चितता

Confidence runs through the form of होना and the subjunctive: ज़रूर होगा (future, confident), हो सकता है (present, open), शायद … हो (subjunctive, doubtful), and काश + subjunctive for a wish that is already lost. Note that शायद and हो सकता हैं are never used together: one rung at a time.

Watch out:
  • शायद हो सकता है — two hedges in one sentence; Hindi keeps one rung of certainty at a time.
  • काश with the plain past: काश वह आया is heard in speech but the careful form is काश वह आता!, with the habitual form carrying the regret.

सच तो यह है कि — conceding, then holding your ground

A B2 argument concedes before it pushes: कोई शक नहीं कि… मगर (no doubt that… but); सच तो यह है कि (the truth is that); यह सही है, लेकिन सवाल यह है कि… (that is right, but the question is…). Conceding is not giving in — it moves the argument to the point you actually want to defend, and the passive keeps the claim impersonal.

मुहावरे बोलचाल में — idioms that sound earned

A Hindi idiom used correctly does the work of a whole sentence: उसकी बात सुनकर मेरे मुँह में पानी आ गया is warmer than मुझे बहुत अच्छा लगा. But an idiom used at the wrong moment sounds borrowed. The rule is register first: muhavare belong in conversation and informal writing, not in a report — and never two in one sentence.

Culture note

हिन्दुस्तानी Hindustani is the ordinary spoken language of north India, and हिन्दी and उर्दू are its two standards — same grammar, same everyday words, different script and different loan layers. A shop conversation, a courtroom argument and a cricket commentary all run in the middle; what changes is how far a speaker pulls the same sentence towards Sanskrit on one side (धन्यवाद, सूचना, आवश्यकता) or Persian and Arabic on the other (शुक्रिया, ख़बर, ज़रूरत). A learner who can hear that pull is no longer translating.

Source (named, not linked): en.wikipedia.org, the article “Hindustani language”.

The 4 mistakes this level makes

  • मुझे यह किताब पसंद करती है। → मुझे यह किताब पसंद है। पसंद is a noun here, not a verb: the sentence is ‘to me this book is pleasing’, so no करती है and no agreement to chase.
  • वह अध्यक्ष से मिलने गया। (meaning: the chairman went to meet him) → वह अध्यक्ष से मिलने गया। (he went to meet the chairman) · अध्यक्ष उससे मिलने गया। (the chairman went to meet him) Hindi word order carries the roles. Move the ने/से phrase and the meaning moves with it — English can shuffle, Hindi mostly cannot.
  • यह बहुत ज़्यादा महँगा है, थोड़ा सस्ता कीजिए। → यह बहुत महँगा है, थोड़ा कम कीजिए। ज़्यादा plus महँगा doubles the intensity the wrong way, and सस्ता कीजिए asks the seller to make it cheap rather than to lower the price.
  • मैंने उसे फ़ोन किया था, लेकिन उसने नहीं उठाया। (as a present-tense failure) → मैंने उसे फ़ोन किया, लेकिन उसने नहीं उठाया। थी puts the attempt in a finished past that has nothing to do with now; a bare perfective is what a native speaker says about today.

Your task

The same sentence, pulled three ways Take one plain thought — for example ‘I need information about this’ — and write it three times: the everyday way, the Sanskrit-side way, and the Persian side. (ज़रूरत/आवश्यकता, ख़बर/सूचना are the pairs to start from.) Then read all three aloud and notice which one you would use to a friend, to a clerk, and in a letter. That gap is what B2 is made of.

After B2: the B2+ checkpoint

B2+ is not a certificate — it is the half-step between Hindi B2 and the next level, and it is on this site’s ladder because language schools use it. You are at B2+ when you can run a meeting, read a contract, disagree well. Get there by working the recall drills above and the review deck until nothing on this page surprises you, then open the C1 pages.
