Reading Literature Closely

Poetic Devices: Upamā, Rūpak, Anuprās

Hindi poetics names its tools precisely. उपमा compares with a marker (सा, जैसा, समान); रूपक fuses the two things with no marker; अनुप्रास repeats a sound; मानवीकरण gives a thing a human act. Scholars name the device, show the two terms, and say what the fusion adds that plain statement could not.

Simile vs metaphor in Hindi: the marker test

If a comparison word is present — सा/सी/से, जैसा, समान, सदृश, की तरह — it is उपमा. If the poet says the tenor IS the vehicle (चाँद-सा मुख → मुख-चंद्र, or ‘जीवन एक नदी है’), it is रूपक. In analysis, always name उपमेय (what is described) and उपमान (what it is likened to) and the साधारण धर्म (the shared quality). अनुप्रास is counted by repeated consonant onset; मानवीकरण by a human verb on a non-human subject.

उपमेय + (सा/जैसा/समान) + उपमान = उपमा ; उपमेय = उपमान (no marker) = रूपक

Realist Prose: Narrative Tense and Free Indirect Speech

The realist short-story tradition narrates in the simple past (गया, कहा), slides into the habitual past for routine (जाता था), and lets a character's thoughts leak into narration without ‘उसने सोचा कि’ — free indirect speech. Scholars track whose voice each sentence belongs to and how tense shifts manage time.

Tense layering and voice in narration

Backbone events: simple past (वह उठा, दरवाज़ा खोला). Background/routine: habitual past (रोज़ सुबह वह मंदिर जाता था). Earlier past: pluperfect (वह पहले ही जा चुका था). Free indirect speech keeps third person and past tense but uses the character's words, questions and particles: ‘अब क्या करे वह? पैसे तो थे नहीं।’ — no उसने सोचा, yet it is his thought. Spot it by exclamations, questions and तो/ही inside narration.

event = simple past · routine = -ता था · earlier = -चुका था · thought without tag = free indirect (3rd person + character's idiom)

Nayī Kavitā: Fragment, Line Break, Silence

Post-1950 Hindi poetry abandoned fixed metre for free verse: short lines, dropped verbs, images that sit side by side without ‘और’, and a final line that turns the poem. Scholars read the line break as punctuation, ask what the missing verb would have been, and describe the turn (मोड़) rather than paraphrasing the ‘message’.

Reading an ellipsis: supplying the verb

Free verse drops copulas and conjunctions: ‘खिड़की / एक चिड़िया / और फिर कुछ नहीं’. To analyse, (1) supply the most neutral verb in brackets (खिड़की [पर] एक चिड़िया [बैठी]), (2) treat each line break as a pause that changes emphasis, (3) find the turn — usually the last one or two lines reverse or widen the image. Describe what the poem withholds; do not invent a moral.

Sandhi: Where Sounds Meet

Tatsam Hindi words fuse at their joints by Sanskrit rules. विद्या + आलय = विद्यालय (अ+आ→आ), सूर्य + उदय = सूर्योदय (अ+उ→ओ), देव + इंद्र = देवेंद्र (अ+इ→ए). Scholars split a long word into its parts to read it, and know the three families: स्वर, व्यंजन, विसर्ग sandhi.

Samās: Compounds and Who Is in Charge

Hindi packs phrases into compounds (समास). The scholar's question is: which member is the head? तत्पुरुष — the last word rules (राजपुत्र = राजा का पुत्र); द्वंद्व — both equal (माता-पिता); बहुव्रीहि — neither, the compound names a third thing (चक्रपाणि = the one with a discus in hand, Vishnu); कर्मधारय — adjective + noun (नीलकमल); द्विगु — number first (त्रिलोक); अव्ययीभाव — indeclinable first (यथाशक्ति).

The head test

Expand the compound (विग्रह) and ask what it refers to. If the expansion ends on the last word (राजा का पुत्र → a पुत्र), it is तत्पुरुष (or कर्मधारय if the first is an adjective: नीला कमल). If you need ‘और’ (माता और पिता), द्वंद्व. If the expansion needs ‘वाला/जिसका’ and points outside (चक्र है पाणि में जिसके → विष्णु), बहुव्रीहि. A number first (तीन लोकों का समूह) → द्विगु. An adverb-like whole (शक्ति के अनुसार) → अव्ययीभाव. Note: पीतांबर as ‘yellow cloth’ is कर्मधारय; as ‘the one wearing yellow’ (Krishna) it is बहुव्रीहि — context decides.

Every Hindi sentence is an archaeological site. चाय came through Chinese via Persian; अलमारी, चाबी, पाव and बाल्टी are Portuguese; क़ानून, अदालत, सज़ा are Arabic via Persian; स्टेशन, रेल, बस are English; and the core — पानी, हाथ, आँख — is tadbhav from Sanskrit through Prakrit. Academic Writing in Hindi

The Research Abstract: Five Moves

A Hindi शोध-सार follows five moves in order: पृष्ठभूमि (background), उद्देश्य (aim), पद्धति (method), निष्कर्ष (findings), निहितार्थ (implications). Each move has fixed phrasing; the verbs are impersonal passive (किया गया, पाया गया). Scholars write it last, keep it under 200 words, and never put a claim in the abstract that the paper does not support.

Impersonal passive and move signals

Research Hindi hides the researcher: अध्ययन किया गया (a study was conducted), आँकड़े एकत्र किए गए (data were collected), पाया गया कि (it was found that). Move signals: पृष्ठभूमि — ‘…के संदर्भ में’, ‘हाल के वर्षों में’; उद्देश्य — ‘प्रस्तुत अध्ययन का उद्देश्य … का विश्लेषण करना है’; पद्धति — ‘… प्रतिदर्श पर … पद्धति से’; निष्कर्ष — ‘परिणाम दर्शाते हैं कि’; निहितार्थ — ‘ये निष्कर्ष … के लिए उपयोगी हो सकते हैं’. Hedge findings (संकेत देते हैं), do not overclaim (सिद्ध करते हैं).

पृष्ठभूमि → उद्देश्य → पद्धति (प्रतिदर्श + उपकरण) → निष्कर्ष (hedged) → निहितार्थ

The Critical Review: Praise, Fault, Verdict

A Hindi समीक्षा of a book, film or paper is not a summary. It states the work's aim, judges how far it is met, gives evidence from the text, and ends with a verdict for a named reader. The tone is firm and courteous: faults are named precisely, never sneered at; praise is specific, never gushing.

Thesis Paragraphs: Claim, Counter, Synthesis

An academic paragraph in Hindi makes one claim, supports it, faces the strongest counter-argument honestly, and ends by synthesising — not by repeating the claim louder. The connectives are a toolkit: अतः, तथापि, इसके विपरीत, दूसरी ओर, निस्संदेह … किंतु, इस प्रकार. Scholars give the opponent their best case before answering it.

Steel-manning and the synthesis move

Paragraph skeleton: (1) claim — ‘यह तर्क दिया जा सकता है कि …’; (2) support — ‘क्योंकि … ; उदाहरणार्थ …’; (3) strongest counter, stated fairly — ‘इसके विपरीत, आलोचकों का मत है कि …, और यह आपत्ति निराधार नहीं है’; (4) answer — ‘तथापि, यह आपत्ति … को ध्यान में नहीं रखती’; (5) synthesis — ‘इस प्रकार, … और … दोनों को स्वीकार करते हुए, … अधिक उपयुक्त प्रतीत होता है’. Never ‘refute’ by adjective (मूर्खतापूर्ण तर्क); refute by missing premise or counter-evidence.

Culture note

Hindi scholarship inherits two toolkits. From Sanskrit poetics come the names of devices (अलंकार), the rules of sound-junction (संधि) and compounding (समास), and a habit of precise classification. From the modern university come the research abstract, the critical review and the argued paragraph, written in an impersonal register that hides the author and hedges every claim. Between them sits the literature itself: the Chhayavad poets' dense imagery, the realist short story's tense-shifting narration, and the free verse of the नई कविता that trusts the reader to supply the missing verb. A scholar reads with both toolkits open, and writes so that a stranger could check every judgement against the text.

The 4 mistakes this level makes

  • ‘रात की चादर’ उपमा है। → ‘रात की चादर’ रूपक है — कोई ‘सी/जैसी’ नहीं है। The marker test: no comparison word means metaphor, not simile.
  • नर + इंद्र = नरैंद्र → नरेंद्र (गुण: अ + इ → ए) वृद्धि (ऐ) applies only when the second vowel is ए/ऐ.
  • मैंने सिद्ध किया कि योजना असफल है। (30 उत्तरदाता) → परिणाम संकेत देते हैं कि योजना अपेक्षित प्रभाव नहीं दिखा सकी। Impersonal voice and a hedge sized to the sample.
  • यह तर्क मूर्खतापूर्ण है। → यह तर्क इस पूर्वधारणा पर टिका है कि …, जो आँकड़ों से पुष्ट नहीं होती। Refute a premise or cite counter-evidence; never refute by adjective.

Produce a four-part dossier in Hindi: (1) a close reading of 300 words that names at least four devices with tenor and vehicle, and tracks one tense shift or one line-break turn; (2) a word-study table of fifteen words from the text sorted by layer (तत्सम, तद्भव, देशज, अरबी-फ़ारसी, पुर्तगाली, अंग्रेज़ी), with any sandhi or samās split; (3) a 150-word abstract of your own reading in the five moves; (4) a five-move argued paragraph defending one interpretive claim about the text, with the strongest counter-reading stated fairly before your synthesis.
