Register and Respect

Choosing Pronouns and Verb Forms

Hindi marks respect in two places at once: the pronoun (तू, तुम, आप) and the verb that agrees with it. Choosing the right pronoun but leaving the verb in the familiar form is the most common register error at this level. Read every sentence twice: once for the pronoun, once for the verb ending.

WordSay itMeaning
आपāpyou (formal, respectful)
तुमtumyou (familiar)
तूtūyou (intimate; to a child)
जीjīrespect particle added to a name or word
कृपयाkṛpayāplease (formal)
सम्मानपूर्वकsammānpūrvakrespectfully
बुज़ुर्गbuzurgelder
लहजाlahjātone; manner of speaking
Pronoun and verb agreement

आप takes plural verb forms even when one person is addressed: आप आते हैं, आप बोलिए। तुम takes the second-person familiar forms (आते हो, बोलो). तू takes the singular -ता/-ती ending (आता है). The respect of a name is carried by जी, not by the pronoun alone: गुप्ता जी, माँ जी. A respectful pronoun with a familiar verb sounds rude, and a familiar pronoun with a formal verb sounds confused.

आप + plural-agreeing verb (हैं, -िए, -ेंगे) · तुम + -ो / -ते form · तू + -ता / -ती form
WordSay itMeaning
आप कब आएँगे?āp kab āẽge?When will you (formal) come?
तुम कब आओगे?tum kab āoge?When will you (familiar) come?
तू कब आएगा?tū kab āegā?When will you (intimate) come?
Watch out:
  • आप आता है → आप आते हैं: the verb takes plural agreement with आप even for one person.
  • Using तू with a stranger, an elder or an official sounds deliberately rude.
  • Dropping जी from a name: 'प्रकाश, आइए' is abrupt; 'प्रकाश जी, आइए' is warm.

Requests, Permission and Politeness

Requests in Hindi are shaped by the verb and by a softening frame. The formal imperative (-िए) and permission questions (क्या मैं … सकता/सकती हूँ?) let you ask without commanding. Politeness at this level means choosing the frame that fits the cost of the request to the listener.

WordSay itMeaning
अनुमतिanumatipermission
कष्टkaṣṭtrouble; inconvenience
माफ़ कीजिएmāfa kījieexcuse me; sorry
सहायताsahāyatāhelp; assistance
अनुरोधanurodhrequest
सुविधाsuvidhāconvenience
यदि आप चाहेंyadi āp cāheṃif you wish
मेहरबानीmeharbānīkindness; favour
Softened requests

Use the -िए imperative with आप (बैठिए, लिखिए). Soften with क्या मैं … सकता/सकती हूँ? for permission and क्या आप … सकते/सकती हैं? for help. The conditional frame यदि आपको कष्ट न हो, तो … lowers the cost of the request. The verb सकना agrees with the speaker's gender: सकता for a man, सकती for a woman.

क्या + मैं / आप + verb stem + सकता/सकती/सकते + हूँ/हैं ? · यदि आपको कष्ट न हो, तो + polite imperative
WordSay itMeaning
क्या मैं यहाँ बैठ सकता हूँ?kyā maĩ yahā̃ baiṭh saktā hū̃?May I sit here? (said by a man)
क्या आप मेरी सहायता कर सकती हैं?kyā āp merī sahāyatā kar saktī haĩ?Could you help me? (said to a woman)
यदि आपको कष्ट न हो, तो एक गिलास पानी दीजिए।yadi āpko kaṣṭ na ho, to ek gilās pānī dījie.If it is no trouble, please give me a glass of water.
Watch out:
  • Bare imperative with a stranger: 'पानी दो' sounds like an order; 'पानी दीजिए' is the courteous form.
  • A woman saying सकता हूँ: the verb must agree with her gender: सकती हूँ.
  • Asking an elder क्या तुम मदद करोगे?: use आप with the -िए or क्या आप … सकते हैं? frame.

Writing a Formal Letter

Formal Hindi letters follow a stable frame: the addressee line, the subject line, a respectful salutation, a body written in the impersonal passive, and a formal closing. Mastery here is about frame and tone, not long sentences. Keep one register from the first line to the last.

WordSay itMeaning
सेवा मेंsevā mẽto (the service of) — opening of a formal letter
विषयviṣaysubject
महोदयmahodayasir (formal address)
आदरणीयādaraṇīyrespected
सादरsādarwith respect (closing)
भवदीयbhavadīyyours faithfully (closing)
सूचित किया जाता हैsūcit kiyā jātā haiis hereby informed
निवेदनnivedanpetition; respectful request
Frame, passive and closing

The body uses the impersonal passive to keep the tone objective: निवेदन किया जाता है, सूचित किया जाता है। The salutation depends on the addressee: महोदय for a man in office, महोदया for a woman, and आदरणीय with a name for a known person. Close with सादर or भवदीय, never with the informal आपका मित्र.

सेवा में, [title] · विषय: … · आदरणीय / महोदय · body in passive · सादर, [name]
WordSay itMeaning

आपको सूचित किया जाता है कि परीक्षा एक सप्ताह बाद होगी।āpko sūcit kiyā jātā hai ki parīkṣā ek saptāh bād hogī.You are informed that the examination will be held a week later.

Watch out:
  • Closing an official letter with आपका मित्र or बाय.
  • Switching from आप in the body to तुम or तू: keep one register throughout.
  • Writing a body with no actionable request, so the reader does not know what to do.

Clauses with कि, जो and जब

At this level sentences stop being chains of short statements. Hindi builds complexity with subordinate clauses: कि introduces reported or asserted content, जो introduces a relative clause that describes a noun, and जब introduces a time clause. Formal writing often places the subordinate clause first and the main clause after it, marked by वह, वे or तब.

WordSay itMeaning
किkithat (complementiser)
जोjowho; which (relative)
जबjabwhen
तबtabthen; at that time
माननाmānnāto accept; to believe
स्पष्ट होनाspaṣṭ honāto become clear
परिणामpariṇāmresult; outcome
प्रक्रियाprakriyāprocess
Subordinate clauses

Use कि after verbs of saying, thinking and knowing: मुझे लगता है कि … and यह स्पष्ट है कि … . Use जो to describe a noun: वह व्यक्ति जो कल आया था … . The head noun is picked up again in the main clause by वह, वे or यह. Use जब for time: जब बारिश रुकी, तब हम निकले। A long जो clause or जब clause needs its correlative (वह / तब) to close it; without it the sentence hangs.

जब परिणाम आए, तब उसने फ़ैसला बदला।jab pariṇām āe, tab usne faisalā badlā.When the results came, he changed the decision.
Watch out:
  • Using कि where a relative clause is needed: 'वह आदमी कि कल आया' should be 'वह आदमी जो कल आया'.
  • Dropping the correlative (वह, वे or तब) after a long जो or जब clause, which leaves the sentence hanging.
  • Repeating the same noun in both clauses when वह or यह would do: it reads as clumsy in formal prose.

Conditions and Concession

Conditional and concessive clauses let you state what would follow if something happened, or admit a point while holding your position. Hindi uses यदि or अगर with तो for conditions, and चाहे … फिर भी or भले ही … तो भी for concession. The verb in the if-clause is usually in the subjunctive, which is the most common reason learners sound uncertain.

शर्तśartacondition

Conditions and concession

The यदि / अगर clause takes the subjunctive (आएँ, हो, करें) and the main clause with तो states the consequence. चाहे and भले ही introduce a concession, and फिर भी or तो भी mark the contrast in the main clause. बावजूद with a noun means 'despite': इसके बावजूद. Use the subjunctive in these clauses even when the situation is real, because the grammar expresses the hypothetical frame.

यदि / अगर + subjunctive, तो + consequence · चाहे / भले ही + subjunctive, फिर भी / तो भी + main clause
WordSay itMeaning

चाहे बारिश हो, फिर भी परीक्षा होगी।cāhe bārish ho, phir bhī parīkṣā hogī.Even if it rains, the exam will be held.

Watch out:
  • Using the indicative after यदि in a hypothetical: 'यदि आप आते हो' should be 'यदि आप आएँ'.
  • Pairing भले ही with बावजूद in the same clause, which repeats the concession.
  • Omitting the subjunctive in चाहे or भले ही clauses, which makes them sound like plain 'though' statements.

Cause, Result and Purpose

This lesson separates three relations that learners often blur: cause (क्योंकि, चूँकि), result (इसलिए, अतः, परिणामस्वरूप) and purpose (ताकि, जिससे). Cause and result are about what has happened, so they take the indicative. Purpose is about an intended aim, so it takes the subjunctive. That difference is why ताकि and इसलिए are not interchangeable.

Cause, result, purpose

क्योंकि and चूँकि give a reason for something already true, with the indicative: वह देर से आया, क्योंकि ट्रेन लेट थी। इसलिए, अतः and परिणामस्वरूप state the consequence, also indicative. ताकि and जिससे state an intended aim, with the subjunctive: उसने जल्दी काम पूरा किया ताकि शाम को छुट्टी मिले। Confusing purpose with result is the most frequent error at this level.

Telling, Reporting and Arguing

Reported Speech

Reported speech in Hindi introduces what someone said with कि and changes the person of the speaker to third person. Verbs of reporting (कहना, बताना, पूछना) carry the report. A statement uses कि. A yes/no question uses कि or क्या. A wh-question keeps its question word (कब, कहाँ) and drops the question marker. The honorific आप in direct speech usually becomes वे or वह when the speaker is reported.

Indirect speech

Introduce a statement with कि: उसने कहा कि वह कल आएगा। Shift the first person to the third: 'मैं आऊँगा' becomes 'वह आएगा'. Narrating the Past

Past narration in Hindi separates three aspects: the simple past (किया), the imperfective past (कर रहा था for in progress, करता था for habitual), and the perfect or pluperfect (कर चुका था for completed before another past event). A good story uses the simple past for the main events and the other aspects for background, habit and what had already happened.

Aspect in narration

The simple past (गया) marks a completed event. The imperfective past has two forms: कर रहा था (was doing, in progress) and करता था (used to do, habit). The pluperfect कर चुका था shows an event completed before another past event. In a story, the main event is in the simple past, and the background uses the imperfective or pluperfect. Choosing the wrong aspect changes the meaning: वह खड़ा था is 'he was standing', वह खड़ा हुआ is 'he stood up'.

Opinion and Measured Disagreement

Advanced discussion needs ways to take a position and still show respect for the other side. Hindi uses opinion frames (मेरे विचार में, मुझे लगता है कि), agreement with a limit (मैं सहमत हूँ, परंतु …), and disagreement that softens the claim (आपकी बात सही है, पर …). The skill is to disagree with the claim and its evidence, not with the person.

Opinion and qualified disagreement

Frame an opinion as yours: मेरे विचार में, मुझे लगता है कि. Show agreement with a limit: मैं आपसे सहमत हूँ, परंतु इसका एक पहलू और भी है। Disagree with the claim, not the speaker: आपका तर्क दिलचस्प है, पर प्रमाण इसके पक्ष में पर्याप्त नहीं है। Use hedges such as शायद, संभवतः and अधिकांशतः to keep the argument proportionate. Do not use both परंतु and पर in one sentence; it doubles the contrast.

Hindi B3 beyond the drills — culture, reading, listening, idioms

These sections are written for Hindi B3 specifically. The reading text is EkGuru's own; the culture note cites where its facts come from; the idioms and the mistakes list are the ones this level actually trips over.

Culture note

Hindi has three broad registers that can carry the same idea. Colloquial speech says 'चलो, चलते हैं'. A courteous register, used with strangers and elders, says 'चलिए, चलें?'. Formal writing says 'आपसे निवेदन है कि कार्यक्रम में पधारें'. Official notices, newspaper editorials and government letters rely on the formal register, so reading one short official notice each week is a practical way to absorb it. Choose the register by the listener, not by how well you know the words.

The 3 mistakes this level makes

  • तू बड़े बुज़ुर्ग से बोला, 'आप कब आओगे?' → आपने बड़े बुज़ुर्ग से पूछा, 'आप कब आएँगे?' Mixing registers inside one sentence. The pronoun and the verb must stay on the same level of respect.
  • प्रिय अधिकारी, आप यह फ़ॉर्म भर दो। → आदरणीय महोदय, कृपया यह फ़ॉर्म भरिए। In a formal letter a bare -ो imperative reads as an order; use the -िए form and a formal salutation.
  • यह नीति सफल है। → मेरे विचार में यह नीति सफल हो सकती है, परंतु प्रमाण अभी सीमित हैं। An opinion stated as an absolute fact with no frame or hedge reads as dogmatic.
