Family words in Kannada

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

ಅಮ್ಮ, ಅಪ್ಪ, ಅಣ್ಣ, ಅಕ್ಕ — family, including the elder-younger split.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Kannada spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Parents anchor everything: ಅಮ್ಮ (amma — mother) and ಅಪ್ಪ (appa — father) — the first words of every Kannada child and the warmest words in the language. ಅಮ್ಮ calls across market crowds; ಅಪ್ಪ advises over evening coffee. Learn them with love, not just memory.

Siblings split by age, never blend: ಅಣ್ಣ (elder brother), ತಮ್ಮ (younger brother), ಅಕ್ಕ (elder sister), ತಂಗಿ (younger sister). There is no plain brother — every brother is elder or younger, and the word you choose declares the hierarchy. Ask ages early; address correctly always.

Grandparents rule the roost: ಅಜ್ಜ (ajja — grandfather) and ಅಜ್ಜಿ (ajji — grandmother) — storytellers, sweet-sneakers, family courts of final appeal. ಅಜ್ಜಿ ಕತೆ (grandmother's tale) is a whole genre; ಅಜ್ಜ advice ends arguments. Respect flows upward; treats flow down.

The wider net holds daily life: ಮಾವ (uncle), ಅತ್ತೆ (auntie), ಗಂಡ (husband), ಹೆಂಡತಿ (wife), ಕುಟುಂಬ (family itself). ನನ್ನ ಕುಟುಂಬ (my family) introduces them all — practise the sentence with your own names filled in, because you will say it at every Kannada gathering forever.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on ಅಜ್ಜ, ಅಜ್ಜಿ. Kannada grandparents share short sweet names: ಅಜ್ಜ (ajja — grandfather) and ಅಜ್ಜಿ (ajji — grandmother) — no side-splitting needed. Learners need the big six first; the full map unfolds at weddings.

Transfer practice. Choose one real relationship and practise the matching term in a short introduction. Family titles can carry age, affection, respect, and local habit, so they do not always map neatly onto English labels. When unsure, listen to how the family addresses one another and ask before using an intimate form. Start from ಅಮ್ಮ (amma) and decide whether it fits the situation before using it elsewhere.

Context check. For Family words, “Family” belongs to a particular setting. A common mistake is to treat an English gloss as universal; read the language-specific note above and ask a fluent speaker when local usage differs.

Questions learners ask

Why two words for brother?

Yes — Kannada splits siblings by age: ಅಣ್ಣ (elder brother) versus ತಮ್ಮ (younger brother), ಅಕ್ಕ (elder sister) versus ತಂಗಿ (younger sister). Age order is grammar in Kannada families.

Can I call strangers anna and akka?

ಅಣ್ಣ (anna) and ಅಕ್ಕ (akka) address any slightly-older man or woman — shopkeepers, colleagues, strangers. It borrows family warmth for the whole world.

How do I ask about family?

ನಿಮ್ಮ ಕುಟುಂಬದಲ್ಲಿ ಎಷ್ಟು ಜನ? (how many people in your family?) — then listen proudly to the answer. Family questions are love in Kannada culture.
