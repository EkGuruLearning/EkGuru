Kannada

Kannada grammar, plainly explained

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Verb last, respect built in — Kannada grammar in plain English.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Kannada spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Kannada sentences end with the verb: ನಾನು ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಓದುತ್ತೇನೆ (I — book — read). Subject first, object middle, verb last — SOV, the order of half the world's languages. Rearrange your English instincts once, and every Kannada sentence parses cleanly.

Small endings do heavy work: -ಅಲ್ಲಿ (alli — in/on) places things — ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ (at home), ಮೇಜಿನ ಮೇಲೆ (on the table); -ಜೊತೆ (jote — with) joins company — ಸ್ನೇಹಿತನ ಜೊತೆ (with a friend); -ಗೆ (ge) aims motion and wanting — ಶಾಲೆಗೆ (to school), ನನಗೆ ಬೇಕು (I want). Endings, not word order, carry Kannada meaning.

Questions rise at the end: ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತೀರಿ? (where are you going?) — ಎಲ್ಲಿ (where), ಏನು (what), ಯಾರು (who), ಏಕೆ (why), ಹೇಗೆ (how), ಯಾವಾಗ (when). Yes-no questions add -ಆ (-aa): ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿದ್ರಾ? (have you eaten?). Question words plus rising tone — asking is easy.

Negation splits three ways: ಇಲ್ಲ (illa) denies existence — ಹಣ ಇಲ್ಲ (no money); verb endings deny actions — ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತಿಲ್ಲ (I don't know); ಬೇಡ (beda) refuses — ಸಕ್ಕರೆ ಬೇಡ (no sugar, thanks). Three no's for three jobs — Kannada refuses precisely.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on -ಗೆ (ge). Kannada's dative ending aims verbs at people and places: ನನಗೆ (to me), ಶಾಲೆಗೆ (to school), ಮನೆಗೆ (homeward). Sentences like ನನಗೆ ಅರ್ಥವಾಯಿತು (to-me it-is-understood = I understand) reveal Kannada's worldview: experiences come TO you.

Questions learners ask

Where does the verb go?

Put the verb last: ನಾನು ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಓದುತ್ತೇನೆ (I book read). Subject-Object-Verb, always. English instincts will fight you for a week; then SOV feels like home.

How do I say I want something?

ನನಗೆ (nanage — to me), ನಿನಗೆ (ninage — to you familiar), ನಿಮಗೆ (nimage — to you formal). ನನಗೆ ನೀರು ಬೇಕು (I want water) is literally to-me water is-wanted — Kannada wants flow toward people.

How do this and that work?

ಇದು (idu — this, near me), ಅದು (adu — that, far). ಇದು ಏನು? (what is this?) points at anything within reach; mastery takes one shopping trip.
