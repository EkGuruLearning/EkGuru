Kannada among Indian languages

Dravidian roots, Sanskrit friends, Hindi neighbours — Kannada's family place.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Kannada spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Kannada anchors the Dravidian west: ತೆಲುಗು (Telugu), ತಮಿಳು (Tamil), ಮಲಯಾಳಂ (Malayalam) — siblings, not dialects. Shared grammar bones, shared ancient words, four proud literatures. Learn Kannada and Telugu signboards start whispering.

The north speaks Indo-Aryan: ಹಿಂದಿ (Hindi), ಬಂಗಾಳಿ (Bengali), ಮರಾಠಿ (Marathi), ಗುಜರಾತಿ (Gujarati), ಪಂಜಾಬಿ (Punjabi) — Sanskrit-descended cousins. Different family, shared nation: ಭಾರತ (India) holds both, and ಅನುವಾದ (translation) bridges all.

Sanskrit threads everything: ಸಂಸ್ಕೃತ lent Kannada thousands of formal words. Pure-Kannada movements coin Dravidian replacements; daily speech mixes happily. Both registers are real Kannada; learners need both.

Unity crowns diversity: ಏಕತೆ (unity) — 22 official languages, hundreds of mother tongues, one conversation. Kannada people learn Hindi for Delhi, English for globe, Kannada for soul. Every Indian language you meet makes your Kannada richer.

Questions learners ask

Is Kannada related to Hindi?

Distant cousins — Kannada is Dravidian; Hindi, Bengali and Marathi are Indo-Aryan. Shared nation, shared Sanskrit loans, different grandmothers.

Which language is closest to Kannada?

ತೆಲುಗು — the closest sibling, neighbours for a thousand years, scripts that look like twins. The kannada-vs-telugu page maps both.

Why so much Sanskrit in Kannada?

Centuries of poetry, prayer and scholarship — ಸಂಸ್ಕೃತ words fill formal Kannada while grammar stays purely Dravidian. Sanskrit lends vocabulary; Kannada keeps its soul.
