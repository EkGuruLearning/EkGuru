Kannada

Core Kannada vocabulary

Water, money, time, today — the everyday words that unlock daily life.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Kannada spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Survival runs on nouns: ನೀರು (neeru — water), ಆಹಾರ (aahaara — food), ಮನೆ (mane — home), ಹಣ (hana — money), ಸಮಯ (samaya — time). Five words, and you can ask for water, find home, handle money and ask the time — the entire first day in a new city.

Movement words come next: ರಸ್ತೆ (raste — road), ಬಸ್ (bas — bus), ಅಂಗಡಿ (angadi — shop), ಶಾಲೆ (shaale — school). Karnataka moves by bus and foot; knowing ಬಸ್ ನಿಲ್ದಾಣ ಎಲ್ಲಿ? (where is the bus stand?) saves more time than any app.

Opinions need adjectives: ಒಳ್ಳೆ (olle — good), ದೊಡ್ಡ (dodda — big), ಚಿಕ್ಕ (chikka — small), ಹೊಸ (hosa — new), ಹಳೆಯ (haleya — old). ಒಳ್ಳೆಯದು (olleyadu — that's good) approves everything from food to plans — the most useful single opinion in Kannada.

Weather and mood close the kit: ಮಳೆ (male — rain) matters half the year, ಬಿಸಿಲು (bisilu — sun/heat) the other half. ಮಳೆ ಬರುತ್ತಿದೆ (rain is coming) starts conversations with strangers; in Karnataka, weather talk is friendship's front door.

A note on ಇಂದು, ನಾಳೆ, ನಿನ್ನೆ. Kannada time words pivot around today: ಇಂದು (indu — today), ನಾಳೆ (naale — tomorrow), ನಿನ್ನೆ (ninne — yesterday). Master this trio and half your daily sentences assemble themselves — ನಾಳೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (see you tomorrow) alone is worth the lesson.

Questions learners ask

Which words pay off fastest?

Time words first: ಇಂದು (today), ನಾಳೆ (tomorrow), ನಿನ್ನೆ (yesterday), ಈಗ (now). You will use them ten times a day — shopping, planning, chatting — while rare nouns gather dust.

Is mixing English okay?

Kannada happily absorbs English — ಬಸ್ (bus), ಟಿಕೆಟ್ (ticket), ಆಫೀಸು (office). Use the pure Kannada word when you know it, the loanword when you don't. Fluency first, purity later.

Nouns or verbs first?

Nouns first, verbs second, adjectives third. With fifty nouns and the twelve verbs from the verbs page, you can point, ask and survive — grammar glues them later.
