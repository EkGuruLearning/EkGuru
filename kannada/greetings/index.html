Saying hello the Kannada way

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ opens every door in Karnataka — but mornings, welcomes and goodbyes each have their own grace.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Kannada spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara) is the one greeting that never fails. Folded hands, a small nod, and it works with strangers, elders, shopkeepers and officials — exactly the people a learner meets first. Say it clearly, hold the folded hands half a second, and Karnataka opens to you.

Mornings get their own greeting: ಶುಭೋದಯ (shubhodaya — good morning), heard in offices, schools and darshinis until about eleven. Evenings soften to ಶುಭಸಂಜೆ (shubhasanje). Time-stamped greetings signal that you live in the language, not just visit it.

Welcomes are grand in Kannada culture: ಸ್ವಾಗತ (svaagata — welcome) opens homes, shops and hearts. Guests are greeted standing, often with water or coffee before a single question. Return the warmth — ಬಂದೆ, ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (I've come, thank you) — and you are family within minutes.

Goodbyes promise return, never end: ಮತ್ತೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (we'll meet again) is the everyday farewell; ಜಾಗ್ರತೆ (jaagrate — take care) adds affection. Leave every conversation with a ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ and a smile — in Karnataka, how you leave is remembered longer than how you came.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ vs ಆರಾಮಿದ್ದೀರಾ. ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (chennaagiddeeraa) is the everyday are you well? — warm, quick, everywhere. ಆರಾಮಿದ್ದೀರಾ? is its relaxed elder cousin, heard over filter coffee. Start with ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ; the rest arrives with ajjis.

Questions learners ask

Can I just say namaskaara everywhere?

Yes, and nobody will mind — ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ with folded hands is understood and loved everywhere Kannada is spoken. With close friends a smile and ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (are you well?) feels warmer.

How do I greet an elder respectfully?

ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ with folded hands and a small nod, and ನೀವು (neevu — formal you) until invited closer. Add ಅಣ್ಣ (anna) or ಅಕ್ಕ (akka) for someone slightly older — it warms everything instantly.

How do Kannada people say goodbye?

ಮತ್ತೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (matte sigona — we'll meet again) is the warm standard. ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (hogi barteeni — I'll go and come back) is the classic respectful farewell — receive it with a smile and a nod.
