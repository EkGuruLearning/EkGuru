Find your starting point

Private by design. Nothing you pick here is saved or uploaded. The suggestion comes from simple rules — your goal, level, time and reading preference — not from AI or a profile.

What the three questions actually are

The form above asks only what changes the answer. You can read the whole rule set here, decide for yourself, and skip the form:

1 · Your goal

If you chooseThe plan starts with
Everyday conversationGreetings, numbers, family and food — the words that come up in the first week of using the language, then present-tense sentences you can actually say out loud.
TravelAirport, hotel, directions, ordering food and asking a price, drilled as whole sentences rather than word lists.
SpeakingSound first: pronunciation, rhythm and the phrases that carry a conversation, with the script introduced later so reading never blocks speaking.
Reading & writingThe script itself — letters in stroke order, then reading practice, then writing your own sentences.
GrammarHow the language fits together: word order, verb endings, gender, the questions learners ask on day thirty.
Family & heritageHome vocabulary and the respectful forms you use with grandparents and elders, plus the register that sounds warm rather than textbook.

2 · Your level

Three honest options: complete beginner (you cannot yet read a sign), some basics (you know greetings and numbers), conversational (you can hold a simple conversation). If you are between two, take the lower one and finish it quickly — the eleven-rung ladder explains where each one sits, including what A1 to C2 actually mean.

3 · The time you can give

5–10 minutes a day, 15 minutes, or 30 and more. This is the honest variable: a 15-minute habit beats a two-hour Sunday, and the plan is built to be finished in the time you really have rather than the time you wish you had. Whatever you pick, the practice and quiz pages on this site are sized to that same sitting.

What you get at the end

A short, specific route: which lesson to open first, which practice set goes with it, and what to do the following week. Hindi is fully built; the other languages are labelled honestly — a real starter pack, or "coming", with no empty lessons pretending to be a course.

Prefer to look around first? Every free guide, the course list, or a tutor for the conversations the site cannot have with you.
