Formal and informal Gujarati

તમે or તું? આવો or આવ? Respect levels, decoded.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Gujarati spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Gujarati has two yous: તમે (formal and plural — strangers, elders, officials) and તું (familiar — friends, children, family). English erased this distinction centuries ago; Gujarati keeps it razor-sharp. Every conversation opens with an invisible choice — make it consciously, and make it respectful.

Verbs mirror the split: આવો (please come) versus આવ (come!), કહો (please say) versus કહે (say!), જાઓ (please go) versus જા (go!). The -ો ending is pure respect — same verb, different universe. Stems bark; -ો invites.

Giving and forgiving show the range: આપો (please give) versus આપ (give, familiar), માફ કરજો (please forgive) versus માફ કર (forgive, familiar). Beggars and kings both hear આપો — politeness in Gujarati costs nothing and buys everything.

Strangers get titles: સાહેબ (sir), ભાઈ (brother), બહેન (sister). સાહેબ, મદદ કરો (sir, please help) opens any door; barking orders at strangers closes them. Gujarati warmth is earned with two syllables of respect.

Questions learners ask

When do I use તમે?

તમે for every adult you don't know well — shopkeepers, officials, elders, strangers. તું waits for close friends, children and family. Wrong-side errors embarrass; learners are forgiven, but respect first is always safest.

How do I make a command polite?

Use the -ો form: આવ becomes આવો, જા becomes જાઓ, કહે becomes કહો. One ending, instant politeness — the highest-value sound in Gujarati.

How do I address strangers?

સાહેબ (sir) and બહેન for slightly-older strangers. સાહેબ, સમય શું છે? (sir, what time is it?). Better than pointing and shouting in any language.
