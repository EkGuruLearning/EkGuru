Gujarati for absolute beginners

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Zero to first conversation — your first week of Gujarati, step by step.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Gujarati spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Day one speaks: નમસ્તે (hello), હા (yes), ના (no) — say them to mirrors, pets, shopkeepers. Pronunciation need not be perfect; attempts earn smiles and corrections. Speaking starts speaking; nothing else does.

Days two to four add survival: આભાર (thanks), માફ કરજો (sorry), પાણી (water), કેટલા? (how much?). Four days, seven words, infinite transactions. Survival Gujarati fits in a pocket and works everywhere.

Days five to seven build sentences: હું શિક્ષક છું (I am a teacher), મને ચા જોઈએ છે (I want tea), તમે કેમ છો? (how are you?). Frames plus words equal conversation — slots fill fast when frames stand ready.

Week two opens the script: અ આ ઇ ઈ, then ક ખ ગ ઘ — Gujarati script through familiar words. Read what you already say; letters become friends, not walls. Speak-first learners read faster — motivation decodes.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on શરૂઆત. શરૂઆત (sharuaat — beginning) is the bravest word in Gujarati — આજથી શરૂઆત (starting from today). Every fluent speaker once said their first broken નમસ્તે. Begin badly; continue daily; arrive surely.

Transfer practice. Select three forms you can use this week and attach each to a real routine: greeting someone, asking a simple question, or describing what you need. Practise for a few minutes on separate days. If recall fails, reveal the answer, say it once, and try again later rather than copying it repeatedly. Start from શરૂઆત (sharuaat) and decide whether it fits the situation before using it elsewhere.

Questions learners ask

What are my first seven words?

નમસ્તે, હા, ના, આભાર, માફ કરજો, સારું, હું — seven words that open every door in Gujarat. Speak them from day one.

Script first or speech first?

No — speak first in Roman letters, add Gujarati script in week two. Speakers stay motivated; script-drillers quit. Talk today, read tomorrow.

How much daily practice?

Twenty focused minutes: five words, one dialogue, one song line. Daily beats marathon — streaks compound into fluency.
