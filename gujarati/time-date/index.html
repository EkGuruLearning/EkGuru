Time and dates in Gujarati

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

કેટલા વાગ્યા? સોમવાર to રવિવાર — time, days and dates.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Gujarati spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Clock time strikes: કેટલા વાગ્યા? (what time?), એક વાગ્યો (one o'clock), પાંચ વાગ્યા (five o'clock). સાડાપાંચ (5:30), પોણાસાત (6:45), સવાછ (6:15) — halves and quarters sing. Ask time daily; numbers stick.

Day parts flow: સવાર (morning), બપોર (afternoon), સાંજ (evening), રાત (night). સવારે (in the morning), રાત્રે (at night) — suffixes time the day. Parts first, precision after.

Weeks cycle planet-wise: સોમવાર (Monday) through રવિવાર (Sunday) — Moon-day to Sun-day. આજે સોમવાર છે (today is Monday) — date yourself daily. Weekdays rhythm life.

Dates frame plans: આજે (today), કાલે (tomorrow), ગઈકાલે (yesterday), તારીખ (date). કાલે મળીશું? (shall we meet tomorrow?) — propose, confirm, arrive. Plans in Gujarati keep in Gujarati.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on વાગ્યા. વાગ્યા (vaagyaa — has struck) makes time musical — કેટલા વાગ્યા? (how much has struck?), પાંચ વાગ્યા (five has struck). Clocks strike, bells ring, Gujaratis ask poetically. Time doesn't pass; it chimes.

Questions learners ask

How do I ask the time?

કેટલા વાગ્યા? (how much has struck?) — answer પાંચ વાગ્યા (five o'clock). Time strikes in Gujarati; ask it everywhere.

Days of the week?

સોમવાર મંગળવાર બુધવાર ગુરુવાર શુક્રવાર શનિવાર રવિવાર — chant the week; planet-days roll musically.

Today, tomorrow, yesterday?

આજે (today), કાલે (tomorrow), ગઈકાલે (yesterday), પરમદિવસે (day after/before). Four words frame every plan.
