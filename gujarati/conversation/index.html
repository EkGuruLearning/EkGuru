Gujarati

Real Gujarati conversation

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

હું, તમે, હા, ના — hold your first real Gujarati conversations.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Gujarati spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

People anchor talk: હું (I), તમે (you formal), તે (he/she), તેઓ (they). હું ગુજરાતી શીખું છું (I'm learning Gujarati) — gender shows on verbs, proudly. Introduce yourself; conversations ignite.

Questions drive dialogue: શું? (what?), ક્યાં? (where?), ક્યારે? (when?), શા માટે? (why?), કેવી રીતે? (how?). તમે ક્યાં રહો છો? (where do you live?) — one question word plus any verb equals conversation.

Politeness oils everything: કૃપા કરીને (please) and માફ કરજો (sorry/excuse me). માફ કરજો, ધીમે બોલો (sorry, speak slowly) — apologies plus requests, the learner's armour.

Repair keeps talk alive: મને સમજાયું નહીં (I didn't understand), ફરી કહો (please say again), આનો અર્થ શું? (what does this mean?). Broken Gujarati beats silent perfection — stumble forward daily.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on મને. મને (mane — to-me) is Gujarati's magic word — મને જોઈએ છે (I want), મને સમજાયું (I understood), મને ખબર છે (I know). Experiences flow TOWARD you in Gujarati; master મને and fifty sentences unlock.

Practice: recall, then use

Use this quick recall drill for Real Gujarati conversation. Hide the script and romanisation columns; say the Gujarati form for each cue before revealing its row, then check spelling and sound. The phrase-table speaker buttons provide a model when a matching browser voice is available.

  1. I
  2. What is your name?
  3. Nice! / great!

Transfer practice. Turn two rows into a brief exchange: open, answer, and add one follow-up question of your own. Keep the turns short enough to say without reading. In a real conversation, listen for the other person’s pace and leave room for a reply rather than delivering a memorised speech. Start from હું (hun) and decide whether it fits the situation before using it elsewhere.

Context check. For Real Gujarati conversation, “Nice! / great!” belongs to a particular setting. A common mistake is to treat an English gloss as universal; read the language-specific note above and ask a fluent speaker when local usage differs.

Questions learners ask

How do I ask someone's name?

તમારું નામ શું? (what's your name?) — answer મારું નામ રાહુલ છે (my name is Rahul). Names first, friendship follows.

Yes and no in Gujarati?

હા (yes), ના (no) — short, clear, universal. જી adds respect; નહીં denies verbs. Yes-no fluency first.

When I don't understand?

મને સમજાયું નહીં (I didn't understand) and ધીમે બોલો (please speak slowly). Honesty tunes every speaker to your level.
