Gujarati vs Hindi

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Gujarati and Hindi share some Indo-Aryan vocabulary, but their scripts and sound patterns are not interchangeable; compare real pairs before guessing.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Gujarati spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

The cousins grew side by side: ગુજરાતી bloomed in Gujarat, हिंदी spread across the north. Same Indo-Aryan mother, sister scripts — Gujarati dropped the headline bar while Hindi kept it. History in every sentence.

Shared words delight learners: ઘર/घर (house), પાણી/पानी (water), નામ/नाम (name), મિત્ર/मित्र (friend) — identical or near. Core vocabulary overlaps enormously; learn one list, own two languages' hearts.

Split sounds mark the border: Gujarati ળ (lla) against Hindi ल, મને (mane — to-me) against मुझे, હા (haa — yes) against हाँ. Pronouns and yes-no drifted most — precisely the words learners say most. Drill the pairs; cross the border daily.

Greetings show both faces: નમસ્તે serves both while જય શ્રી કૃષ્ણ stands Gujarati-warm — yet each understands the other's instantly. Cousins recognise cousins; learn either with love and both reward you.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on ઘર = घर. Gujarati ઘર (house) and Hindi घर are the SAME word in sister scripts — thousands work this way. Learn Gujarati's core well and Hindi signboards start reading themselves.

Questions learners ask

Can Gujaratis understand Hindi?

Largely yes for simple speech — ઘર, પાણી, નામ, ના/नहीं are near-identical. Speed and slang divide them; patience reunites them.

Why do they sound similar yet different?

Gujarati kept the ળ and three genders; Hindi simplified to two and lost the flap. Cousins with different habits — precisely the words learners say most.

Which should I learn first?

Gujarati first for Gujarat, Hindi structure second for the nation. Each language halves the other's difficulty — learn one, get the second at a discount.
