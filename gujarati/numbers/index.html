Counting in Gujarati

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

એક બે ત્રણ to સો — count, pay and bargain in Gujarati.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Gujarati spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

One to ten sings: એક બે ત્રણ ચાર પાંચ છ સાત આઠ નવ દસ — chant it walking, cooking, waiting. Gujarati numbers love rhythm; your tongue learns faster than your brain. Ten numbers, total freedom in every shop.

Teens climb steadily: અગિયાર બાર તેર ચૌદ પંદર સોળ સત્તર અઢાર ઓગણીસ વીસ — eleven to twenty. ઓગણીસ (19) and વીસ (20) open the tens; climb daily and teens become trivial.

Tens multiply power: વીસ ત્રીસ ચાળીસ પચાસ સાઠ સિત્તેર એંસી નેવું સો — 20 to 100. Combine freely: એકવીસ (21), પચ્ચીસ (25), સત્તાવીસ (27) — the tongue-twisters that prove fluency.

Hundreds rule markets: સો (100), બસો (200), હજાર (1000), લાખ (100,000). દોઢસો (150) and અઢીસો (250) charm shopkeepers — halves and quarters in pure Gujarati. Money talks; count it in Gujarati.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on કેટલા? કેટલા? (ketlaa — how much / how many?) is Gujarat's most useful question — કેટલા રૂપિયા? (how many rupees?), કેટલા વાગ્યા? (what time is it?). One word, every transaction. Ask કેટલા fearlessly; numbers come to you.

Transfer practice. Choose a practical setting—a price, a quantity, an age, or an appointment time—and use the number in a complete request. Say the number once, pause, and repeat it at a natural pace. Check the script form as well as the romanisation; a familiar Arabic numeral is not a substitute for recognising the local form. Start from એક (ek) and decide whether it fits the situation before using it elsewhere.

Context check. For Counting in Gujarati, “Thousand” belongs to a particular setting. A common mistake is to treat an English gloss as universal; read the language-specific note above and ask a fluent speaker when local usage differs.

Questions learners ask

How do I start counting?

એક બે ત્રણ ચાર પાંચ છ સાત આઠ નવ દસ — chant to ten, then અગિયાર બાર તેર ચૌદ પંદર to twenty. Rhythm first, precision follows.

How do tens work?

વીસ (20), ત્રીસ (30), ચાળીસ (40), પચાસ (50) — then combine: એકવીસ (21), બત્રીસ (32). Tens plus units, endlessly.

Big numbers?

સો (100), હજાર (1000), લાખ (100,000). કેટલા? (how many?) asks; numbers answer. Markets run on these three.
