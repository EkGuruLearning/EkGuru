Gujarati grammar, plainly explained

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Gujarati grammar works with three grammatical genders, postpositions and verb-final clauses; follow each pattern through a Gujarati example.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Gujarati spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Three genders shape everything: masculine (છોકરો — boy), feminine (છોકરી — girl), neuter (બાળક — child, ઘર — house). Adjectives triple — સારો/સારી/સારું — and past verbs triple too. Gender is not decoration; it is Gujarati's skeleton.

Pronouns simplify persons: હું (I), તું (you-informal), તમે (you-formal), તે/તેઓ (he-she/they), અમે (we). અમે vs આપણે (exclusive vs inclusive we) — Gujarati distinguishes; English doesn't. Choose your we wisely.

Postpositions replace prepositions: -ને (to), -એ (by/doer), -સાથે (with-person), -માં (in). ઘરે જા (go home), ચમચીથી ખા (eat with a spoon). Suffixes after nouns — think backwards from English.

Verbs anchor sentences last: હું ભાત ખાઉં છું (I rice eat), તે શાળાએ જાય છે (she to-school goes). Present (ખાઉં છું), past (ખાધું), future (ખાઈશ) — tense on verbs, time words framing. Verb-final: the one rule that never breaks.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on એ. એ (e) marks the doer in past tense — રામે કર્યું (Ram did), મેં ખાધું (I ate). Ergative, elegant, everywhere in stories. Tiny suffix, enormous workload.

Questions learners ask

Three genders — really?

Masculine, feminine, neuter — nouns, adjectives AND verbs agree. સારો/સારી/સારું (good), જાઉં છું forms shift by gender in past tense. Triples everywhere; learn them as sets.

What are postpositions?

ને marks direction (અમદાવાદને — to Ahmedabad, rare), એ marks doers (રામે કર્યું — Ram did), સાથે means with. Postpositions follow nouns — learn each with ten examples.

What is Gujarati word order?

Subject-Object-Verb: હું ભાત ખાઉં છું (I rice eat). Verb last, always. English order is the classic beginner error.
