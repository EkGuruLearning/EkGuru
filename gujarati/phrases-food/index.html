Food phrases in Gujarati

ઢોકળા to શીખંડ — order, eat and praise food in Gujarati.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Gujarati spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Meals structure the day: નાસ્તો (breakfast), જમવાનું (lunch/dinner), ચા (tea). ચા પીશું? (shall we have tea?) — Gujarat runs on chai and conversation. Accept every cup; refuse nothing twice.

Ordering is easy bravery: મને એક ચા જોઈએ છે (I want one tea), બિલ આપો (please give the bill). એક પ્લેટ ઢોકળા આપો (give one plate of dhokla) — order Gujarat's soul in Gujarati. Vendors reward attempts with extra chutney.

Staples fill the thali: ભાત (rice), રોટલી (flatbread), દાળ (lentils), શાક (vegetables). શાક શું છે? (what's the vegetable dish?) — ask daily, eat variously. Thali eating teaches vocabulary with every bite.

Tastes tune the tongue: તીખું (spicy), ગળ્યું (sweet) — શીખંડ (sweet yogurt dessert) crowns feasts. ઓછું તીખું કરજો (make it less spicy) — then ખૂબ સરસ! (very nice!). Praise the cook; second helpings follow.

A note on જમવાનું. જમવાનું (jamvaanun — meal/food) is sacred in Gujarat — જમ્યા? (have you eaten?) is love disguised as a question. Answer હા, જમ્યો (yes, done) and watch faces glow. Food is affection, served hot.

Questions learners ask

How do I talk about meals?

જમવાનું (meal), નાસ્તો (breakfast/snacks), ચા (tea). મને ભૂખ લાગી છે (I'm hungry) opens every food conversation.

How do I order food?

આ શું છે? (what is this?) and મને ઢોકળા જોઈએ છે (I want dhokla). Point, ask, order — menus fear the curious.

How do I describe tastes?

તીખું (spicy), ગળ્યું (sweet), ખારું (salty), ખાટું (sour). ઓછું તીખું (less spicy) saves sensitive tongues.
