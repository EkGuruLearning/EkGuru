Flashcards by topic in Gujarati

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Small Gujarati topic decks and spaced retrieval make daily review easier to sustain than a single long session.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Gujarati spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Cards carry memory: કાર્ડ (card) — one word, one side; meaning, the other. યાદશક્તિ (memory) strengthens with every recall; ભૂલવું (forgetting) is just unreviewed memory. Build decks per topic — greetings, food, verbs — and review each separately.

Spacing schedules success: અંતર (interval) — review new cards tomorrow, then in three days, seven, thirty. ફરી ફરી (again and again), but wider each time. Cramming fills hours; spacing fills years.

Rhythms sustain streaks: રોજ (daily) quick decks, અઠવાડિયે (weekly) full reviews, મહિને (monthly) championships. Ten daily minutes beat Sunday marathons; streaks turn study into ટેવ (habit).

Scores gamify growth: ગુણ (marks), સ્પર્ધા (contest), સિદ્ધિ (achievement) — even નિષ્ફળતા (defeat) teaches. Quiz yourself, beat yesterday's score, celebrate loudly. Flashcards are games that pay in fluency.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on પુનરાવર્તન. પુનરાવર્તન (punraavartan — revision, repeating) is Gujarati's own word for spaced review — recalling again, and again, at widening intervals. The language itself teaches the method: revisit, widen, remember.

Questions learners ask

How often should I review?

ફરી ફરી (again and again) — but spaced: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 30 days. અંતર (spacing) beats cramming; યાદશક્તિ (memory) loves intervals.

How many cards per topic?

Five to ten per topic — quality over quantity. Master one કાર્ડ deck fully before opening the next; half-known decks rot.

Daily or weekly review?

રોજ (daily) short sessions — ten minutes, every day, no exceptions. Streaks compound; ભૂલવું (forgetting) loses to routine.
