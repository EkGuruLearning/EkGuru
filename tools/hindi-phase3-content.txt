=== BODY 1 ===
  <h1>100 Most Common Hindi Words — With Pronunciation</h1>
  <p class="lede">If you learn one thing first, learn this. Every word below is high-frequency: you will hear it in the first hour of any real Hindi conversation. They are grouped by meaning, and each one is written three ways — English, Devanagari and a romanisation you can read aloud.</p>
  <p>Hindi is written in Devanagari, and it is unusually kind about pronunciation: the letters say what they say. Once you know that <b>आ</b> is a long "aa", that <b>न</b> is "n" and that a dot under a letter (ड़, ढ़) means a rolled "r" sound, you can read every word on this page out loud. The romanisation column is a starting aid, not a crutch — the sooner you read the Devanagari column directly, the faster your accent settles.</p>
  <div class="note"><b>How to use this page.</b> Do not swallow all 100 words in one sitting. Take one group a day: read the Devanagari aloud, cover the romanisation, and say each word. Then test yourself — the <a href="/learn/hindi/vocabulary/">vocabulary page</a> and the <a href="/learn/hindi/practice/quiz/">Hindi quiz</a> both draw on this same core.</div>

  <h1>How to Practise Speaking Hindi Alone — A Realistic Plan</h1>
  <p class="lede">You can get genuinely far in Hindi without a teacher and without a partner. Not all the way — nobody learns to handle real conversation alone — but far enough that your first real conversation will not be your first spoken Hindi sentence. Here is the routine, in the order it should be built.</p>
  <h2>1. Shadow before you speak</h2>
  <p>Shadowing means copying speech in real time. Take one line you already know — <b>नमस्ते, आप कैसे हैं?</b> (namaste, aap kaise hain? — hello, how are you?) — and say it while you read it, out loud, five times. Then say it with the page closed. The point is not comprehension; it is making your mouth move in an unfamiliar way until it stops being unfamiliar.</p>
  <p>Start with phrases you cannot get wrong: <b>नमस्ते</b>, <b>धन्यवाद</b>, <b>माफ़ कीजिए</b>, <b>यह कितने का है?</b> (yah kitne ka hai? — how much is this?). Five of these, said properly, are worth more than fifty words read silently.</p>
  <h2>2. Talk to yourself on a schedule</h2>
  <p>Narrate your day in Hindi for two minutes, twice a day. Keep it dull on purpose: what you are doing, what you want, what is next. Dull sentences repeat, and repeated sentences are the ones that stick.</p>
  <table class="tbl tri"><thead><tr><th>English</th><th>Hindi</th><th>Say it</th></tr></thead><tbody>
  <tr><td>I am eating</td><td><b>मैं खा रहा हूँ</b></td><td>main kha raha hoon</td></tr>
  <tr><td>I am going home</td><td><b>मैं घर जा रहा हूँ</b></td><td>main ghar ja raha hoon</td></tr>
  <tr><td>I want tea</td><td><b>मुझे चाय चाहिए</b></td><td>mujhe chai chahiye</td></tr>
  <tr><td>I do not understand</td><td><b>मुझे समझ नहीं आया</b></td><td>mujhe samajh nahin aaya</td></tr>
  <tr><td>Please speak slowly</td><td><b>धीरे बोलिए</b></td><td>dheere boliye</td></tr>
  <tr><td>I am learning Hindi</td><td><b>मैं हिंदी सीख रहा हूँ</b></td><td>main Hindi seekh raha hoon</td></tr>
  </tbody></table>
  <p>If you are a woman speaking, several of these change ending: <b>खा रही हूँ</b>, <b>जा रही हूँ</b>, <b>सीख रही हूँ</b>. Say your own version out loud rather than the other one — practicing the wrong ending for your own speech is a habit that takes months to unlearn.</p>
  <h2>3. Record yourself once a week</h2>
  <ol>
    <li>Read ten words from the <a href="/learn/hindi-alphabet-for-beginners/">alphabet guide</a> aloud and record them.</li>
    <li>Play it back and compare with the sounds described on that page. Listen for the sounds, not the accent.</li>
    <li>Record the same ten words next week. The improvement is the motivation.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>Recording is uncomfortable for about three weeks and then becomes the most useful habit on this list, because it is the only way to hear what other people actually hear. Pay particular attention to the aspirated consonants — <b>क/ख</b>, <b>ग/घ</b>, <b>त/थ</b>, <b>द/ध</b>, <b>प/फ</b>, <b>ब/भ</b>. English speakers flatten them, and it is the difference people notice most.</p>
  <div class="note"><b>A useful trick.</b> Exaggerate the breath on the aspirated letters on purpose while practising — say <b>खाना</b> with far too much air. You will feel silly, and you will hear the difference. When you drop back to normal speed, your mouth will be in the right place.</div>
  <h2>4. Drill the sounds Hindi has and English does not</h2>
  <p>Hindi has three sounds English does not, and they are the ones worth deliberate practice:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><b>ड़ / ढ़</b> — a rolled "r" made with the tongue tip, as in <b>बड़ा</b> (bada, big) and <b>पढ़ना</b> (padhna, to read).</li>
    <li><b>ट / ड / ण</b> — retroflex letters, made with the tongue curled back, as in <b>टमाटर</b> (tamaatar, tomato).</li>
    <li><b>Nasal vowels</b> — <b>माँ</b> (maan, mother) has a nasal quality English does not mark at all. Ignore it and "mother" becomes "moon".</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Take one of these a day and build a sentence around it. The <a href="/learn/hindi-alphabet-for-beginners/">alphabet guide</a> has every letter with the position of the tongue described.</p>
  <h2>5. Feed the habit with the words you already have</h2>
  <p>Speaking practice fails when the words are not there yet. Spend ten minutes on the <a href="/learn/hindi-100-most-common-words/">100 most common words</a> every day, or run through the <a href="/learn/hindi/vocabulary/">vocabulary page</a>, so that your speaking time is spent building sentences rather than hunting for vocabulary.</p>
  <p>A principle worth trusting: it is better to say a simple true sentence with words you own than a complicated false one with words you have just met.</p>
  <h2>6. Know when you are ready for a person</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>You can greet, thank and ask where something is without translating in your head.</li>
    <li>You can say what you did yesterday and what you will do tomorrow, even badly.</li>
    <li>You can ask someone to repeat themselves — <b>फिर से बोलिए</b> (phir se boliye) — and catch the first three words of the answer.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>That is the point to find a human: a tutor, a colleague, a shopkeeper, an online exchange. Before that, the person is mostly doing the work your own routine should be doing.</p>
  <h2>The honest limits of practising alone</h2>
  <p>No amount of shadowing teaches you to understand an unfamiliar voice at natural speed, and it cannot correct an error you cannot hear. A routine alone is a strong bridge and a bad destination. Our tutors teach Hindi one to one, and everything on this site stays free either way.</p>
  <h2>The daily twenty minutes</h2>
  <ol>
    <li><b>5 minutes:</b> read the alphabet page aloud — two letters, their sounds, and two words each.</li>
    <li><b>5 minutes:</b> ten words from the 100 most common words, said out loud and written down.</li>
    <li><b>5 minutes:</b> shadow one phrase, or narrate your day in three sentences.</li>
    <li><b>5 minutes:</b> write two sentences, including one negative sentence with <b>नहीं</b>.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>Keep it that small and it will survive a busy week, which is the only test that matters. A twenty-minute habit kept for a year beats a three-hour session that happens twice.</p>
