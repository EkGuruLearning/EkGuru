A useful first vocabulary list is not a promise that these are the statistically exact hundred most frequent words in every country. It is a practical set of words that lets a beginner greet someone, ask a question, name daily things and build a short sentence. The “say it” column is an English-friendly cue, not formal phonetic transcription.

Common mistakes and a useful practice loop

Do not learn a word without its stress mark, article or phrase. el café and la casa are easier to use than bare nouns. Do not treat every pronunciation cue as universal; the s/θ difference in gracias is one visible regional choice. Do not memorize por and para as a single English “for”; their uses diverge and deserve separate examples.

For practice, cover the English column and recall ten Spanish words. Then cover the Spanish column and retrieve them from English. Choose three and put each into a true sentence: Hoy necesito agua. (“Today I need water.”), Mi amiga vive aquí. (“My friend lives here.”), Quiero más tiempo. (“I want more time.”) Say each sentence aloud twice and change one detail. Review the same ten tomorrow, then add ten new words only when the first set is reasonably secure.

One hundred words do not equal fluency. They are a foothold: a way to ask, answer, describe and notice patterns. Use them with real listening and the sentence-building guide, and keep a note of the words you actually needed this week. Your own conversations will tell you what should come next.
