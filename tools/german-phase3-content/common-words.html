Pronunciation note: This is a curated Standard German beginner list, not a ranked corpus-frequency chart. German uses the Latin alphabet, so transliteration is unnecessary. The English-friendly pronunciation tips are learner aids, not IPA; check audio, especially for umlauts, long vowels, and regional forms.

Learn words as complete, useful pieces

A word list is most useful when you can recall its meaning and put it into a sentence. Read each German item, say it aloud, and then cover the English gloss. For a noun, learn the article with it: das Buch is more useful than Buch alone because the article records grammatical gender. For a verb, notice that the table gives the infinitive; in a real sentence its ending changes with the subject.

Pronunciation is not a second spelling. German already uses a Latin alphabet, and an English approximation can only remind you of a sound. The umlauts ä, ö, and ü are real spelling distinctions, ß and ss follow regional spelling conventions, and written letter patterns do not replace listening. Use a dictionary with audio or a course model when you need to learn a word you will say often.

Use the list in natural sentences

Try a short present-tense sentence: Ich lerne Deutsch. (“I am learning German.”) The verb lernen changes to match ich. For a polite request, Können Sie mir bitte helfen? means “Can you please help me?” Here, the conjugated verb comes first because it is a yes-or-no question. In a statement, it normally sits second: Heute lerne ich Deutsch. (“Today I am learning German.”)

Use nouns with articles in a phrase: das Wasser (the water), der Bahnhof (the station), die Stadt (the city). A word’s article can change in a sentence because German has grammatical cases, so the table gives basic forms, not every form a noun can take. When a preposition appears, check whether it requires a particular case instead of copying the English word order.

Common mistakes with beginner vocabulary

Do not memorise only the noun and postpone its article; that turns a short vocabulary task into a larger grammar repair later. Do not learn Sie as interchangeable with lowercase sie: capitalization and the verb form distinguish formal “you” from “she” or “they.” Also learn a verb as an infinitive plus a sentence, because its ending changes: ich lerne but du lernst.

Another common mistake is reading an English-friendly cue as if it were an exact pronunciation key. German spelling patterns are useful, but a cue cannot show all vowel quality, stress, or regional variation. Listen to the word in a sentence, note the recording’s variety, and keep the original spelling visible. If an item comes from a regional phrase list, label the region rather than adding it to a Standard German sentence without context.

Practice: turn ten entries into a mini-dialogue

Choose ten items from the list: two pronouns, two nouns with articles, two verbs, two question words, and two polite expressions. Write a four-line dialogue that says hello, asks where the station is, gives a simple answer, and thanks the other person. Read the German aloud, then cover the English meanings and recreate the conversation from memory. Verify the noun articles and verb endings before you save the final version.
