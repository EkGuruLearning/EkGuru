The seven days of the week

Every Punjabi day ends in ਵਾਰ (-vār), which just means "day". The rest of the name is a planet or a god, so once you know the opening sound you can almost always guess the day.

Punjabi has one word for two directions of time. ਕੱਲ੍ਹ (kall) means both "tomorrow" and "yesterday", and ਪਰਸੋਂ (parson) means both "the day after tomorrow" and "the day before yesterday". Context and the verb tell you which — this is how speakers really talk, not a mistake in the table.

The traditional desi months

Alongside the calendar months, Punjab keeps its own solar months — the traditional desi months, also used in the Sikh Nanakshahi calendar. These are the names you will hear around festivals and farming. The year begins with ਚੇਤ (Chet) in mid-March.

Both sets are real and answer different questions. If someone in Punjab says a festival is in ਸਾਵਣ (Sawan), that is a traditional month; if they say it is in ਜੁਲਾਈ (July), that is the calendar month.

Questions people ask

How do you say the days of the week in Punjabi?

Every day ends in ਵਾਰ. Sunday ਐਤਵਾਰ, Monday ਸੋਮਵਾਰ, Tuesday ਮੰਗਲਵਾਰ, Wednesday ਬੁੱਧਵਾਰ, Thursday ਵੀਰਵਾਰ, Friday ਸ਼ੁੱਕਰਵਾਰ, Saturday ਸ਼ਨਿੱਚਰਵਾਰ.

Why does Punjabi have two sets of month names?

Everyday and official life uses the Gregorian months written in Gurmukhi — ਜਨਵਰੀ, ਫ਼ਰਵਰੀ, ਮਾਰਚ and the rest. The traditional desi months — ਚੇਤ, ਵੈਸਾਖ, ਜੇਠ — are used for festivals, farming seasons and the Nanakshahi calendar. Both are correct; they are used in different situations.

What does ਕੱਲ੍ਹ (kall) mean?

It means both "tomorrow" and "yesterday". Context and the verb tense tell you which — ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਆਵਾਂਗਾ is "I will come tomorrow", ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਆਇਆ ਸੀ is "I came yesterday". The same single word covers both directions of time.

How do you say today, tomorrow and yesterday?

Today is ਅੱਜ (ajj), tomorrow and yesterday are both ਕੱਲ੍ਹ (kall), the day after and the day before are both ਪਰਸੋਂ (parson). The four parts of the day are morning ਸਵੇਰ, afternoon ਦੁਪਹਿਰ, evening ਸ਼ਾਮ and night ਰਾਤ.
