Days, Months and Telling the Time in Hindi

A free guide from EkGuru · updated 2026-09-11

A note on the Hindi spellings

These guides are written and typed by hand, and Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong — a missing matra, the wrong nukta, a halant in the wrong place. If a word here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page, and please tell us so it gets fixed. The romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard — Hindi has no single agreed romanisation, so you will see shukriya and shukriyaa both used.

Days, months and clock times come up in almost every conversation, and Hindi handles the clock in a way English does not. The half-hour words in particular catch out every learner, so they are worth learning properly.

The days of the week

EnglishHindiSay itNamed after

Every one ends in -वार, meaning day, and each is named for a planet — exactly as the English names are, through Norse and Latin. Monday is the Moon's day in both languages. Learn the seven planet names and the days come free.

Yesterday, today, tomorrow — and the trap

आज aaj today · कल kal · परसों parson

Here is the oddity: कल means both yesterday and tomorrow. The same word. Which one is meant comes entirely from the verb tense:

  • kal main gaya tha — yesterday I went
  • kal main jaunga — tomorrow I will go

And परसों means both "the day before yesterday" and "the day after tomorrow", by the same logic. Hindi treats time as distance from today, not direction.

The months

In everyday life the English months are used almost universally, written in Devanagari: जनवरी, फ़रवरी, मार्च, अप्रैल, मई, जून, जुलाई, अगस्त, सितंबर, अक्टूबर, नवंबर, दिसंबर.

The traditional Hindu calendar months — चैत्र, वैशाख, ज्येष्ठ and so on — appear for festivals and religious dates. You will hear them around Diwali and Holi. For everyday use, the English names are correct and normal.

Telling the time

क्या समय हुआ है? kya samay hua hai? — what time is it? In speech most people say time kya hua hai?, using the English word.

On the hour: एक बजे ek baje (one o'clock), दो बजे do baje, and so on. Baje literally means "struck", as in a bell.

The half-hour words

This is the part worth slowing down for. Hindi has dedicated words for the half hour, and they are used constantly:

TimeHindiLiterally
1:30डेढ़ बजे dedh bajeone and a half
2:30ढाई बजे dhai bajetwo and a half
3:30साढ़े तीन saadhe teenthree and a half
4:30साढ़े चार saadhe chaarfour and a half

Note that 1:30 and 2:30 have their own irregular words — dedh and dhai — and from 3:30 onwards you use saadhe plus the number. This irregularity is not optional; saying saadhe do for 2:30 marks you out immediately.

Quarters

सवा sawa = quarter past. Sawa teen is 3:15.
पौने paune = quarter to. Paune chaar is 3:45.

Both are used with the coming hour for paune, so paune chaar is a quarter to four, not a quarter past.

Parts of the day

सुबह subah morning · दोपहर dopahar afternoon · शाम shaam evening · रात raat night

Hindi has no am/pm. You attach the part of the day instead: सुबह के आठ बजे — eight in the morning.

The numbers underneath all of this are covered in our guide to Hindi numbers, which explains why they are irregular and which ones you actually need.

Quick check

Which half-hour words confuse every learner?

Dhai and saadhe — ढाई (dhai, 2½) and साढ़े (saadhe, half past) — the half-hour words that confuse every learner.

Which day is रविवार (ravivaar)?

Sunday — रविवार (ravivaar) is Sunday — named after Ravi, the sun.

Which day is सोमवार (somvaar)?

Monday — सोमवार (somvaar) is Monday — from som, the moon.
