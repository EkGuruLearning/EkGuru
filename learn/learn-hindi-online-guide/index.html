How to Actually Learn Hindi Online in 2026 — An Honest Guide

A free guide from EkGuru · updated 2026-09-11

A note on the Hindi spellings

These guides are written and typed by hand, and Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong — a missing matra, the wrong nukta, a halant in the wrong place. If a word here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page, and please tell us so it gets fixed. The romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard — Hindi has no single agreed romanisation, so you will see shukriya and shukriyaa both used.

Hindi is spoken by well over half a billion people. It is also one of the easier major languages for an English speaker to speak, and one of the harder ones to find good teaching for. Here is a straight comparison of the options, including where our own approach falls short.

Apps — Duolingo, Memrise and the rest

Good for: the first two weeks. Vocabulary drilling. Building the daily habit. They are free, and free matters.

Where they fail: pronunciation. An app cannot hear you. Hindi has pairs of sounds — त and ट, द and ड — that English does not distinguish at all, and an app will happily let you say the wrong one for a year without ever mentioning it. Learners who use only apps typically end up with a decent vocabulary and an accent nobody can follow.

They are also poor at the gender system. In Hindi the verb changes depending on whether the speaker is male or female: a man says main karta hoon, a woman says main karti hoon. Apps teach this as a rule to memorise. It needs to become a reflex, and reflexes come from speaking.

YouTube

Good for: listening practice, and it is genuinely excellent for this. Free, endless, and real Hindi at natural speed.

Where it fails: no structure and no feedback. You will watch thirty videos, feel productive, and still not be able to hold a conversation. Use it alongside something structured, not instead of it.

Textbooks

Good for: grammar, which is where most self-taught learners have gaps. Teach Yourself Hindi and Rupert Snell's Complete Hindi are both solid.

Where they fail: the Hindi in textbooks is more formal than the Hindi anyone speaks. You will learn dhanyavaad for thank you, then discover everyone says shukriya, and that between friends nobody says either.

A tutor

Good for: everything the others cannot do. Correcting your pronunciation in the moment. Answering "why is it like that?". Adapting when you are stuck. Making you actually speak, which is uncomfortable and is exactly why it works.

Where it fails: cost, and it is not small. A tutor is typically $6–$6 an hour. It also requires scheduling, which apps do not.

Being straight with you: you do not need a tutor for every hour of study. The most efficient approach most learners land on is one or two tutor sessions a week for speaking and correction, with free resources filling the rest. That is a fraction of the cost of daily lessons and works better than either extreme.

How long does it really take?

The US Foreign Service Institute classes Hindi as a Category III language: roughly 1,100 class hours to professional working proficiency for an English speaker. That is their full-time estimate for diplomats. For ordinary goals:

GoalRealistic time
Greetings, politeness, ordering food2–4 weeks
Reading Devanagari aloud4–8 weeks
Simple conversation with a patient speaker4–6 months
Comfortable everyday conversation1–2 years
Following films without subtitles2–3 years

Anyone promising fluency in three months is not being honest with you.

What actually makes the difference

  1. Twenty minutes daily beats three hours on Sunday. This is not motivational filler; it is how memory consolidation works.
  2. Speak from week one, badly. Learners who wait until they feel ready never feel ready.
  3. Learn the script early. Romanised Hindi feels easier and quietly caps your progress, because it hides which vowel is which.
  4. Have a real reason. Family, travel, a film you love, someone specific you want to talk to. Learners with a concrete reason finish; learners who want to "learn a language" mostly do not.

Where to start today, for free

Learn five Devanagari letters — अ आ क ख ग — and write your own name. It takes fifteen minutes and it is the moment the script stops being decoration and starts being writing. Our alphabet guide walks through all of them.
