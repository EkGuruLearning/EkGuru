Hindi Word Order — Why the Verb Goes Last

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

A free guide from EkGuru · updated 2026-09-11

A note on the Hindi spellings

These guides are written and typed by hand, and Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong — a missing matra, the wrong nukta, a halant in the wrong place. If a word here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page, and please tell us so it gets fixed. The romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard — Hindi has no single agreed romanisation, so you will see shukriya and shukriyaa both used.

English is Subject–Verb–Object: I eat bread.
Hindi is Subject–Object–Verb: I bread eat.

That single difference makes early Hindi feel like reading a sentence backwards. It stops after a few weeks, and there is a trick that speeds it up.

The basic pattern

EnglishHindiLiterally
I eat breadमैं रोटी खाता हूँI bread eat am
She reads a bookवह किताब पढ़ती हैshe book reads is
We live in Delhiहम दिल्ली में रहते हैंwe Delhi in live are

Notice the last one: दिल्ली में — "Delhi in", not "in Delhi". Hindi does not have prepositions, it has postpositions, which come after the noun.

The common postpositions

में mein in · पर par on · से se from/with · को ko to · का/की/के ka/ki/ke of · तक tak until · के लिए ke liye for

Every one of them goes after the word it applies to. मेज़ पर (table on), दिल्ली से (Delhi from), मेरे लिए (me for).

The trick that makes it click

Stop translating English word by word. Instead, ask yourself three questions in this order and say the answers:

  1. Who? → मैं
  2. What / where? → रोटी, दिल्ली में
  3. Doing what? → खाता हूँ

Answer in that order and the word order comes out right without thinking about it. Learners who do this reach natural sentences much faster than those who translate and then rearrange.

Questions

For a yes/no question, add क्या at the start and change nothing else:

आप हिंदी बोलते हैं। — You speak Hindi.
क्या आप हिंदी बोलते हैं? — Do you speak Hindi?

For other questions, the question word goes just before the verb, not at the start as in English:

  • आप कहाँ रहते हैं? — Where do you live?
  • यह क्या है? — What is this?
  • आप कब आएँगे? — When will you come?
  • यह कितने का है? — How much is this?

Negatives

नहीं goes immediately before the verb:

मैं हिंदी बोलता हूँ → मैं हिंदी नहीं बोलता
Note that hoon is usually dropped in the negative. Saying nahin bolta hoon is not wrong, but nahin bolta is what people actually say.

Word order is flexible, but not free

Hindi allows you to move things for emphasis, which English does with tone:

मैं कल दिल्ली जाऊँगा — I will go to Delhi tomorrow
कल मैं दिल्ली जाऊँगा — Tomorrow I will go to Delhi

The one thing that does not move is the verb. It stays at the end. Everything else can shuffle.

क्रिया सबसे आख़िर मेंThe verb goes last. Always.

What to practise first

Build ten sentences a day using this frame: subject + place/object + verb. Say them aloud. Within two weeks the ordering stops being a translation exercise and starts being how you think in Hindi.

The verb endings in these examples change with gender — that is covered in our guide to Hindi gender.

Quick check

Where does the verb go in a Hindi sentence?

At the end — At the end — Hindi is subject-object-verb, so the action is the last thing you hear.

What does Hindi use instead of English prepositions like “in” and “on”?

Postpositions — Postpositions — small words that go AFTER the noun, not before it.

Why do Hindi sentences feel “backwards” at first?

Because the verb comes last — Because the verb comes last, so you wait for the whole sentence before you know the action.
