Bengali grammar

Bengali word order, verbs, pronouns and the mistakes everyone makes — explained, not listed.

1. The verb goes last

Bengali is subject–object–verb: আমি ভাত খাই (ami bhat khai) — मैं चावल खाता हूँ — “I eat rice (word-for-word: I · rice · eat)”. Learn this once and every sentence parses.

2. No grammatical gender

Unlike Hindi, adjectives never change: the word for “good” stays the same for boys, girls and books. One less thing to memorise.

4. Present tense — one full verb

“To eat” (খাওয়া) in the present. Notice আপনি/তুমি/তুই: formal, familiar and intimate forms differ — the verb tells you the relationship.

6. Negation is one word

Put না (na) after a present-tense verb: আমি জানি না (ami jani na) — I don't know. Past tense tucks it inside: খাইনি (khaini) — didn't eat.

The sentence ladder

Do not learn grammar as a list of rules. Learn it as a ladder with seven rungs, where every rung is something you can say out loud today.

  1. Say what you are. Simple I-sentences with no verb change.
  2. Say what you want. The same pattern with a noun from the common words list.
  3. Say what you do. The present-tense table above, first person only.
  4. Say what someone else does. The same table, third person — this is where the endings move.
  5. Say what happened. Past tense, five sentences about yesterday.
  6. Say what will happen. Future tense, five sentences about tomorrow.
  7. Say what does not happen. Negative forms of five sentences you have already built.

Small words, big sentences

Two groups of small words turn correct sentences into natural ones: the little words that mean in, at or to, and the words used when counting things.

The grammar rule that matters most. Notice আপনি/তুমি/তুই: formal, familiar and intimate forms differ — the verb tells you the relationship. Learn the pattern, then change one thing at a time — verb, then person, then tense. Learners who change everything at once produce sentences they cannot debug.
