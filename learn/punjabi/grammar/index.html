Punjabi grammar

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Punjabi word order, verbs, pronouns and the mistakes everyone makes — explained, not listed.

1. The verb goes last

Punjabi is subject–object–verb: ਮੈਂ ਰੋਟੀ ਖਾਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ। (Main rotī khāndā hān.) — मैं रोटी खाता हूँ। — “I eat bread. (word order: I – bread – eat)”. Learn this once and every sentence parses.

2. Two genders, everywhere

Every noun is masculine or feminine, and verbs and adjectives agree with it. ਖਾਂਦਾ (khāndā) is a man eating; ਖਾਂਦੀ (khāndī) is a woman eating. ਨੀਲਾ ਘਰ (blue house, m) but ਨੀਲੀ ਕਮੀਜ਼ (blue shirt, f). Citation forms in this course are masculine — the tables always show both.

4. Present tense — one full verb

“To eat” (ਖਾਣਾ) in the present. The present is stem + -ਦਾ (m) / -ਦੀ (f) + helper: ਹਾਂ (I), ਹੈਂ (tūn), ਹੈ (is), ਹੋ (tusīn — always plural!), ਹਨ (they). So ਮੈਂ ਖਾਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ (I eat, m), ਤੁਸੀਂ ਖਾਂਦੇ ਹੋ (you eat, respectful), ਉਹ ਖਾਂਦੇ ਹਨ (they eat).

6. Negation with ਨਹੀਂ (nahīn)

ਨਹੀਂ (nahīn) goes before the verb: ਮੈਂ ਨਹੀਂ ਖਾਂਦਾ (I don't eat), ਉਹ ਨਹੀਂ ਆਇਆ (he didn't come). For commands use ਨਾ or ਮਤ: ਇੱਥੇ ਨਾ ਬੈਠੋ (don't sit here).

7. Postpositions (not prepositions)

inमेंਵਿੱਚਬਿੱਲੀ ਘਰ ਵਿੱਚ ਹੈ (Billī ghar vich hai) — The cat is in the house.

8. Counting words

Punjabi has no classifiers — put the number straight before the noun. But the noun itself goes plural: ਇੱਕ ਕਿਤਾਬ (one book), ਦੋ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ (two books), ਤਿੰਨ ਰੁਪਏ (three rupees).

PunjabiHindiNote
ਇੱਕ ਕਿਤਾਬ (ikk kitāb)एक किताबone book — number + noun, no extras
ਦੋ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ (do kitābān)दो किताबेंtwo books — the noun itself goes plural (-ਾਂ)
