Spanish Numbers 1 to 100 — Counting, Prices and Time

Written by: AI-assisted draft for owner review. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-05. Editorial policy and corrections.

Spanish numbers are built from a learnable core: memorize the first fifteen, notice how sixteen to twenty-nine compress into one word, then use tens plus y for most numbers from thirty-one upward. The forms in this guide are standard written Spanish. Prices, currencies and number formatting still depend on the country you are in.

The stress mark in dieciséis matters. Read the list aloud in small groups instead of drilling twenty items until they blur together. Once the first ten are comfortable, 11–15 form a compact sequence; 16–19 visibly echo diez and the next number.

From 31 to 99, connect the tens and units with y: treinta y uno (31), cuarenta y cinco (45), noventa y nueve (99). Say the tens first, pause briefly, then add the unit. The pattern makes 68 much less mysterious: sesenta y ocho.

Uno changes before a noun

Uno shortens to un before a masculine singular noun: un libro (one book). Before a feminine noun, use una: una mesa (one table). The same adjustment appears in larger numbers ending in one: veintiún libros, treinta y una mesas. The number agrees with the noun's grammatical gender; it is not the speaker's gender.

For exactly 100, use cien: cien personas (“one hundred people”). In numbers above 100, the form is ciento before the rest: ciento uno (101). You do not need numbers past 100 for this guide, but learning the change prevents a common overgeneralization.

Prices, dates and time in a real place

Ask ¿Cuánto cuesta? for one item and ¿Cuánto cuestan? for more than one. Repeat the amount, or ask ¿Me lo puede escribir? (“Can you write it down for me?”). Currency changes by country: do not assume a Spanish-language price is in euros. Written separators for thousands and decimals can also vary by country and publication, so confirm an amount before making a large payment.

Dates are often spoken with the day before the month: el cuatro de octubre (“the fourth of October”). Months are normally written in lowercase. For time, one o’clock uses singular Es la una; other hours use plural Son las dos, Son las cinco. Keep the schedule and local context in view—meal times and public-transport timetables differ by place.

Common number mistakes

Do not write veinte y uno for 21; the correct form is veintiuno. Do not forget y from 31 upward, or add it inside 21–29. Keep the written accents in 16, 22, 23 and 26. Remember the change from uno to un before a masculine noun. When speaking a price, slow down: the tens and units can sound like one stream to a learner.

Practice: count what is around you

  1. Count objects from one to ten, pointing to each one.
  2. Pick a random tens number and make three values: cuarenta y dos, cuarenta y siete, cuarenta y nueve.
  3. Say three prices aloud, then ask ¿Cuánto cuesta? and answer in a full phrase.
  4. Say today's date and one appointment time: Es la una or Son las tres.
  5. Cover the table and write the accents in dieciséis, veintidós, veintitrés, veintiséis from memory.

Repeat the patterns tomorrow with different objects and prices. If you need a regional form for a receipt, timetable or bank transfer, ask a local speaker to confirm it.
