Spanish for Travel — Essential Phrases for Real Situations

Written by: AI-assisted draft for owner review. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-05. Editorial policy and corrections.

Travel Spanish is most useful when a phrase helps you do something: ask where a platform is, explain a food need, check a price or get help. Memorize short sentence frames that you can adapt. The expressions below are broadly understandable, but local words and preferred levels of formality vary across Spanish-speaking places.

Start an interaction politely

A greeting buys you a moment and makes a request feel less abrupt. Try Buenos días, ¿me puede ayudar? (“Good morning, can you help me?”). In many regions, usted is a polite singular form; in others, tú is ordinary with most people. If a person uses a casual form with you, you can follow their lead. You do not need to guess someone's age or status to speak respectfully.

SpanishMeaningSay it, approximately
Hola, buenos días.Hello, good morning.OH-lah, BWEH-nos DEE-as
Por favor, ¿me puede ayudar?Please, can you help me?por fah-VOR, meh PWEH-deh ah-yoo-DAR
No entiendo. ¿Puede repetirlo?I do not understand. Can you repeat it?noh en-TYEN-doh, PWEH-deh reh-peh-TEER-loh
¿Puede hablar más despacio?Can you speak more slowly?PWEH-deh ah-BLAR mahs des-PAH-syoh
¿Cómo se dice esto en español?How do you say this in Spanish?KOH-moh seh DEE-seh ES-toh en es-pah-NYOL

At an airport, station or bus stop

Transport vocabulary changes by place: a bus, coach, subway and railway station do not always use the same local word. Add a map point or show your destination if you are unsure. You can ask the broad question first, then point: ¿Dónde está la estación? (“Where is the station?”). For tickets, have the destination written down; proper names are easier to show than to spell aloud.

SpanishMeaningSay it, approximately
¿Dónde está la estación?Where is the station?DON-deh es-TAH lah es-tah-SYON
¿A qué hora sale el tren?What time does the train leave?ah keh OH-rah SAH-leh el tren
Un billete / boleto a Madrid, por favor.One ticket to Madrid, please.oon bee-YEH-teh / boh-LEH-toh
¿Este autobús va al centro?Does this bus go to the center?ES-teh ow-toh-BOOS bah al SEN-troh
¿Dónde puedo tomar un taxi?Where can I get a taxi?DON-deh PWEH-doh toh-MAR oon TAK-see
Me bajo aquí.I get off here.meh BAH-hoh ah-KEE
¿Cuánto tarda?How long does it take?KWAN-toh TAR-dah

Regional words are worth learning after arrival. For example, billete and boleto can both mean ticket, but the local preference differs. You can make a sentence easier by including the object: ¿Me puede indicar dónde se toma el autobús? (“Can you show me where to catch the bus?”). A gesture or map is a useful partner to language, not a failure.

At a hotel or guesthouse

A booking confirmation, address and name on your phone solve many problems before you say a word. Keep the key noun and polite request simple. A regional speaker may use different words for a room, a lift or a booking; repeating the noun from the confirmation can help both of you find the same thing.

SpanishMeaningSay it, approximately
Tengo una reserva a nombre de …I have a reservation under the name …TEN-goh OO-nah reh-SER-bah
¿A qué hora es el desayuno?What time is breakfast?ah keh OH-rah es el deh-sah-YOO-noh
¿Hay wifi?Is there Wi-Fi?eye WEE-fee
No funciona la llave.The key does not work.noh foon-SYOH-nah lah YAH-beh
¿Puede llamar un taxi?Can you call a taxi?PWEH-deh yah-MAR oon TAK-see

Ordering food and explaining a need

Use a polite opener such as Quisiera …, por favor (“I would like …, please”) or ¿Me trae …? (“Could you bring me …?”). Menus and meal customs differ; ask what a dish contains instead of assuming that a familiar name means the same thing everywhere. If you have an allergy, state it clearly and confirm the response. Do not rely on a phrasebook alone for a severe allergy; write the ingredient down and use a translation support you trust.

SpanishMeaningSay it, approximately
Una mesa para dos, por favor.A table for two, please.OO-nah MEH-sah PAH-rah dos
¿Me trae el menú, por favor?Could you bring me the menu, please?meh TRAH-eh el meh-NOO
Quisiera agua sin gas.I would like still water.kee-SYEH-rah AH-gwah seen gahs
¿Qué recomienda?What do you recommend?keh reh-koh-MYEN-dah
No como carne.I do not eat meat.noh KOH-moh KAR-neh
Soy alérgico / alérgica a …I am allergic to …soy ah-LER-hee-koh / ah-LER-hee-kah
La cuenta, por favor.The bill, please.lah KWEN-tah por fah-VOR

In some places the menu calls itself la carta; elsewhere menú is the everyday choice. Tipping expectations and meal timing vary. Observe the local setting or ask politely rather than assuming that what you know from one country applies everywhere.

Prices, directions and a quick clarification

For a price, ask ¿Cuánto cuesta? for one item or ¿Cuánto cuestan? for several. In response, numbers may be spoken quickly, so ask the person to write the amount or show the total. For directions, a map plus ¿Está cerca? (“Is it nearby?”) and ¿A la derecha o a la izquierda? (“To the right or left?”) is enough to get started.

SpanishMeaningSay it, approximately
¿Cuánto cuesta?How much does it cost?KWAN-toh KWEHS-tah
¿Me lo puede escribir?Can you write it down for me?meh loh PWEH-deh es-kree-BEER
¿Está lejos?Is it far?es-TAH LEH-hos
Siga todo recto.Go straight ahead.SEE-gah TOH-doh REK-toh
Gire a la derecha / izquierda.Turn right / left.HEE-reh ah lah deh-REH-chah / ees-KYER-dah
Estoy perdido / perdida.I am lost.es-TOY per-DEE-doh / per-DEE-dah
¿Puede mostrarme en el mapa?Can you show me on the map?PWEH-deh mos-TRAR-meh

If something goes wrong

Learn the phrase that requests help before you need it: Necesito ayuda (“I need help”), Llame a la policía, por favor (“Call the police, please”), and ¿Dónde está el hospital? (“Where is the hospital?”). In an emergency, speak slowly, share your location and use a written place name. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or ask a nearby person to do so.

Useful clarification phrases include ¿Puede repetirlo? (“Can you repeat it?”), No hablo mucho español (“I do not speak much Spanish”), and ¿Puede hablar más despacio? (“Can you speak more slowly?”). They tell the other person how to help you instead of leaving them to guess.

Common travel mistakes and practice

Do not use embarazada to say “embarrassed”; it usually means “pregnant.” Do not assume quiero is rude in every context, but learn quisiera as a softer request. Do not use one local transport word as if it were universal. And do not hide an allergy inside a vague phrase—name the ingredient, confirm it, and carry a written note for serious risks.

Practise a three-turn role-play: greet a hotel worker, explain your request, and ask them to repeat one detail. Then play both roles out loud. Change the situation to a café or station but reuse the same frames. Record the phrases you needed and listen for the words that disappeared when you were nervous.
