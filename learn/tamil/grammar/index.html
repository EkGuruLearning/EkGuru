Tamil grammar

Tamil word order, verbs, pronouns and the mistakes everyone makes — explained, not listed.

1. The verb goes last

Tamil is subject–object–verb: நான் சாதம் சாப்பிடுகிறேன் (nāṉ sātam sāppiḍukiṟēṉ) — मैं चावल खाता हूँ — “I eat rice (word-for-word: I · rice · eat)”. Learn this once and every sentence parses.

2. Gender lives in the verb (third person)

Like Telugu, Tamil nouns don't force masculine/feminine on you, but third-person verbs agree three ways: அவன் சாப்பிடுகிறான் (he eats), அவள் சாப்பிடுகிறாள் (she eats), அது சாப்பிடுகிறது (it eats). First and second person never split.

4. Present tense — one full verb

“To eat” (சாப்பிட) in the present. One verb, seven endings: the ending tells you WHO — and in the third person, their gender. Spoken Tamil shortens the long formal endings (இருக்கிறீர்கள் → இருக்கீங்க); the tables below show the written forms.

6. Negation hides inside the verb

Tamil negates with an infix: நான் சாப்பிடுகிறேன் (I eat) → நான் சாப்பிடமாட்டேன் (sāppiḍamāṭṭēṉ, I don't eat). The -மாட்ட- chunk in the middle carries the whole 'not'.

The sentence ladder

Do not learn grammar as a list of rules. Learn it as a ladder with seven rungs, where every rung is something you can say out loud today.

  1. Say what you are. Simple I-sentences with no verb change.
  2. Say what you want. The same pattern with a noun from the common words list.
  3. Say what you do. The present-tense table above, first person only.
  4. Say what someone else does. The same table, third person — this is where the endings move.
  5. Say what happened. Past tense, five sentences about yesterday.
  6. Say what will happen. Future tense, five sentences about tomorrow.
  7. Say what does not happen. Negative forms of five sentences you have already built.

The grammar rule that matters most. One verb, seven endings: the ending tells you WHO — and in the third person, their gender. Spoken Tamil shortens the long formal endings (இருக்கிறீர்கள் → இருக்கீங்க); the tables below show the written forms. Learn the pattern, then change one thing at a time — verb, then person, then tense. Learners who change everything at once produce sentences they cannot debug.
