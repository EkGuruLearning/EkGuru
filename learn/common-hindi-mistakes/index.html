10 Mistakes Every Hindi Learner Makes — And How to Stop

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

A free guide from EkGuru · updated 2026-09-11

A note on the Hindi spellings

These guides are written and typed by hand, and Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong — a missing matra, the wrong nukta, a halant in the wrong place. If a word here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page, and please tell us so it gets fixed. The romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard — Hindi has no single agreed romanisation, so you will see shukriya and shukriyaa both used.

These are the ten that come up again and again. Most learners make at least six of them, and every one is fixable once you can see it.

1. Getting gender agreement wrong

❌ Main karta hoon said by a woman
✅ Main karti hoon

The verb agrees with the speaker's gender in the present tense. This is the most common error by a wide margin. Full explanation in our gender guide.

2. Confusing त and ट

English has one T. Hindi has two, and they are different words.

त — tongue against your teeth, softer than English
ट — tongue curled to the roof of the mouth, close to English T

Same for द/ड and न/ण. An app cannot hear you say these wrongly. A person can.

3. Putting the verb in the middle

❌ Main khaata roti hoon
✅ Main roti khaata hoon

The verb goes last. Always. See our word order guide.

4. Using में where nothing is needed

❌ Main school mein jaata hoon
✅ Main school jaata hoon

With जाना (to go), the destination takes no postposition. Mein means you are going around inside the school, not to it.

5. Saying dhanyavaad to friends

Not wrong, but formal enough to sound stiff. Most people say शुक्रिया in conversation — and among close family, often nothing at all. Thanking a family member for something small can feel distancing rather than polite.

6. Using तू too early

Between close friends it is warm. From a stranger it is rude. Start with आप for everyone and let people invite you to relax it. See our politeness guide.

7. Forgetting the ने in past tense

❌ Main khaana khaaya
✅ Maine khaana khaaya

With a transitive verb in the past, the subject takes ने. This one is genuinely difficult and even fluent second-language speakers slip on it. Intransitive verbs — गया, आया, सोया — do not take it.

8. Translating "I am" too literally

❌ Main hoon bhookha
✅ Mujhe bhookh lagi hai — literally "hunger has attached to me"

Hindi expresses many states as things happening to you rather than things you are. Cold, hunger, thirst, fear and liking all work this way: mujhe thand lag rahi hai, mujhe chai pasand hai.

9. Reading only romanised Hindi

Roman script cannot distinguish त from ट, or the long and short vowels. Every learner who avoids Devanagari hits a ceiling on pronunciation and does not know why. Six weeks of twenty minutes a day is enough to read — our alphabet guide covers it.

10. Waiting until you feel ready to speak

The most damaging of the ten, and it is not a grammar point. Learners who wait until they are confident never become confident. Learners who speak badly from week one become fluent.

Say the wrong thing out loud. Get corrected. Say it again. That is the entire method, and it is why the single most useful thing you can add to free study is someone who talks back.

Every mistake on this list is a speaking mistake — meaning every one of them proves you opened your mouth and tried. Fix them in order, one a week, and keep talking.

The honest summary

Mistakes 1, 2 and 7 need a person to hear you. The rest you can fix by reading carefully and paying attention. Do not pay for lessons to learn word order — pay for lessons to fix your pronunciation and your gender agreement, because those are the two that self-study genuinely cannot reach.
