Learn to read Hindi the way every Indian child does: letters, then signs, then words, then a story.

🔊 Tap any Hindi word to hear it. Progress is ticked off in your own browser and never leaves it.

Nothing marked yet Start at lesson 1

Open any class-1 Hindi reader printed in India and the order is always the same. First the स्वर (vowels) and व्यंजन (consonants), each with the one picture-word a child learns it by. Then words made of bare letters — कमल , नमक . Then one मात्रा per chapter: आ की मात्रा के शब्द, इ की मात्रा के शब्द, and so on through all twelve, each chapter with a word ladder, a handful of sentences and a short passage that uses only what has been taught. Then the dots, the half letters, and finally a real story. This section is that book, free, with sound on every word.

Who this is for. Anyone starting Devanagari from zero — adult learners, children of the diaspora, parents teaching at home. It assumes nothing. If you already read Hindi slowly, start at lesson 5 and use the word ladders as speed drills.

How to use it

  1. One lesson a day, twenty minutes. The matra lessons are the slow ones; give them two days if you need to.
  2. Say everything aloud. Tap the speaker, copy it, tap again. Silent reading builds recognition; speech builds reading.
  3. Write what you read. Each lesson ends with a copying or dictation task. That is the step textbooks have and apps skip.
  4. Do not jump ahead. Every word in lesson 9 uses only the signs from lessons 1 to 9. The ladder only works if you climb it in order.
  5. Turn the pages. Each lesson opens one step at a time — the sign, the words, then six short practice pages (match, listen, build the word, picture, read it, order the sentence) made from that lesson's own words — and ends with the quick check. Press Next, or choose Whole lesson to read it straight through.

Where this fits

This section teaches reading. It deliberately avoids grammar, gender and tenses — those are in Daily Hindi (days 16 onward) and the A1 course. The alphabet guide explains the system in one sitting; the barakhadi chart drills all twelve signs on every letter; this section is the slow, graded walk between the two — the one a child actually takes.

Finished all eighteen?

You can read. Now get the sounds checked by a person — a single lesson fixes the two or three letters you cannot hear yourself mispronouncing.
