Hindis worksheet — sample

Five questions from the Hindis quiz bank on this page’s topic. Pick a topic and press Make worksheet for a fresh sheet, or print this one as it is.

1. How many letters are there in the Devanagari alphabet?

2. Why is the alphabet the right place to start, before vocabulary?

3. What does the guide give you to help the letters stick?

4. What does this guide honestly compare?

5. What does the guide say about “learn Hindi in 30 days” promises?

Answers

1. 44 — 44 — each with one sound. That finite size is why the alphabet comes first: once you know the letters you can read anything.

2. Hindi is phonetic and finite — Because Hindi is phonetic and finite. Two or three weeks on the letters and you can sound out any word, which makes vocabulary easier, not harder.

3. A printable chart and a learning order — A printable chart and the order to learn the letters in — pin the chart up and work through the letters in that order.

4. Apps, YouTube, textbooks and tutors — A frank comparison of every way to learn Hindi online — apps, YouTube, textbooks and a tutor.

5. They are unrealistic — The guide is frank that real fluency takes consistent months-to-years of work, not a month.

How the Hindi worksheet is built

Choose a topic and a number of prompts, and the builder lays out a printable worksheet with writing space and an answer section at the end. It is designed for paper: one topic per sheet, prompts in a readable size, answers on their own block so they can be folded away. Printing from the browser prints just the sheet, with the watermark and this site's name kept on it.

Marking and reusing a sheet

Write your answers first, then check them against the answer block and note the ones that needed a second attempt — those are the words to review tomorrow. Nothing is stored for you, so print a fresh sheet for a fresh attempt; five finished sheets make an honest revision pack.
