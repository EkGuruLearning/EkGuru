Meet the vowels

After this lesson you can recognise the eleven independent vowel letters, distinguish some common pairs and explain how a vowel sign differs from a full letter. Start with three, then come back for the rest.

अ
अनार

anaar · pomegranate

The first vowel: a short, central sound, roughly like the vowel in “about”.

आ
आम

aam · mango

A longer open vowel, roughly like the vowel in “father”.

इ
इमली

imlee · tamarind

A short front vowel, roughly like the vowel in “sit”.

English comparisons are approximate. Roman spellings here are learning aids, not exact pronunciation rules.

The full set

अ · आ · इ · ई · उ · ऊ · ऋ · ए · ऐ · ओ · औ

अं has anusvara and अः has visarga: these are vowel-plus-mark forms, not two extra independent vowels. The sound of anusvara depends on context. Chandrabindu ँ marks vowel nasalisation; visarga ः is uncommon in everyday Hindi.

Independent vowels and matras

A full vowel can begin a syllable. After a consonant, a dependent vowel sign (matra) usually changes its inherent vowel: क is ka, का is kaa, and कि is ki. Notice that ि is written to the left of क but pronounced after it. Practise the इ matra.

Listen and compare

Listen to the examples, then repeat them slowly. Compare the first vowels in अनार and आम, then the first vowels in इमली and ईख. Vowel quality and length both matter; English approximations vary.

Handwriting practice

Choose a letter and trace the pale printed model with a mouse, finger or stylus. Clear or undo and try from memory. Compare the headline, curves and spacing yourself. Printed fonts are models, not verified stroke-order diagrams; handwriting styles vary. No automatic recognition or scoring is used.

Check your learning

Choose an answer for each question, then check. Try again as often as you like.

1. Which independent vowel begins आम (mango)?

2. In कि, when is the vowel sound pronounced?

3. What is the dot in अं?

Answer key and explanations
  1. आम begins with आ, the longer open vowel.
  2. कि is pronounced ki: the sign is placed before the consonant visually, but follows it in speech.
  3. अं combines अ with anusvara; it is not an extra independent vowel.
