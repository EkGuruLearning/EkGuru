Hindi Verbs — Present, Past and Future in One Page

A note on the Hindi spellings

These guides are written and typed by hand, and Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong — a missing matra, the wrong nukta, a halant in the wrong place. If a word here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page, and please tell us so it gets fixed. The romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard — Hindi has no single agreed romanisation, so you will see shukriya and shukriyaa both used.

Hindi verbs change with tense, gender and number at once, which sounds worse than it is. There are only a handful of endings, and they repeat across every verb in the language.

All examples use करना (karna, to do). The stem is कर — remove the -ना from any verb and you have its stem.

Present — what you do habitually

WhoMale speakerFemale speaker
मैं (I)करता हूँकरती हूँ
तुम (you, informal)करते होकरती हो
आप (you, formal)करते हैंकरती हैं
वह (he/she)करता हैकरती है
हम (we)करते हैंकरती हैं
वे (they)करते हैंकरती हैं

The pattern: -ता masculine singular, -ती feminine, -ते masculine plural or formal. Then हूँ / हो / है / हैं depending on who.

Present continuous — what you are doing now

Stem + रहा/रही/रहे + हूँ/है/हैं

मैं कर रहा हूँ — I am doing (man)
मैं कर रही हूँ — I am doing (woman)
वे कर रहे हैं — they are doing

Past — what you did

Stem + आ/ई/ए

मैंने किया — I did (man or woman; see the ने rule below)
वह गया — he went · वह गई — she went
हम आए — we came

The ने rule, which catches everyone

In the past tense, if the verb is transitive — it takes an object — the subject takes ने, and the verb then agrees with the object, not the subject.

  • मैंने रोटी खाई — I ate bread (khaai, because roti is feminine)
  • मैंने खाना खाया — I ate food (khaaya, because khaana is masculine)

Intransitive verbs — go, come, sleep, sit — take no ने:

  • मैं गया — I went. Not maine gaya.

This is the hardest single rule in beginner Hindi. Even confident second-language speakers slip on it, and getting it wrong will not stop you being understood.

Future — what you will do

Stem + ऊँगा/ऊँगी, ोगे, ेंगे

मैं करूँगा — I will do (man) · मैं करूँगी — (woman)
तुम करोगे — you will do
आप करेंगे · हम करेंगे · वे करेंगे

क्रियाThe verb is the engine.

The five verbs to learn first

These five cover an enormous amount of everyday speech:

होना to be · करना to do · जाना to go · आना to come · चाहना to want

होना is irregular and worth memorising outright: हूँ, हो, है, हैं.

The honest shortcut

You do not need all three tenses to start speaking. Learn the present properly, and use kal (yesterday/tomorrow) with it for everything else while you build up. "Main kal Delhi jaata hoon" is not correct Hindi, but you will be understood perfectly, and you will be speaking — which is the thing that actually moves you forward.

Add the past tense in month two, the ने rule in month three, and stop worrying about it.
