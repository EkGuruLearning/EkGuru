Telugu grammar

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Word order, verbs, pronouns and the mistakes everyone makes — explained, not listed.

1. The verb goes last

Telugu is subject–object–verb: నేను అన్నం తింటాను (nēnu annaṁ tinṭānu) — मैं चावल खाता हूँ — “I eat rice (word-for-word: I · rice · eat)”. Learn this once and every sentence parses.

2. Gender lives in the verb (third person)

Telugu nouns don't force masculine/feminine on you, but third-person verbs agree: వాడు తింటాడు (he eats), ఆమె తింటుంది (she eats). First and second person never split — నేను తింటాను is said by men and women alike.

4. Present tense — one full verb

“To eat” (తినడం) in the present. One verb, seven endings: the ending tells you WHO — and in the third person, their gender. Formal మీరు shares its ending with they.

you (intimate)నువ్వుతింటావుtinṭāvu
you (formal)మీరుతింటారుtinṭāru

6. Negation changes the ending

Telugu negates by swapping the verb ending: నేను తింటాను (I eat) → నేను తినను (tinanu, I don't eat). No extra word — the ending does the work.

8. Counting words

Telugu counts directly — but people get special number-words:

TeluguHindiNote
ఒక పుస్తకం (oka pustakaṁ)एक किताबone book — number + noun, no extras
రెండు పండ్లు (reṇḍu paṇḍlu)दो फलtwo fruits — counting form as listed
ముగ్గురు మనుషులు (mugguru manuṣulu)तीन लोगthree people — ముగ్గురు is the people-form of three; Telugu numbers change for humans
