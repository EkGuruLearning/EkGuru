Urdu grammar

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Word order, verbs, pronouns and the mistakes everyone makes — explained, not listed.

1. The verb goes last

Urdu is subject–object–verb: میں روٹی کھاتا ہوں۔ (Main roṭī khātā hūn.) — मैं रोटी खाता हूँ। — “I eat bread. (word order: I – bread – eat)”. Learn this once and every sentence parses.

2. Two genders, just like Hindi

Every noun is masculine or feminine, and verbs and adjectives agree with it — exactly like Hindi. کھاتا (khātā) is a man eating; کھاتی (khātī) is a woman eating. نیلا گھر (blue house, m) but نیلی قمیض (blue shirt, f). If you know Hindi, you already know this chapter.

4. Present tense — one full verb

“To eat” (کھانا) in the present. The present is stem + -تا (m) / -تی (f) + helper: ہوں (I), ہو (tum), ہیں (āp and they), ہے (is). So میں کھاتا ہوں (I eat, m), آپ کھاتے ہیں (you eat, respectful), وہ کھاتی ہے (she eats). Exactly like Hindi — only the script changed.

6. Negation with نہیں (nahīn)

نہیں (nahīn) goes before the verb: میں نہیں کھاتا (I don't eat), وہ نہیں آیا (he didn't come). For commands use نہ or مت: یہاں نہ بیٹھیں (don't sit here). Exactly like Hindi.

8. Counting words

Urdu has no classifiers — put the number straight before the noun. But the noun itself goes plural: ایک کتاب (one book), دو کتابیں (two books), تین روپے (three rupees). Exactly like Hindi.
