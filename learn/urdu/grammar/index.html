Urdu grammar

Urdu word order, verbs, pronouns and the mistakes everyone makes — explained, not listed.

1. The verb goes last

Urdu is subject–object–verb: میں روٹی کھاتا ہوں۔ (Main roṭī khātā hūn.) — मैं रोटी खाता हूँ। — “I eat bread. (word order: I – bread – eat)”. Learn this once and every sentence parses.

2. Two genders, just like Hindi

Every noun is masculine or feminine, and verbs and adjectives agree with it — exactly like Hindi. کھاتا (khātā) is a man eating; کھاتی (khātī) is a woman eating. نیلا گھر (blue house, m) but نیلی قمیض (blue shirt, f). If you know Hindi, you already know this chapter.

4. Present tense — one full verb

“To eat” (کھانا) in the present. The present is stem + -تا (m) / -تی (f) + helper: ہوں (I), ہو (tum), ہیں (āp and they), ہے (is). So میں کھاتا ہوں (I eat, m), آپ کھاتے ہیں (you eat, respectful), وہ کھاتی ہے (she eats). Exactly like Hindi — only the script changed.

6. Negation with نہیں (nahīn)

نہیں (nahīn) goes before the verb: میں نہیں کھاتا (I don't eat), وہ نہیں آیا (he didn't come). For commands use نہ or مت: یہاں نہ بیٹھیں (don't sit here). Exactly like Hindi.

8. Counting words

Urdu has no classifiers — put the number straight before the noun. But the noun itself goes plural: ایک کتاب (one book), دو کتابیں (two books), تین روپے (three rupees). Exactly like Hindi.

تین روپے (tīn rupay)तीन रुपयेthree rupees — same pattern as Hindi

The sentence ladder

Do not learn grammar as a list of rules. Learn it as a ladder with seven rungs, where every rung is something you can say out loud today.

  1. Say what you are. Simple I-sentences with no verb change.
  2. Say what you want. The same pattern with a noun from the common words list.
  3. Say what you do. The present-tense table above, first person only.
  4. Say what someone else does. The same table, third person — this is where the endings move.
  5. Say what happened. Past tense, five sentences about yesterday.
  6. Say what will happen. Future tense, five sentences about tomorrow.
  7. Say what does not happen. Negative forms of five sentences you have already built.

The grammar rule that matters most. The present is stem + -تا (m) / -تی (f) + helper: ہوں (I), ہو (tum), ہیں (āp and they), ہے (is). So میں کھاتا ہوں (I eat, m), آپ کھاتے ہیں (you eat, respectful), وہ کھاتی ہے (she eats). Exactly like Hindi — only the script changed. Learn the pattern, then change one thing at a time — verb, then person, then tense. Learners who change everything at once produce sentences they cannot debug.
