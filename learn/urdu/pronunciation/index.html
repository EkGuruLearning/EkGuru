Urdu pronunciation, reading & writing

The sounds learners get wrong, and the path from your first letter to reading real words. Urdu is written right to left in the beautiful Nastaliq style — start at the RIGHT end of the line! Letters change shape (start/middle/end) and join up, there are no capital letters, and short vowels (a/i/u) are usually left unwritten: you read مل as 'mil' (meet) or 'mal' by context. Dots do heavy lifting: ب ت ث ن ی are the same stroke with different dots. Persian gave Urdu پ چ ژ گ, India gave it ٹ ڈ ڑ, and nasalization comes from ں (nūn-ghunna): میں (main, I) vs مین — count the dots, then read.
