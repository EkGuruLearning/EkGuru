Urdu basics — the script, sounds and first steps

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

The starting point: the Urdu (Nastaliq), your first words, and the honest answer to where to begin. Urdu is written right to left in the beautiful Nastaliq style — start at the RIGHT end of the line! Letters change shape (start/middle/end) and join up, there are no capital letters, and short vowels (a/i/u) are usually left unwritten: you read مل as 'mil' (meet) or 'mal' by context. Dots do heavy lifting: ب ت ث ن ی are the same stroke with different dots. Persian gave Urdu پ چ ژ گ, India gave it ٹ ڈ ڑ, and nasalization comes from ں (nūn-ghunna): میں (main, I) vs مین — count the dots, then read.

The alphabet at a glance

8 vowels and 40 consonants. The full tables with sounds are on the pronunciation page — learn to recognise them before you memorise any words.

The order to learn in

  1. Script + sounds — Urdu is phonetic: learn the letters and you can read anything.
  2. Greetings + pronouns — say hello, introduce yourself, ask where.
  3. Numbers 1–20 — prices, time and quantities unlock immediately.
  4. Word order + present tense — one pattern (میں روٹی کھاتا ہوں۔) builds a thousand sentences.
  5. Topics (food, travel, shopping) — learn what your life needs first.
