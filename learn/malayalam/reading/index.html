How to read Malayalam script — a step-by-step guide

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-03. Editorial policy and corrections.

Draft: not yet reviewed by a native speaker. Existing availability and indexing are preserved by owner instruction; that is not an editorial approval. Suggest a correction.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Malayalam has the roundest letters of any Indian script — all curves, no headline bar, written left to right. It is fully phonetic: each letter is one sound, always. There are 13 vowels (അ ആ ഇ ഈ ഉ ഊ ഋ എ ഏ ഐ ഒ ഓ ഔ, plus അം/അഃ) and 34 consonants, including the famous ഴ (ḻa) — a sound between r, l and d that even other Indians practise (മഴ, maḻa, rain). Letters stack into tall conjuncts: സ + ് + ത + ് + ര + ീ = സ്ത്രീ (strī, woman). Read slowly and sound every curve. Below is the path that gets you from "those are just shapes" to reading a real Malayalam sentence, in six steps you can work through in a fortnight of short sessions.

Step 1 — Meet the vowels

The Malayalam has 13 vowels, shown below with the sound each one makes.

Read the table left to right, out loud. Do not try to memorise it in one pass: recognition first, recall later. Two passes today and two tomorrow beats an hour tonight.

A realistic pace. Two letters a day with five minutes of writing each. In about three weeks the alphabet stops being a wall and becomes a slightly slow tool — which is exactly what it should feel like.

Step 5 — Read a whole sentence

Reading words is one skill; reading a sentence is a second one. Start with the word order you already know — ഞാൻ ചോറ് കഴിക്കുന്നു. — then read slowly, then at speaking speed.

Read each phrase below aloud three times. When you can read them without pausing, you have crossed from decoding to reading.

Step 6 — Make it a habit

  1. Five minutes reading the letter tables aloud, every day.
  2. Five minutes typing the words you learned in the practice typing trainer.
  3. Once a week, re-read a page you have already studied and notice how much faster it goes.
  4. Never read only in your head: the mouth learns the script faster than the eye does.

When reading starts to feel routine, push on to the grammar basics and the speaking-alone plan — reading is the door, not the room.
