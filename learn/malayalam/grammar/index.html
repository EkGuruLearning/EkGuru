Malayalam grammar

Malayalam word order, verbs, pronouns and the mistakes everyone makes — explained, not listed.

1. The verb goes last

Malayalam is subject–object–verb: ഞാൻ ചോറ് കഴിക്കുന്നു. (Ñān chōṟŭ kaḻikkunnu.) — मैं चावल खाता हूँ। — “I eat rice. (word order: I – rice – eat)”. Learn this once and every sentence parses.

2. Three genders, zero verb trouble

Malayalam has masculine, feminine and neuter pronouns (അവൻ/അവൾ/അത്) — but the verbs NEVER agree with any of them. കഴിക്കുന്നു means I/you/he/she/we/they eat, all at once. Adjectives don't change either: വലിയ വീട് (big house), വലിയ കുട്ടി (big child). Gender is the easiest chapter in this course.

4. Present tense — one full verb

“To eat” (കഴിക്കുക) in the present. Here is Malayalam's famous gift: the present is stem + -ഉന്നു for EVERYONE. ഞാൻ, നീ, അവൻ, അവൾ, നമ്മൾ, അവർ — all take കഴിക്കുന്നു. One form, no exceptions. The table below proves it.

6. Negation with ഇല്ല (illa)

ഇല്ല (illa) melts into the verb: വരില്ല (won't come), അറിയില്ല (don't know), കഴിക്കില്ല (won't eat). Alone, ഇല്ല just means 'no'. To refuse an offer, വേണ്ട (vēṇṭa — 'don't want') is warmer: ചായ വേണ്ട (no tea for me, thanks). And അല്ല (alla) denies identity: ശരിയല്ല (that's not right).

8. Counting words

Malayalam has no classifiers — put the number straight before the noun. But the noun itself goes plural with -കൾ: ഒരു പുസ്തകം (one book), രണ്ട് പുസ്തകങ്ങൾ (two books), മൂന്ന് രൂപ (three rupees).

MalayalamHindiNote

The sentence ladder

Do not learn grammar as a list of rules. Learn it as a ladder with seven rungs, where every rung is something you can say out loud today.

  1. Say what you are. Simple I-sentences with no verb change.
  2. Say what you want. The same pattern with a noun from the common words list.
  3. Say what you do. The present-tense table above, first person only.
  4. Say what someone else does. The same table, third person — this is where the endings move.
  5. Say what happened. Past tense, five sentences about yesterday.
  6. Say what will happen. Future tense, five sentences about tomorrow.
  7. Say what does not happen. Negative forms of five sentences you have already built.
