Day 4 — Thank you, sorry, please

The idea

Hindi politeness does not work like English politeness. कृपया (please) is almost never spoken — courtesy lives in the verb ending instead. And between family and close friends, thank you is often omitted entirely, which is warmth rather than rudeness.

Today's task

Do this, not just read it

Learn शुक्रिया before धन्यवाद. It is the one people actually use.

The mistake to avoid

Watch for this

Saying धन्यवाद to a friend. It is correct and it is stiff, like saying 'I am much obliged' to your flatmate.

Where this sits in the thirty days

This is day 4 of 7 in the sounds and script phase (days 1–7), whose job is hearing Hindi accurately and reading Devanagari slowly. It assumes you have done day 3. It sits in the middle of the phase, which is where most of the actual learning happens and where most people quietly stop. Day 5 continues from here.

How long today should take

About 23 to 33 minutes if you do the task properly rather than reading past it. Missing a day is not a reason to stop — the sequence matters, the calendar does not.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.
