Day 12 — Reading your first words

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

The idea

You now know enough letters to read. Not fluently — slowly, sounding out, the way a child does. That is the correct stage and skipping it is why so many learners never read at all.

Today's task

Do this, not just read it

Read these aloud: नाम, काम, घर, पानी, बड़ा, छोटा. Sound out each letter, then say the whole word.

The mistake to avoid

Watch for this

Reading the romanisation first and then 'checking' the Devanagari. Cover the roman column.

Where this sits in the thirty days

This is day 5 of 7 in the first sentences phase (days 8–14), whose job is saying true things about yourself without assembling them word by word. It assumes you have done day 11. It sits in the middle of the phase, which is where most of the actual learning happens and where most people quietly stop. Day 13 continues from here.

How long today should take

About 30 to 40 minutes if you do the task properly rather than reading past it. Script days are slower than they look: recognising a letter takes a few seconds, writing it takes a minute, and the writing is what makes the recognition stick. The speaking part is not optional decoration — saying it aloud badly is worth more than reading it correctly, because your mouth learns nothing from your eyes. There are 5 new words today. If that is too many, take the first three and leave the rest; 5 half-known words are worth less than three you can produce. Missing a day is not a reason to stop — the sequence matters, the calendar does not.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.

Practise today's task with a person

Today's task is a speaking task. Doing it alone is better than not doing it, and doing it with someone who corrects you is worth about five times as much.
