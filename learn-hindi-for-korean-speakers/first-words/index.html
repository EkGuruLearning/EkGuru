The first 100 Hindi words, for Korean speakers

Word frequency is steep in every language: the first two hundred words carry a startling share of ordinary conversation. Learn those before anything more interesting, and check first which ones Korean has already given you.

A word worth knowing about

차 (cha) / चाय (chaay)

Looks like: tea in both. In practice: a true cognate — both trace to the same Chinese root through different routes. Several more like it exist along the old trade paths.

Words you may already have

Three structural gifts that almost no course will tell you about. Word order, above. Particles: 은/는, 이/가, 을/를 and 에 do precisely the work Hindi's ने, को, से and में do, in the same position after the noun. And grammatical politeness — Korean encodes respect in the verb ending, and so does Hindi, so the 요/습니다 versus 반말 instinct is exactly the आप/तुम/तू instinct. Korean's three-way stop distinction is a fourth: your ear already separates plain, aspirated and tense consonants, which means क versus ख costs you nothing while it defeats English speakers for months.

This matters practically, not just as a curiosity. If Korean has given you a few hundred half-recognisable Hindi words, a beginner course that starts you at zero is wasting weeks. Find out first, then start where you actually are.

How to store them so they stick

Never a bare noun. Store a short phrase that carries the gender: not किताब but यह किताब अच्छी है. The adjective ending encodes the gender, so recalling the phrase recalls the gender — and you practise the agreement at the same time.

Say it aloud before you check. Recognition and production are different abilities and only one of them is worth having. A deck reviewed silently builds the one you need less.

Ten a day, daily. Twenty minutes every day beats three hours on a Sunday by a wide margin, because memory consolidates between sessions and there is nothing to consolidate if they are a week apart.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.

Questions

How many Hindi words do I need?

About 500 for everyday survival, 1,500 for comfortable conversation. The first 200 do most of the work, which is why they are worth learning before anything more interesting.

Do Korean speakers already know any Hindi words?

Often yes. a true cognate — both trace to the same Chinese root through different routes. Several more like it exist along the old trade paths.

Should I learn words or phrases?

Phrases, mostly. A bare noun teaches you a translation; a short sentence teaches you the word doing its job, including its gender and the postposition it takes. In Hindi the gender is not optional information.
