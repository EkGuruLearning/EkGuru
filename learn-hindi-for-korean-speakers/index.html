Learn Hindi for Korean speakers

Your first language decides which parts of Hindi are easy and which are slow, and almost no course takes account of it. This page does. It is written for someone who already speaks Korean and wants to know what that is worth before spending a year finding out.

Where the verb goes

Identical where it counts. Korean is subject-object-verb and so is Hindi: 저는 밥을 먹습니다 and मैं चावल खाता हूँ have the same shape, element for element. The rebuilding-every-sentence problem that English speakers face simply does not arise for you.

What Korean gives you for free

Three structural gifts that almost no course will tell you about. Word order, above. Particles: 은/는, 이/가, 을/를 and 에 do precisely the work Hindi's ने, को, से and में do, in the same position after the noun. And grammatical politeness — Korean encodes respect in the verb ending, and so does Hindi, so the 요/습니다 versus 반말 instinct is exactly the आप/तुम/तू instinct. Korean's three-way stop distinction is a fourth: your ear already separates plain, aspirated and tense consonants, which means क versus ख costs you nothing while it defeats English speakers for months.

What it does not prepare you for

Voicing. Korean does not distinguish voiced from voiceless the way Hindi does — ㄱ is somewhere between g and k depending on position, while Hindi keeps क and ग permanently separate. That is your real sound work. Grammatical gender is the other: Korean has none at all, and Hindi marks it on nouns, adjectives and verbs together, so it must be built from nothing.

Sounds to work on

WhatWhy it matters
Voicing, not aspirationYou already have aspirated versus plain. What is new is that Hindi also contrasts voiced क/ग, प/ब, त/द independently. This is the mirror image of the English speaker's problem.
Retroflex ट ठ ड ढNo Korean equivalent. The tongue curls back. Expect a few weeks.
ㄹ splits into twoKorean's single liquid becomes Hindi's separate ल and र, which are different letters in different words.
No final-consonant neutralisationKorean neutralises final stops; Hindi releases them fully. दोस्त ends in a real t.

Grammar, compared with Korean

FeatureHow it compares
Particles map onto postpositions은/는/이/가/을/를 behave like ने/को. Same job, same position.
Honorifics transfer directlyThe 요/습니다 register is आप; 반말 is तुम. You will handle this more naturally than any European learner.
Gender is genuinely newKorean has no grammatical gender. Learn every Hindi noun with its gender attached from day one, or relearn several hundred later.
No countersHindi has nothing like 개, 명, 마리. Numbers attach directly to nouns. A rare simplification.

A word worth knowing about

차 (cha) / चाय (chaay)

Looks like: tea in both. In practice: a true cognate — both trace to the same Chinese root through different routes. Several more like it exist along the old trade paths.

A realistic first thirty days

  1. Days 1–7: voicing drills. क versus ग, प versus ब, त versus द. Your specific bottleneck, and not the one you would expect.
  2. Days 8–15: Devanagari. It is an alphabet with vowel marks, conceptually closer to Hangul's syllable blocks than to Latin, and Korean learners usually move through it quickly.
  3. Days 16–26: gender, drilled hard, because you have no foundation for it.
  4. Days 27–30: sentences. The structure is already yours — this part will feel like recognition rather than learning.

Notice that the order is not the order a general course would use. That is the whole point of organising this by your first language: the sequence that works depends on what you already have.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.

Questions Korean speakers ask

Is Hindi hard for Korean speakers?

It depends entirely on what you are comparing. Three structural gifts that almost no course will tell you about. Word order, above. Particles: 은/는, 이/가, 을/를 and 에 do precisely the work Hindi's ने, को, से and में do, in the same position after the noun. What is the hardest part of Hindi for a Korean speaker?

Voicing. Korean does not distinguish voiced from voiceless the way Hindi does — ㄱ is somewhere between g and k depending on position, while Hindi keeps क and ग permanently separate. That is your real sound work. Grammatical gender is the other: Korean has none at all, and Hindi marks it on nouns, adjectives and verbs together, so it must be built from nothing.

Do Korean speakers need to learn Devanagari?

Not to speak. Many learners work in romanised Hindi for months. If you want to read signs, messages or anything written, the script is phonetic and consistent, and most learners read simple words within a few weeks.

How long does Hindi take from Korean?

Nobody can give you an honest number, because it depends on hours per week far more than on your first language. What this page can tell you is which parts will be quick for you and which will be slow, which is more useful than a figure.

The part you cannot do alone

Everything above can be read. The sound work in particular cannot be self-assessed — you cannot hear your own errors, which is precisely why they fossilise. A tutor is the only mechanism that fixes that, and an hour a week is enough.
