Bengali pronunciation: sound Bengali, not textbook

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Six sounds that matter, two traps to dodge — the fastest pronunciation upgrade in the language.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Learn Bengali spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Six sounds carry Bengali pronunciation, and the first is the famous one: অ is aw, as in English law. ক is kaw, গান is gaan… wait, no — গান keeps its া (aa), which is exactly the point: only the built-in অ says aw. And at word ends it rounds further to o: কথা (kotha), ভালো (bhalo).

Second: the three sh-letters. শ, ষ and স all sound like English sh in modern Kolkata speech — শহর (shohor), কষ্ট (koshto), সকাল (shokal). Purists keep subtle distinctions; learners should say sh everywhere and be understood everywhere. One less thing to fear.

Third and fourth: the r-family. র is a light tap or trill (রাত — rat, night). ড় is the flap — tongue flicks like the tt in American butter (বড় — boro, big). Mix them and words change meaning, so drill minimal pairs: করা/কড়া, পড়া/পরা. Fifth: য় is just y (মেয়ে — meye). Sixth: ছ is breathy chh — push air through ছেলে (chhele, boy).

Two traps to dodge. Trap one: stress sits early and light — Bengali does not punch syllables like English. Trap two: the ো vs অ ending (bhalo not bhalaw). Record yourself reading this page's table, compare with the speaker buttons at 0.2×, and fix one sound a week. In six weeks you will sound like someone's neighbour.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on the final -o. Word-final অ rounds to o: কথা is kotha not kawtha, ভালো is bhalo. Middle অ stays aw: কলকাতা is Kawlkata. Final-o, middle-aw — chant it until it is reflex.

Questions learners ask

How is অ really pronounced?

Like <i>aw</i> in English <i>law</i>: ক is <i>kaw</i>, কথা is <i>kotha</i>. At the end of words অ often rounds fully to <i>o</i>. This one sound is the audible line between Hindi speakers trying Bengali and real Bengali speakers.

Are শ, ষ and স all the same sound?

To a learner's ear, yes — all three (শ ষ স) sound like English <i>sh</i> in most Kolkata speech. Say <i>sh</i> for all of them and refine later; nobody will misunderstand you.

র vs ড় — what is the difference?

র is a light trill/tap, ড় is the English-like flap (like the <i>tt</i> in American <i>butter</i>). Wrong one, wrong word: করা (kora — to do) versus কড়া (kora — strict). Minimal pairs are the gym for this distinction.
