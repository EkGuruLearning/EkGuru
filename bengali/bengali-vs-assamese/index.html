Bengali vs Assamese

মই vs আমি, বৰ vs খুব — India's closest sisters, compared word by word.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Learn Bengali spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Bengali and Assamese are the closest sisters in India: neighbours along the Brahmaputra, sharers of grammar, script and most vocabulary. আমি (ami — I) in Bengali is মই (moi) in Assamese; আপনি (apni) is আপুনি (apuni); খুব (khub — very) is বৰ (bor). Same sentences, shifted sounds — learn one and the other leans in.

The scripts are twins with two tells. Assamese ৰ (ro) carries a diagonal stroke where Bengali র stands plain; Assamese ৱ (wo, as in বৰ ভাল pronounced bor bhal) has no Bengali equivalent at all. Everything else — অ আ ক খ, the matra wire, the conjunct stacks — reads identically. One diagonal letter, and you know which sister wrote it.

The great sound shift is the স-transformation: where Bengali says sh (স, শ, ষ), Assamese says kh — অসম is Oxom, not Oshom. Hear it once and every Assamese word tilts into focus; the spelling stays familiar while the mouth goes its own way. It is the single strangest and most lovable fact in either language.

Mutual understanding runs like Spanish-Portuguese: slowly, generously, with laughter. Shared words carry you (ভাল, জল, মাছ, ঘৰ/ঘর), shifted sounds test you, and goodwill translates the rest. Learn Bengali first for its ocean of books and films — then spend one week on Assamese songs, and greet the sister properly: নমস্কাৰ.

Questions learners ask

Can Bengalis and Assamese understand each other?

Partly — like Spanish and Portuguese speakers meeting slowly. Shared grammar, huge shared vocabulary, but the sounds drift: Assamese turns every স/শ/ষ into খ (kh) — অসম becomes Oxom. Give it a week of listening and the fog lifts.

How do I tell the scripts apart?

Two letters: ৰ (Assamese ro, with its diagonal) where Bengali writes র, and ৱ (Assamese wo) with no Bengali twin. Spot a ৰ and you are reading Assamese — the single most useful letter in the northeast.

Which should I learn first?

Start with Bengali — ten times the learners, films, books and teachers — then add Assamese ears later. The grammar transfers almost whole; only the sounds and a few hundred words need relearning. Two sisters, one effort, nearly.
