Shopping and bargaining in Bengali

দাম কত? কম হবে? — market Bengali that pays for itself from the first shop.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

The market runs on one question: দাম কত? (dam koto — how much?). The answer comes as a number plus টাকা (taka) — পঞ্চাশ টাকা (fifty taka). Listen for the number, repeat it back to confirm — পঞ্চাশ? — and you have completed your first Bengali transaction before you know any grammar at all.

Then comes the dance. বেশি দাম (beshi dam — too expensive), said thoughtfully, opens bargaining anywhere outside fixed-price shops. কম হবে? (kom hobe — will it come down?) is the direct question; ছাড় দিন (chhar din — give a discount) is its bolder cousin. Start near two-thirds, meet in the middle, smile throughout — bargaining in Bengal is a friendly game, and grim faces lose.

Count what you buy like a local: একটা (one thing), দুটো (two things), তিনটে (three things). এক কেজি আলু (one kilo of potatoes), দুটো শাড়ি দেখি (let me see two saris — দেখি, dekhi, is the magic browsing word). Shopkeepers hear the counters and switch you from tourist prices to real prices.

Close the deal with three sentences: এটা নেব (eta nebo — I'll take this), ঠিক আছে (thik achhe — agreed), ধন্যবাদ (dhonnobad — thank you). And the escape hatch every browser needs: শুধু দেখছি (shudhu dekhchhi — just looking), which is honoured in every market in Bengal without a flicker of annoyance.

A note on দাম, মূল্য and ভাড়া. দাম (dam) is the price of things, ভাড়া (bhara) is the fare for rides and rent — never swap them. মূল্য (mullo) is the formal written word for price, seen on bills and boards. Three words, three worlds: দাম কত? in the shop, ভাড়া কত? in the taxi, মূল্য on the receipt.

Questions learners ask

Should I bargain in Bengal?

In markets and small shops, always — start around two-thirds of the asking price and meet in the middle with a smile. In malls and fixed-price shops, never; the price tag is the price. কম হবে? (kom hobe — will it come down?) asked cheerfully is the whole technique.

How do I count things I am buying?

দুটো and তিনটে, never bare দুই and তিন before a noun. Things take টা/টে (একটা শাড়ি — one sari), people take জন. Counters are the fastest way to sound like a buyer instead of a tourist.

How do I say I am just looking?

শুধু দেখছি (shudhu dekhchhi — just looking), with a smile. It is respected everywhere and frees you to browse ten shops before buying in the eleventh.
