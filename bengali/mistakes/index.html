Mistakes every Bengali learner makes

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

না at the end, টা on everything, no feminine verbs — ten classic slips, fixed for good.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Learn Bengali spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Mistake one: আমি যায়. It feels right and it is wrong — যায় (jay) belongs to সে (he/she), while আমি takes যাই (jai). আমি যাই, তুমি যাও, আপনি যান: person and ending must march together. Every verb works this way, so learn verbs in threes from day one and this error never takes root.

Mistake two: hunting feminine verbs. সে খায় is he eats AND she eats — Bengali verbs never change for gender, full stop. Learners arriving from Hindi keep inventing feminine endings; stop, there are none, and this is one of the kindest simplifications in any major language. Gender lives in nouns and pronouns, never in verbs.

Mistake three: না in English position. I do not know becomes আমি জানি না — the না parks itself at the very end, after the verb. আমি না জানি sounds as broken to Bengali ears as me not knows does to yours. Rule of thumb: when in doubt, put না last and you will usually be right.

Mistake four: bare numbers on nouns. দুই বই (two books) is understood and instantly foreign — দুটো বই (duto boi) is how people speak. Things take টা/টে, people take জন: তিনটে আম (three mangoes), দুজন বন্ধু (two friends). Four mistakes, four fixes — and every one of them upgrades you from textbook speaker to real speaker the day you fix it.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on খাবার and খাওয়া. The most delicious confusion in Bengali: খাবার (khabar) is food, the thing — খাওয়া (khaoa) is eating, the act. খাবার খাওয়া (khabar khaoa — eating food) uses both at once and every learner says it backwards at least once. Food is খাবার; the eating of it is খাওয়া.

Questions learners ask

Why is আমি যায় wrong?

আমি যায় — because যায় (jay) is he/she goes, while I go is আমি যাই (ami jai). Bengali verbs change with every person, so the pronoun and the ending must match: আমি যাই, তুমি যাও, সে যায়. Drill the threes and the error dies.

Where are the feminine verb forms?

Because Bengali verbs don't care about gender at all — সে খায় means he eats and she eats identically. Learners from Hindi or French backgrounds keep hunting for feminine endings that don't exist. There are none; celebrate.

Where does না go in a sentence?

At the very END: আমি জানি না (I don't know), সে যাবে না (he won't go). English puts not early, Bengali puts না last — the most common word-order slip in the language, and the easiest to fix once you see it.
