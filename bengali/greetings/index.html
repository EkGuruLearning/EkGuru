Saying hello the Bengali way

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

নমস্কার opens every door in Bengal — but friends, elders and goodbyes each have their own rules.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Learn Bengali spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

নমস্কার (nomoshkar) is the one greeting that never fails. Folded hands, a small nod, and it works with strangers, elders, shopkeepers and officials — exactly the people a learner meets first. Muslim Bengalis often greet with আসসালামু আলাইকুম or the shorter আদাব (adab); if someone greets you that way, answering the same way is both correct and courteous.

Friends do not nomoshkar each other — it would sound like a speech. They ask কী খবর? (ki khobor — what's the news?) or simply start talking. A useful halfway step is দাদা (dada — elder brother) or দিদি (didi — elder sister) for anyone a little older than you, relative or not. A rickshaw puller called দাদা will quite literally take better care of you.

Bengali has three levels of you, and this is the part worth learning early. আপনি (apni) is formal — strangers, elders, anyone you respect. তুমি (tumi) is for friends, peers and younger family. তুই (tui) is intimate — small children, your closest friends, and God. When unsure, আপনি is never wrong; sliding down to তুমি happens naturally when someone invites it.

And goodbye: Bengalis rarely say anything as final as goodbye. The person leaving says আসি (ashi — I'm off, I'll be back), and the person staying answers এসো (esho — do come again). For thanks, ধন্যবাদ (dhonnobad) works everywhere; অনেক ধন্যবাদ (many thanks) costs nothing extra and buys real warmth.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on the Bengali spellings. Bengali hides small traps for new eyes — the ৎ that silently ends words, vowel signs that wrap around both sides of a letter, joint letters that merge beyond recognition. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and tell us so it gets fixed. The romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

Questions learners ask

Can I just say namaskar?

Yes, and nobody will mind — নমস্কার is understood everywhere in Bengal. But friends will smile, because it sounds formal. With friends try কী খবর? (ki khobor — what's new?) instead.

How do I greet an elder respectfully?

নমস্কার with folded hands and a small nod, and use আপনি (apni — formal you) until they invite you to তুমি. Add দাদা (dada) or দিদি (didi) for someone a little older — it warms everything.

How do Bengalis say goodbye?

You ask leave instead of saying goodbye: আসি (ashi — I'm off). The other person answers এসো (esho — come again). It is one of the loveliest small customs in the language.
