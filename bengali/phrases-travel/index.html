Travel phrases for Bengal in Bengali

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

ডান, বাম, সোজা — directions, fares and tickets in the sentences travellers actually say.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Learn Bengali spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Directions are three words plus pointing: ডান (dan — right), বাম (bam — left), সোজা (shoja — straight). ডানদিকে যান (go to the right side), বামদিকে (to the left), সোজা যান (go straight). Add সামনে (shamne — ahead) and পেছনে (pechhone — behind), and you can follow any set of directions a helpful stranger draws in the air.

Every journey starts with two questions. কত দূর? (koto dur — how far?) tells you whether to walk or ride. ভাড়া কত? (bhara koto — how much is the fare?) asked BEFORE sitting down is the difference between the local price and the tourist price. Memorise these two before you memorise anything else on this page.

Stations and stops have their own vocabulary: বাস স্টপ (bus stop), রেল স্টেশন (rail station), টিকিট কাউন্টার (ticket counter), প্ল্যাটফর্ম (platform). টিকিট (ticket) plus your destination plus দিন (din — give) buys almost anything: একটা টিকিট দিন (one ticket please). Platforms are announced in Bengali first — শিয়ালদহ প্ল্যাটফর্মে (on Sealdah platform) is the kind of sentence your ears will learn to catch.

In the vehicle, three sentences cover everything: চলুন (cholun — let's go), থামুন (thamun — please stop), এখানে (ekhane — here). পৌঁছে গেছি (pouche gechhi — I've arrived) closes the journey with a smile. Drivers in Bengal respect passengers who speak even ten words — speak them, and the city starts helping you.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on যাব, খাব, করব. The future tense in Bengali is almost suspiciously easy: verb stem plus -ব (bo). যাব (jabo — will go), খাব (khabo — will eat), দেখব (dekhbo — will see). One ending, every verb, no exceptions that matter — the future is the first tense that makes learners grin.

Questions learners ask

How do I tell a taxi driver where to go?

Start with the place, end with যাব (jabo — will go): স্টেশন যাব (station jabo — I'll go to the station). Add ভাড়া কত? (bhara koto — how much is the fare?) before you sit down, and you will travel Bengal at local prices.

How do I ask where something is?

এটা কোথায়? (eta kothay — where is this?) while pointing at the address on your phone. Pointing plus three words beats a paragraph of memorised grammar every single time.

How do I ask the driver to stop?

থামুন (thamun — please stop). Say it early, say it clearly, and have the fare ready — Bengal's drivers stop fast and appreciate passengers who do the same.
