Bengali verbs: করি, করলে, করব

Present, past and future with zero jargon — one verb teaches you the pattern for nearly all of them.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Learn Bengali spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Bengali verbs are kinder than they look. Take করা (kora — to do): the root কর never changes, and small endings do all the work. Present tense: আমি করি (ami kori — I do), তুমি করো (tumi koro — you do), আপনি করেন (apni koren — you do, formal). Three endings, and the pattern repeats across almost every verb in the language.

The past adds one sound: ল. করলাম (korlam — I did), করলে (korle — you did), করলেন (korlen — you did, formal). The future adds ব: করব (korbo — I will do), করবে (korbe — you will), করবেন (korben — you will, formal). Present, past, future — three endings each, all built on the same root.

The verb to be splits in two, and this split is the most useful thing on this page. হই (hoi) is for identity: আমি ছাত্র (ami chhatro — I am a student). আছি (achhi) is for state and place: আমি ভালো আছি (I am fine), আমি বাড়িতে আছি (I am at home). Identity versus situation — feel that difference and half of Bengali sentences unlock.

Start with five verbs and refuse to add a sixth until these are automatic: করা (do), যাওয়া (go), আসা (come), খাওয়া (eat), দেওয়া (give). Everything you want to say in your first month is built from these five plus nouns. Fluency is not knowing many verbs; it is these five at speed.

A note on করব vs করবো. You will see the future ending written both ways — করব and করবো. Both are the same word; the extra ো marks the o sound explicitly. Spelling wobbles like this are normal in Bengali; pronounce the o either way.

Questions learners ask

What is the difference between হই and আছি?

Both mean <i>am/is</i>, split by meaning: হই (hoi) for identity — আমি ছাত্র (I am a student) — and আছি (achhi) for state and place — আমি ভালো আছি (I am fine), আমি বাড়িতে আছি (I am at home). Mix them and you still get understood; split them and you sound right.

How do I say I/you do something?

Endings, not separate words. আমি করি (I do), তুমি করো (you do), আপনি করেন (you do, formal). Learn the three endings for one verb and you can conjugate nearly every verb you meet.

How do I talk about yesterday?

Lengthen the past: করলাম (I did), করলে (you did), করলেন (you did, formal). One extra ল sound carries the whole past tense.
