Counting in Bengali: 1 to 100

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

এক দুই তিন — plus the little counting words that textbooks forget and markets demand.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Learn Bengali spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Bengali digits — ০১২৩৪৫৬৭৮৯ — are everywhere in Bengal: price tags, bus numbers, clocks, exam papers. International digits appear too, but the Bengali set is the one you cannot fake, so learn to read it first. The shapes are friendly: ১ ২ ৩ are barely disguised cousins of 1 2 3.

The words one to ten are এক, দুই, তিন, চার, পাঁচ, ছয়, সাত, আট, নয়, দশ. Eleven to twenty go their own way (এগারো, বারো, তেরো…), and then the tens take over: বিশ (20), তিরিশ (30), চল্লিশ (40), পঞ্চাশ (50), ষাট (60), সত্তর (70), আশি (80), নব্বই (90), একশো (100). Past twenty, numbers are built by glueing tens to units — একুশ (21), বত্রিশ (32) — and hearing them in a market teaches them faster than any list.

Now the part textbooks skip: you almost never put a bare number next to a noun. Things take টা — একটা বই (one book), তিনটে আম (three mangoes). People take জন — একজন মানুষ (one person), দুজন বন্ধু (two friends). Using the bare number is understood and instantly marks you as foreign; the counters are small words with an outsized payoff.

Two places to spend your new numbers immediately. Money: the currency is the টাকা (taka), and দাম কত? (dam koto — how much?) is the most useful sentence in Bengal. Time: the clock reuses টা in a special form — তিনটে means three o'clock. Learn that one sentence and that one clock trick, and your numbers are already earning their keep.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on টা becoming টে. Counters shift shape in real speech: একটা (one thing) but তিনটে (three o'clock), দুটো (two things). Natives never think about it and learners should not memorise tables of it — hear each form inside its sentence, on this page and in the market lessons, and the pattern absorbs itself.

Questions learners ask

Which digits will I actually see in Bengal?

Both, mixed freely. Shop signs, bus numbers and clocks usually show Bengali digits; phone numbers and prices are often in international digits. Learn to read Bengali digits first — that is the direction you cannot fake.

What are counters (টা, জন)?

Little counting words that sit between the number and the noun: একটা বই (one book), দুজন মানুষ (two people). Skip them and you sound like a textbook; use them and you sound like a person.

How do I ask the price of something?

দাম কত? (dam koto — how much?). Then listen for the number plus টাকা (taka). Market Bengali is the fastest teacher there is.
