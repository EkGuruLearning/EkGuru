Bengali among Indian languages

Sister scripts, cousin words, 22 official languages — Bengali's place in the family.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Learn Bengali spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Bengali belongs to the Indo-Aryan family — daughter of Sanskrit, sister of Hindi, Urdu, Marathi and Gujarati, cousin to Punjabi and Nepali. Two hundred and thirty million speakers make it the world's seventh language by native speakers, and India's second after Hindi. This is not a small language you are learning; it is a civilisation.

The scripts are family portraits: Bengali অ আ ক খ and Devanagari अ आ क ख share the same ancient skeleton, the same matra wire, the same conjunct habit. Learn one script and the second comes at half price — Hindi readers decode Bengali signs within weeks, sounding them out like a familiar song in a new singer's voice.

The Dravidian south — Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam — is a different family entirely: different ancestors, different grammar deep down, though millennia of neighbours have swapped thousands of words. A Bengali in Chennai starts nearly from zero; a Bengali in Delhi starts halfway. India's language map rewards travellers who notice.

Twenty-two official languages share one constitution, and code-switching oils every border: আমি meeting-এ যাচ্ছি (I'm going to the meeting) is as natural as breathing. Respect the mix but learn the pure forms first — মাতৃভাষা (mother tongue) before mashup. Then কটা ভাষা জানেন? (how many languages do you know?) becomes your favourite bragging question.

A note on বাংলা ভাষা. Speakers call it বাংলা (Bangla) among themselves and Bengali in English — never Bengalese, never Bengalee in modern use. বাংলা ভাষা (Bangla bhasha — the Bengali language) is the full formal name, heard in anthems, examinations and proud introductions.

Questions learners ask

How close are Bengali and Hindi?

Cousins, through Sanskrit: both Indo-Aryan, both SOV, both with আপনি/आप formality. Shared words everywhere (পানি/पानी, বই/किताब differs though!). Hindi speakers learn Bengali fast — and Bengalis usually arrive with Hindi already loaded.

How many official languages does India have?

Twenty-two, in the Constitution's Eighth Schedule — Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Gujarati, Urdu, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi and twelve more. India has no national language; it has twenty-two official ones and hundreds of living mothers.

Do Bengalis mix English into Bengali?

Constantly, and joyfully — Bangla-English mixing (আমি meeting-এ যাচ্ছি) mirrors Hinglish exactly. Purists grumble in both languages; everyone under forty code-switches mid-sentence. Learn the pure forms first, then join the mix.
