Marathi among Indian languages

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Indo-Aryan roots, Sanskrit elders, Dravidian neighbours — Marathi's family place.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Marathi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Marathi anchors western India: हिंदी (Hindi), गुजराती (Gujarati), बंगाली (Bengali) — cousins, not strangers. Shared Sanskrit roots, shared Devanagari curves, proud separate literatures. Learn Marathi and Hindi signboards start whispering.

The south speaks Dravidian: तमिळ (Tamil), तेलुगू (Telugu), कन्नड (Kannada), मल्याळम (Malayalam) — different family, shared nation: भारत (India) holds both, and भाषांतर (translation) bridges all.

Sanskrit threads everything: संस्कृत lent Marathi thousands of formal words — तत्सम vocabulary crowns speeches and scriptures. Daily speech mixes happily. Both registers are real Marathi; learners need both.

Unity crowns diversity: एकता (unity) — 22 official languages, hundreds of mother tongues, one conversation. Marathis learn Hindi for Delhi, English for globe, Marathi for soul. Every Indian language you meet makes your Marathi richer.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on अभिजात. अभिजात (abhijaat — classical) crowns Marathi officially — classical-language status with 2000 years of literature behind it. Dnyaneshwar to Deshpande, unbroken. Classical pride runs deep and joyful; learners inherit the celebration.

Questions learners ask

Is Marathi related to Hindi?

Close cousins — Marathi and Hindi are both Indo-Aryan, both Devanagari, sharing thousands of words. Same grandparents, different parents.

Which language is closest to Marathi?

हिंदी for daily overlap, गुजराती next door, then संस्कृत above all. The marathi-vs-hindi page maps the closest pair.

Why so much Sanskrit in Marathi?

Two millennia of poetry, prayer and scholarship — संस्कृत words fill formal Marathi while grammar stays Prakrit-born. Sanskrit lends vocabulary; Marathi keeps its soul.
