Marathi

Reading Marathi

Words to sentences to stories — reading Marathi from first signs to full pages.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Marathi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Reading starts with words: शब्द (word) by शब्द — वाचा (please read) signs, labels, names. Marathi spelling mirrors speech, so sounding out works: see दुकान, say dukaan, know shop. Phonetic scripts reward beginners richly.

Sentences assemble next: वाक्य (sentence) — subject, object, verb-last. Read SOV sentences end-first for meaning: find the verb, then ask who-did-what. Backwards parsing feels odd for a week, then automatic forever.

Paragraphs and articles follow: परिच्छेद (paragraph), लेख (article), कविता (poem). Children's stories first — short, illustrated, kind — then मासिक (magazines), then साहित्य (literature) itself. Marathi's written heritage spans eight centuries; start at the shallow end.

Dictionaries deepen everything: शब्दकोश (dictionary) — five new words per page, written down, reviewed nightly. Reading without recording entertains; reading with recording teaches. Mine every page; keep every gem.

A note on पेपर. Marathi newspapers — लोकसत्ता, सकाळ — use formal written Marathi, nobler than street speech. Headlines first: big type, short lines, daily habit. One headline a day keeps illiteracy away.

Questions learners ask

What should I read first?

Signboards: दुकान (shop), स्टेशन (station), हॉटेल (restaurant) — big letters, daily repetition, instant rewards. Streets teach faster than textbooks.

When am I ready for books?

Children's books — short वाक्य (sentences), pictures for context, full conjuncts. Then comics, then पेपर headlines, then articles.

How do I handle unknown words?

Keep a शब्दकोश (dictionary) app open and mine unknown words — five per page, no more. Reading with a dictionary beats studying word lists: context glues memory.
