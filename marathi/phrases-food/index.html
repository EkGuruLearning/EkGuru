Food phrases in Marathi

वडा पाव to पुरणपोळी — order, eat and praise food in Marathi.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Marathi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Meals structure the day: नाश्ता (breakfast), जेवण (lunch/dinner), चहा (tea). चहा घेऊया? (shall we have tea?) — Maharashtra runs on cutting chai and conversation. Accept every cup; refuse nothing twice.

Ordering is easy bravery: मला एक चहा हवं आहे (I want one tea), बिल द्या (please give the bill). एक वडा पाव द्या (give one vada pav) — order Mumbai's soul in Marathi. Street vendors reward attempts with extra chutney.

Staples fill the thali: भात (rice), पोळी (flatbread), डाळ (lentils), भाजी (vegetables). भाजी काय आहे? (what's the vegetable dish?) — ask daily, eat variously. Thali eating teaches vocabulary with every bite.

Tastes tune the tongue: तिखट (spicy), गोड (sweet) — पुरणपोळी (sweet stuffed bread) crowns feasts. कमी तिखट करा (make it less spicy) — then खूप छान! (very nice!). Praise the cook; second helpings follow.

A note on जेवण. जेवण (jevan — meal) is sacred in Maharashtra — जेवण झालं का? (have you eaten?) is love disguised as a question. Answer हो, झालं (yes, done) and watch faces glow. Food is affection, served hot.

Questions learners ask

How do I talk about meals?

जेवण (meal, esp. lunch/dinner), नाश्ता (breakfast/snacks), चहा (tea). मला भूक लागली (I'm hungry) opens every food conversation.

How do I order food?

हे काय आहे? (what is this?) and मला वडा पाव हवं आहे (I want vada pav). Point, ask, order — menus fear the curious.

How do I describe tastes?

तिखट (spicy), गोड (sweet), खारट (salty), आंबट (sour). कमी तिखट (less spicy) saves sensitive tongues.
