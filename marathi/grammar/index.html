Marathi

Marathi grammar, plainly explained

Three genders, postpositions, verb-final order — grammar without tears.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Marathi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Three genders shape everything: masculine (मुलगा — boy), feminine (मुलगी — girl), neuter (मूल — child, घर — house). Adjectives triple — चांगला/चांगली/चांगलं — and verbs triple too. Gender is not decoration; it is Marathi's skeleton.

Pronouns simplify persons: मी (I), तू (you-informal), तुम्ही (you-formal), तो/ती/ते (he/she/it), आम्ही (we). आम्ही vs आपण (exclusive vs inclusive we) — Marathi distinguishes; English doesn't. Choose your we wisely.

Postpositions replace prepositions: -ला (to), -ने (by/with-instrument), -शी (with-person), -मध्ये (in). मुंबईला जा (go to Mumbai), चमच्याने खा (eat with a spoon). Suffixes after nouns — think backwards from English.

Verbs anchor sentences last: मी भात खातो (I rice eat), ती शाळेत जाते (she to-school goes). Present (खातो), past (खाल्लं), future (खाईन) — tense on verbs, time words framing. Verb-final: the one rule that never breaks.

A note on ला. ला (laa) is Marathi's direction-giver — मुंबईला (to Mumbai), घरी (homeward), मला (to-me). Stick ला on places and people to show movement or feeling-targets. Tiny suffix, enormous workload.

Questions learners ask

Three genders — really?

Masculine, feminine, neuter — nouns, adjectives AND verbs agree. मोठा/मोठी/मोठं (big), जातो/जाते/जातं (go). Triples everywhere; learn them as sets.

What are postpositions?

ला marks direction (मुंबईला — to Mumbai), ने marks doers (रामने केलं — Ram did), शी/शी means with. Postpositions follow nouns — learn each with ten examples.

What is Marathi word order?

Subject-Object-Verb: मी भात खातो (I rice eat). Verb last, always. English order is the classic beginner error.
