# Hindi A1–C2 CEFR Blueprint



**Design:** 8 units × 4 core lessons per level is recommended for the future product (192 core lessons), with labs and reviews outside that count. This is a blueprint, not course generation. CEFR is action-oriented; grammar lists support tasks rather than define levels.



## A1 — formulaic survival and script decoding

**Boundary:** A2 must not reteach isolated alphabet or introductions as its main outcome; script remains embedded remediation.

**Recommended units:** Devanagari sound map; identity and courtesy; family and possession; numbers/time; food and shopping; places and directions; daily routine; health and immediate needs

**Grammar:** SOV default and copular clauses; pronouns मैं/हम/आप/तुम with safe default आप; gender and direct singular/plural; है/हैं and negation नहीं; का/की/के; में/पर; basic को; habitual and progressive present; imperatives आइए/कीजिए and simple questions

**Vocabulary domains:** personal data; family; 0–100; days/time; food; prices; places; transport; body/basic health

**Pronunciation:** independent/dependent vowels; dental vs retroflex stops; aspirated vs unaspirated stops; anusvara/chandrabindu in common words; syllable timing and schwa-deletion awareness without requiring theory

**Communication tasks:** introduce self; ask/answer price and location; order politely; read signs/menu items; write a short form/message

**Skill outcomes:** listen: slow clear one-turn exchanges with visual support; speak: memorized phrases and short recombinations; read: letters, words, signs and very short notices; write: copy then independently type/write words and 3–5 linked sentences; interaction: ask for repetition and use courtesy formulas; mediation: relay a name, number, price or simple instruction

**Pragmatics/culture:** default to आप with strangers; नमस्ते is broadly usable; relationship and setting affect address; avoid teaching तू as a neutral you

**Final assessment:** script decoding/encoding; survival listening; two-turn roleplay; short form and message

**Promotion:** 80% core knowledge; 75% each skill; all real-world tasks completed; at least two independently scored speaking and writing performances; delayed review >=75% after 7–14 days; no compensation across a skill below threshold; automated scoring may support but not solely certify open production



## A2 — daily independence across predictable situations

**Boundary:** B1 must move beyond rehearsed transactions to connected narration, explanation, repair and opinion.

**Recommended units:** home and neighbourhood; travel and booking; services and public help; school/work routines; past experiences; plans and invitations; health and advice; social relationships

**Grammar:** oblique forms before postpositions; को/से functions in common constructions; perfective and ने introduction with agreement; future and intention; चाहिए/पड़ना/सकना; conjunctive participle कर/करके; basic जो…वह and जब…तब; comparison से

**Vocabulary domains:** housing; journeys; appointments; weather; clothes; study/work; events; common symptoms; digital communication

**Pronunciation:** consonant clusters and conjuncts; natural phrase grouping; common schwa deletion; loan sounds फ़/ज़/ख़ recognition

**Communication tasks:** book/change plans; describe a past event; give sequential instructions; make/decline invitation politely; resolve a routine service problem

**Skill outcomes:** listen: short announcements and predictable conversations at moderate speed; speak: connected short turns about routine and past/future; read: messages, schedules, simple articles and instructions; write: notes, invitations, complaints and 80–120-word descriptions; interaction: initiate, clarify and close routine exchanges; mediation: summarize practical details for another person

**Pragmatics/culture:** आप/तुम choice with explicit relationship cues; soft requests ज़रा…, क्या आप…; basic colloquial reductions recognized in audio

**Final assessment:** multi-step service scenario; announcement listening; short narrative; functional message

**Promotion:** 80% core knowledge; 75% each skill; all real-world tasks completed; at least two independently scored speaking and writing performances; delayed review >=75% after 7–14 days; no compensation across a skill below threshold; automated scoring may support but not solely certify open production



## B1 — connected independent narration, explanation and opinion

**Boundary:** B2 must require evidence, counterposition, professional genres and reliably controlled complex grammar—not longer B1 stories.

**Recommended units:** life stories; education and careers; travel disruptions; media and technology; community and environment; relationships and advice; culture and entertainment; projects and decisions

**Grammar:** aspect contrasts habitual/progressive/perfective; ergative agreement with ने and object marking; compound verbs लेना/देना/जाना nuance; experiencer constructions मुझे…लगता है; relative-correlative sets in varied cases; reported statements and requests; conditionals अगर…तो; causatives introduction

**Vocabulary domains:** biography; work/study processes; media; environment; emotions; community; problem solving; arts

**Pronunciation:** connected speech at normal conversational speed; focus via stress/particles ही/भी/तो; intonation for question, continuation and stance

**Communication tasks:** tell a structured story; explain a process; give reasons and examples; repair misunderstanding; summarize a familiar report

**Skill outcomes:** listen: main points of clear standard speech and narrative; speak: sustained familiar discourse with pauses and self-repair; read: straightforward news/features and narratives; write: 150–220-word connected narrative/opinion; interaction: manage unprepared familiar conversation; mediation: summarize key points and explain a process

**Pragmatics/culture:** disagree with reasons rather than blunt contradiction; distinguish textbook formal wording from everyday alternatives; introduce common Hinglish as context-dependent recognition, not a default norm

**Final assessment:** story retelling; problem-solving dialogue; article summary; reasoned opinion

**Promotion:** 80% core knowledge; 75% each skill; all real-world tasks completed; at least two independently scored speaking and writing performances; delayed review >=75% after 7–14 days; no compensation across a skill below threshold; automated scoring may support but not solely certify open production



## B2 — clear argument and professional/academic independence

**Boundary:** C1 must test implicit stance, register flexibility, dense authentic input, synthesis and editing rather than only explicit B2 argument.

**Recommended units:** workplace collaboration; evidence and claims; public issues; formal correspondence; negotiation; news and bias; academic comparison; presentations

**Grammar:** full causative contrasts including करवाना; passive and impersonal constructions; subjunctive/contingent and unreal conditions; reported speech and commands; participial modifiers; complex subordination यद्यपि/हालाँकि/जबकि; modality संभावना/अनिवार्यता; compound and conjunct verb choice

**Vocabulary domains:** institutions; policy; business; research basics; risk; law/public service; journalism; negotiation

**Pronunciation:** discourse-level phrasing; contrastive emphasis; clear delivery under spontaneous load; tolerance of mainstream speaker variation

**Communication tasks:** argue with evidence; chair a meeting segment; write formal complaint/proposal; compare sources; negotiate a compromise; present and answer questions

**Skill outcomes:** listen: extended standard speech, news and argument with explicit organization; speak: clear detailed argument without strain; read: complex factual prose and viewpoint articles; write: 250–400-word reports, formal correspondence and arguments; interaction: sustain negotiation and adapt explanations; mediation: synthesize two accessible sources and relay positions

**Pragmatics/culture:** calibrate directness by hierarchy and medium; recognize evaluative wording and source stance; switch between everyday and professional lexicon

**Final assessment:** source-based argument; professional simulation; news listening; formal report

**Promotion:** 80% core knowledge; 75% each skill; all real-world tasks completed; at least two independently scored speaking and writing performances; delayed review >=75% after 7–14 days; no compensation across a skill below threshold; automated scoring may support but not solely certify open production



## C1 — flexible, nuanced academic/professional discourse

**Boundary:** C2 must demand semantic precision, effortless reformulation, rhetorical control, culturally situated inference and original high-stakes production.

**Recommended units:** stance and evidentiality; discourse architecture; institutional Hindi; academic exposition; media rhetoric; mediation across registers; idiom and cultural allusion; advanced interaction

**Grammar:** fine aspect and vector-verb choices; complex relative and reduced participial structures; nominalisation and formal compound postpositions; focus, word order and particles; ellipsis in fluent speech; advanced negation and scope; dense subordination and cohesion; Sanskritic/Perso-Arabic lexical register choices

**Vocabulary domains:** abstract analysis; governance; research; editorial stance; organizational strategy; literary/media criticism; ethics; social nuance

**Pronunciation:** intonation for qualification and contrast; long-turn chunking; near-effortless intelligibility across formal and colloquial delivery

**Communication tasks:** synthesize conflicting sources; qualify a recommendation; edit register and cohesion; infer attitude; mediate technical content for a public audience; conduct professional interview

**Skill outcomes:** listen: extended implicit and structurally complex speech including some unfamiliar topics; speak: fluent well-structured long turns with flexible formulation; read: dense journalism, essays, policy and accessible literature; write: 500–800-word synthesis, proposal or critique; interaction: intervene diplomatically and negotiate nuanced positions; mediation: integrate, reformulate and explain implications across audiences

**Pragmatics/culture:** infer irony, hesitation and strategic ambiguity; control honorifics and institutional politeness; recognize code-switching purpose and avoid indiscriminate use

**Final assessment:** two-source synthesis; register editing; implicit-stance listening; professional panel simulation

**Promotion:** 80% core knowledge; 75% each skill; all real-world tasks completed; at least two independently scored speaking and writing performances; delayed review >=75% after 7–14 days; no compensation across a skill below threshold; C1/C2 require human moderation or dual-rater rubric for high-stakes certification



## C2 — precise, rhetorically controlled and culturally agile mastery

**Boundary:** Nothing is passed merely for rare vocabulary or length; every task must require precision, inference, synthesis, rhetorical or stylistic control.

**Recommended units:** semantic precision; implication and inference; rhetoric and persuasion; advanced professional genres; scholarly synthesis; literary and figurative language; negotiation under ambiguity; style and editorial mastery

**Grammar:** scope and information structure under marked order; subtle modality/evidentiality interaction; full stylistic effects of aspect and light verbs; complex clause compression and expansion; deliberate ambiguity and disambiguation; rhetorical parallelism, contrast and reformulation; idiom/proverb inflection and contextual force; creative norm-bending versus error

**Vocabulary domains:** near-synonym networks; specialized but transferable academic language; rhetorical metalanguage; idiom/collocation; administrative and literary style; humour and wordplay

**Pronunciation:** fine shades through rhythm, pause and prominence; spontaneous long-form delivery; adaptation to audience and genre without loss of clarity

**Communication tasks:** negotiate competing hidden interests; deliver expert presentation and hostile Q&A; produce publishable editorial/academic prose; interpret humour/allusion; translate and justify nuance; rewrite one proposition in multiple voices

**Skill outcomes:** listen: virtually any speech, including compressed, idiomatic and implicit discourse with reasonable exposure; speak: spontaneous precise extended discourse with fine meaning distinctions; read: complex literary, professional and scholarly texts critically; write: publishable, stylistically deliberate synthesis and argument; interaction: manage ambiguity, face and rhetoric in high-stakes exchanges; mediation: reconstruct arguments, preserve stance and adapt culturally loaded meaning

**Pragmatics/culture:** distinguish proposition, implicature, presupposition and rhetorical effect; shift formal/colloquial/literary/administrative style intentionally; explain regional or ideological markedness without stereotyping

**Final assessment:** multi-source critical synthesis; high-stakes negotiation; style portfolio with rationale; unprepared expert presentation and questions

**Promotion:** 80% core knowledge; 75% each skill; all real-world tasks completed; at least two independently scored speaking and writing performances; delayed review >=75% after 7–14 days; no compensation across a skill below threshold; C1/C2 require human moderation or dual-rater rubric for high-stakes certification



## Progression discontinuities

| Transition | Genuine change | Must not be repeated as the main challenge |

|---|---|---|

| A1→A2 | isolated survival turns become multi-step predictable independence | alphabet drills, name/hello loops |

| A2→B1 | rehearsed scenarios become connected narration, explanation and repair | scripted shopping with harder nouns |

| B1→B2 | familiar opinion becomes evidence-based argument and professional genre control | longer personal stories only |

| B2→C1 | explicit organized argument becomes implicit stance, synthesis, mediation and register flexibility | connector lists and formal vocabulary alone |

| C1→C2 | fluent high-level performance becomes precise semantic/rhetorical/cultural control under ambiguity | simply longer texts or rarer words |



## Global language core versus India/country context

**Global Hindi core:** script/sound system, grammar, high-frequency lexicon, register model, transferable communicative functions, rubrics and SRS prerequisites. **Context packs:** India public services, school/work conventions, transport, currency, regional listening, festivals/media/business etiquette. Country/learner-goal variants substitute scenarios and domain lexicon while retaining objective IDs; they may not clone dialogues or stereotype communities. Hindi-speaking diaspora packs require local institutions and code-switching realities.
