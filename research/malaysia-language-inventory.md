## 4. Constitutional backbone (what the entries cite)

- Art.152: Malay is the national language; Art.152(1)(b) preserves other
  communities' use/teaching of their languages — the legal basis for the
  vernacular-school system (unique in SE Asia at this scale).
- National Language Act 1967: Malay sole official language; English
  permitted in official use (de facto: courts/tertiary/business).
- Education arc: PPSMI 2003 (English for Science/Maths) → MBMMBI 2012
  reversal (Malay-medium restored, English "strengthened") → DLP
  dual-language program (parental choice) — status TBD §8.
- Sabah/Sarawak autonomy (MA63): English co-official in practice (Sarawak
  2015 ordinance); Iban/Kadazan-Dusun in state media/schools.

## 5. The ethnicity-proxy artefact (read community ≠ language)

Census ethnicity tables are routinely misquoted as language figures:
"Chinese 23%" ≠ Hokkien/Cantonese/Hakka/Mandarin split (urban youth shift
to Mandarin+English; rural elderly Hokkien/Cantonese-monolingual);
"Indian 7%" ≠ Tamil (Malayalam/Telugu/Punjabi minorities inside);
"Bumiputera 70%" folds Malay with 60+ Borneo/Orang-Asli languages. Rules:

- Entries use survey-based speaker estimates, never ethnicity counts.
- KL Cantonese (`yue`) as inter-Chinese lingua franca vs Penang Hokkien
  (`nan`) prestige vs Mandarin school standard — three functions, no
  single "Chinese Malaysian language".
- Iban (`iba`) Sarawak-dominant vs Kadazan-Dusun (`dtp`) Sabah-cluster:
  dtp is a CLUSTER code (Bundu/Liwan/Rungus...) — member split TBD §8.
