# Thailand Language Inventory — Deep Audit (Phase 6)

Batch: southeast-asia (pass 1) · Status: PRELIMINARY · Date: 2026-09-14

Thailand is the inventory's quiet paradox: no constitutionally-named
official language, three "regional dialects" with distinct ISO codes and
16M/6.5M/5.5M speakers, and 3–4M Myanmar/Cambodia/Lao migrant workers whose
languages are invisible in the data. This file is the Phase 6 deep audit:
the statute-not-constitution backbone, the dialect-vs-language politics
(codes used, positions not taken), and the migrant-language + Kedah-Malay
rulings pass 2 must make.

## 3. Gap analysis (EkGuru coverage)

- P1 plan for tha is correct (official 60M + regional lingua franca).
- tts (16M, Isan — Lao-continuum) is the largest Tai `none` after tha/lao;
  promotion watch; identity politics noted, not adjudicated.
- **Migrant-language gap (serious)**: Khmer (`khm` ~1M workers), Lao (`lao`
  ~1M workers), Burmese (`mya`) and Shan (`shn`, Mae Hong Son + workers)
  are NOT entered — jointly 2–4M residents invisible in the data. Pass-2
  immigrant-threshold ruling mandatory (§8, same rule as ZA sna).
- **Kedah-Malay gap**: Satun province speaks Kedah Malay (`meo`, distinct
  code — NOT Pattani `mfa`) — uninventoried. Pass-2 entry candidate.
- Mon (`mnw`, Kanchanaburi ~100K), Kuy (`kdt`, Surin ~300K), Moken/Moklen
  (Chao Lay), hill-tribe cluster (Hmong/Lisu/Lahu/Akha spillovers) sit at/
  below threshold — sweep to confirm §8.
- Teochew ancestry (~1M, Bangkok) is correctly Thai-folded (shift complete)
  — confirm in pass 2.

## 4. Constitutional backbone (what the entries cite)

- NO constitutionally-named official language (2007/2017 charters are
  silent) — Thai is official by statute, Royal Institute standard, and
  universal practice. The entry's `official` role cites practice+statute.
- National Language Policy (Royal Institute / committee drafts): Thai
  first, "local languages" supported — MTB pilots in Patani Malay and
  Karen (Mahidol-led, 2000s–2010s); scale-up status TBD §8.
- Deep-south policy: Yawi (Patani Malay, Jawi script) in Islamic/private
  schools; state-school Malay electives — conflict-zone figures handled
  figures-only throughout this file.
- 1999 Education Act: Thai medium; local-language "supplementary" use
  permitted — the legal ceiling for Isan/Lanna/Dambro in classrooms.

## 5. The dialect-vs-language politics (codes used, positions not taken)

State discourse calls Isan/Lanna/Dambro "Thai dialects" (phasa thin); ISO
assigns distinct codes (`tts`/`nod`/`sou`); linguists split by criterion
(intelligibility vs Abstand vs identity). This inventory's rule:

- Entries use ISO codes with speaker figures; the `regional` role records
  de-facto regional use, not state recognition.
- Isan (`tts`) sits on the Lao continuum (Vientiane intelligibility HIGH;
  written in Thai script, Lao-script heritage) — cross-noted with `lao`,
  never merged, never renamed.
- The same rule covers Pattani (`mfa` vs Kelantan/Government Malay) and
  Northern Khmer (`kxm` vs `khm`): codes decide entries, politics get notes.

## 6. Code treatments specific to Thailand

- **Isan/Lao pair**: `tts` (Isan, TH) vs `lao` (LA) — distinct codes,
  continuum in practice; same script now (Thai script), heritage differs.
- **Northern Khmer**: `kxm` (Surin/Buriram; Khmer-script heritage, Thai-
  script schooling) — distinct from `khm`; separate entries, cross-noted.
- **Pattani vs Kedah**: entry `mfa` (Narathiwat/Yala/Pattani, Jawi script)
  vs missing `meo` (Satun, Kedah Malay) — distinct codes; add meo §8.
- **S'gaw Karen**: `ksw` (Tak/Mae Hong Son + Tak camps) — same code as MM
  side; refugee-camp figures folded with dated note.
- **Lanna/Dambro scripts**: `nod` (Tai Tham/Lanna heritage) + `sou`
  (Thai-script only) — heritage-script notes, learner text in Thai script.
- **TSL**: `tsq` (Thai Sign Language; Bangkok/Chiang Mai standards) —
  distinct from ASL-influenced school history; corpus TBD §8.
