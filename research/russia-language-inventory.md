Batch: eastern-europe (pass 1) · Status: PRELIMINARY · Date: 2026-09-14

Ingush/Adyghe uninventoried), and the script + education-policy rulings

## 4. Constitutional backbone (what the entries cite)

- Constitution Art.68: Russian is the state language federation-wide;
  republics may establish their own state languages, used alongside Russian
  in government, courts, and schools.
- 2002 script law: republic state languages must use Cyrillic-based scripts
  — killed Tatarstan's 1999 Latin-switch law (annulled 2004); the legal
  ceiling on script autonomy.
- 2018 education amendments: minority-language study made voluntary
  (parental choice), ending compulsory Tatar/Bashkir/etc. instruction —
  protests in Kazan/Ufa; all regional bands carry shift-acceleration risk.
- Dagestan constitution: 14 written languages recognised (no single titular
  language) — Avar functions as interethnic lingua franca without formal
  primacy.

## 5. The republic-funnel artefact (status ≠ vitality — read roles with demography)

Republic co-official status correlates weakly with transmission: titular
groups are often minorities in their own republics (Kazan Tatars ~53%,
Ufa Bashkirs ~30%, Yakutsk Yakuts ~50%+), and urban youth shift to Russian
even where the titular language is "official". Consequences:

- `official-regional` is recorded as STATUS (accurate) while bands use
  census L1 (often declining) — the two must never be averaged into a
  "vitality score" without the urban-shift note.
- Chechen (`che`, Chechnya ~95% Chechen) and Tuvan (`tyv`, Tuva ~80%+
  Tuvan) are the vitality outliers — homeland-majority + rural transmission.
- Tatar (`tat`) is the prestige outlier — media, theatre, Kazan Federal
  University pipeline — but Kazan-city transmission still weakens.
