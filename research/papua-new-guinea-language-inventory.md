# Papua New Guinea Language Inventory — Deep Audit (Phase 6)

Batch: melanesia (pass 1) · Status: PRELIMINARY · Date: 2026-09-14

Papua New Guinea is the most linguistically diverse country on earth (≈840
living languages, ~12% of the world total) and the inventory's extreme case:
6 entries cover the national/official layer while 830+ vernaculars sit in the
long tail. This file is the Phase 6 deep audit: the three-language national
policy, the Tok Pisin umbrella, and the highlands-cluster rulings pass 2
must make.

## 1. Coverage

- Entries: **6** (English + Tok Pisin + Hiri Motu + 3 highlands regionals).
- Living languages (Ethnologue 27 / SIL PNG, medium confidence): **≈840**.
- Census language data: **none usable** — figures below are SIL survey
  estimates; highlands totals are the most-cited but least-verified numbers
  in the inventory.

## 3. Gap analysis (EkGuru coverage)

- `tpi` (5M, national lingua franca, growing urban L1) is the largest
  Melanesian `none` — promotion candidate if Pacific demand materialises.
- Highlands cluster (Huli `hui` ~150K, Wahgi `wgi` ~100K, Kewa `kew` ~100K,
  Wiru `wiu`, Duna `duc`) sits in notesLongTail: the enq/imn/kue entries

- PNG Sign Language is UNINVENTORIED (no ISO 639-3 code; village + urban
  signing, ASL influence in schools) — flag added to pass-2 list.
- Motu proper (`meu`, Austronesian, ~40K) vs Hiri Motu (`hmo`, pidgin):
  only the pidgin is entered; confirm the split holds in pass 2.

## 4. Constitutional backbone (what the entries cite)

- Constitution: English is the language of parliament and the official
  record; Tok Pisin and Hiri Motu are recognised national languages
  (three-language policy; National Language Policy 1980s, unevenly applied).
- Education: English medium from Grade 3; Tok Pisin/vernacular "tokples"
  prep-schools recognised but underfunded — the policy/practice gap is the
  Pacific's widest.
- Bougainville autonomy: Tok Pisin + English + vernaculars (Nasioi `nas`,
  Buka) in the autonomous government's language posture — no separate
  officialisation.

## 5. The Tok Pisin umbrella (creole-continuum artefact)

Tok Pisin spans basilect (rural second-language) to acrolect (Port Moresby
L1 youth, "Tok Pisin bilong taun") with heavy English code-mixing at the
top. Consequences:

- `tpi` band uses the 5M total with a confidence penalty; urban-L1 growth
  (Wantok newspaper, FM radio, parliament debate) is the trend to watch.
- Learner Tok Pisin = Wantok-standard orthography (Weeks/SIL), rural-first
  lexicon with urban recognition modules (Phase 10 variation note).
- Continuum neighbours Pijin (`pis`, SB) and Bislama (`bis`, VU) are
  DISTINCT codes — mutually intelligible in practice, never merged in data.

## 6. Code treatments specific to Papua New Guinea

- **Enga**: `enq` (Trans–New Guinea; Wabag) — largest vernacular (300K);
  dialect chain (Mae/Laip vs Raiapu) mutually intelligible, one code holds.
- **Melpa**: `imn` (Mt Hagen; "Hagen") — 150K; Hagen-market lingua franca
  of the Western Highlands.
- **Kuman**: `kue` (Chimbu/Simbu) — 100K; not to be confused with Kumam
  (`kdi`, UG Nilotic) — name-trap noted.
- **Hiri Motu**: `hmo` (Austronesian-based pidgin, "Police Motu") —
  declining under Tok Pisin pressure; Gulf/Western provinces remnant.
- **Motu proper**: `meu` NOT entered (long-tail) — confirm in pass 2 that
  hmo/meu separation matches speaker reality.
- **Kiwai/Gogodala gaps**: Gulf Province clusters (`gog` Gogodala ~30K,
  `kiw` Northeast Kiwai) are below threshold — confirm no entry-class
  cluster was missed in the Fly/Gulf lowlands.
