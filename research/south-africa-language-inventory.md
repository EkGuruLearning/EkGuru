Batch: southern-africa (pass 1) · Status: PRELIMINARY · Date: 2026-09-14

South Africa is the inventory's official-language extreme: 12 official
languages (11 spoken + SASL since 2023) under a Constitution that names them
all, plus a Section 6(5) heritage list no other country replicates. This file
is the Phase 6 deep audit: the s.6 backbone + 2023 SASL amendment, the
Nguni/Sotho cluster structure, and the immigrant-threshold + Sepedi-standard
rulings pass 2 must make.

## 3. Gap analysis (EkGuru coverage)

- All 11 spoken official languages sit at `none` — correct for pass 1: each
  is regionally concentrated (KZN Zulu, E. Cape Xhosa, Gauteng mixed) and
  English covers the learner market. zul (27M) is the promotion watch.
- sna (Zimbabwean diaspora) is the only immigrant entry — the
  Portuguese- (Mozambican), French- (Congolese) and Chewa-speaking
  communities sit in long-tail pending the immigrant-threshold ruling (§8).
- Khoisan (Nama `naq` spillover, N|uu `ngh` moribund, Khwe `xuu`) is
  entirely long-tail: politically salient (PanSALB Khoi-San recognition
  push), numerically tiny. Confirm no-entry holds in pass 2.
- s.6(5) heritage languages (Arabic, German, Greek, Gujarati, Hebrew, Hindi,
  Portuguese, Sanskrit, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu) correctly have NO entries —
  community/religious use only, English-dominant shift nearly complete.

## 4. Constitutional backbone (what the entries cite)

- 1996 Constitution s.6: 11 official languages (Sepedi, Sesotho, Setswana,
  siSwati, Tshivenda, Xitsonga, Afrikaans, English, isiNdebele, isiXhosa,
  isiZulu) — "parity of esteem", PanSALB oversight.
- 18th Amendment (signed July 2023): SASL (`sfs`) becomes the 12th official
  language — first new official language since 1996; curriculum rollout TBD.
- Use of Official Languages Act 2012: national departments/provinces must
  adopt ≥3 working languages; compliance patchy (English-dominant default).
- Education: LiEP 1997 (11-language medium options) vs early-exit reality;
  BELA Act 2024 (school language-policy sign-off) — implementation TBD §8.

## 5. The cluster structure (officialisation artefact — 12 codes, 4 families)

The 11 spoken official languages compress into 4 groups — intelligibility
is HIGH within groups, which matters for pack design:

- **Nguni** (zul/xho/ssw/nbl): Zulu↔Xhosa partially intelligible; Swazi and
  Southern Ndebele outlying. One "Nguni note" serves all four courses.
- **Sotho-Tswana** (nso/sot/tsn): Tswana↔Southern Sotho highly intelligible;
  Northern Sotho standard (Sepedi) more distant. One "Sotho note" serves all.
- **Tswa-Ronga** (tso): stands alone (Mozambique-facing).
- **Venda** (ven): stands alone (Zimbabwe-facing).
- **Afrikaans/English**: the two colonial officials; Afrikaans-university
  retreat (SU/UP language-policy shifts) is the trend to watch.

## 6. Code treatments specific to South Africa

- **Northern Sotho standard**: `nso` entry = Sepedi-based standard;
  Lobedu (`lob`, long-tail) + Sepulana claims contest the standard's
  legitimacy — noted, standard holds for pass 1.
- **Ndebele pair**: ZA `nbl` (Southern) vs ZW `nde` (Northern/S Zimbabwe) —
  distinct codes, Nguni sisters; separate entries, cross-noted.
- **Swazi**: `ssw` (ZAJe/SZ same code) — ZA entry + SZ national, one pack.
- **Tsonga**: `tso` (ZA/MZ same code) — one pack, Mozambique notes fold in.
- **Shona immigrant**: `sna` roles immigrant+widely-spoken (Gauteng/W. Cape
  diaspora); figure 1M is a FLOOR (undocumented undercount) — §8.
- **SASL**: `sfs` roles official+sign (review-documented, no mixing) —
  provincial dialect spread (W. Cape vs Gauteng signing) TBD pass 2.
