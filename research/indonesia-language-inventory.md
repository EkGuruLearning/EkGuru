# Indonesia Language Inventory — Deep Audit (Phase 6)

Batch: southeast-asia (pass 1) · Status: PRELIMINARY · Date: 2026-09-14

Indonesia is the world's second-most linguistically diverse country (≈700
living languages) and the great success story of a constructed national
language: Indonesian (ind) went from colonial-administration Malay to 210M
speakers in a century. This file is the Phase 6 deep audit: the constitutional
backbone (1945 Constitution Art.36 + Youth Pledge 1928), the Javanese paradox
(largest L1 in decline), and the code-boundary rulings pass 2 must make.

## 4. Constitutional backbone (what the entries cite)

- 1945 Constitution Art.36: Indonesian is the state language; regional
  languages "respected and maintained" (Art.32(2) post-amendment).
- Youth Pledge 1928 (Sumpah Pemuda): "one motherland, one nation, one
  language — Indonesian" — the political charter of ind's rise.
- 2009 Language Law (UU 24/2009): Indonesian mandatory in education,
  administration, contracts; regional languages protected in their areas;
  foreign languages restricted (signage, employment).
- Aceh special autonomy (2005): Acehnese + Arabic co-used in religious
  education; only province with formal Sharia-school language policy.

## 5. The Javanese paradox (transmission artefact — do not take L1 at face value)

Javanese (jav, 90M L1) is simultaneously the largest mother tongue in the
country AND in measurable intergenerational decline: urban Central/East Java
parents increasingly raise children in Indonesian, and Krama (high speech
level) competence is collapsing faster than Ngoko. Consequences:

- `jav` band uses the 90M L1 with a confidence penalty + decline note;
  learner Javanese = Solo standard, Ngoko-first pedagogy with Krama
  recognition modules (Phase 10 variation note).
- Speech levels (Ngoko/Madya/Krama + honorific vocab) are a course-design
  constraint shared with Balinese (`ban`, 3 levels) and Sundanese (`sun`,
  undak-usuk) — one pedagogy pattern serves all three.
- Script heritage (Javanese/Sundanese/Balinese/Batak/Lontara/Jawi) is
  cultural-literacy scope, NOT learner-script scope: all courses Latin-first.

## 6. Code treatments specific to Indonesia

- **Malay macro**: `msa` (macro; members incl. `ind`, `zsm`) — ID entry uses
  individual `ind`; never cite `msa` for Indonesian (build bulk-note batch-4).
- **Minangkabau/Banjar**: `min` + `bjn` are Malayic individuals, NOT
  Indonesian dialects — separate entries, cross-noted with ind.
- **Batak peers**: NO Batak macro exists — `bbc` (Toba) entry + sisters
  `btx/bts/akb/btm/btd` are peer codes (build bulk-note batch-4); sisters
  sit in notesLongTail pending threshold ruling.
- **Buginese/Makassar**: `bug` + `mak` (South Sulawesi; Lontara script) —
  distinct codes, separate entries.
- **Acehnese**: `ace` (Chamic — closer to Vietnamese Cham than to Malay!) —
  family field must say Austronesian: Chamic, never Malayic.
- **Hakka**: `hak` (Sinitic) — entry covers Pontianak/Singkawang; Medan
  Hokkien gap flagged (see §3).
- **BISINDO vs SIBI**: `inl` entry = BISINDO (natural community signing);
  SIBI (government Signed-Indonesian system) is a manual code, not a
  language — noted, never entered.
