Why Punjabi is not just "Hindi in different letters"

The two scripts look unrelated because they are. Gurmukhi and Devanagari are separate writing systems, and the same name can come out noticeably different in each — longer, shorter, or with letters you did not expect. Three habits of Punjabi spelling explain almost every surprise:

1. Punjabi does not stack consonants

Hindi piles consonants on top of each other (क्ष, त्र, श्र). Punjabi mostly refuses to. Where Hindi squeezes two letters into one shape, Punjabi writes them side by side and speaks the vowel in between. That is why Arshdeep is ਅਰਸ਼ਦੀਪ and not a stack of four letters, and why Punjabi text often looks longer than the Hindi for the same word.

2. The nasal sound has two forms

Hindi has one nasal mark, the anusvara ं, and uses it everywhere. Punjabi has two and picks by position:

  • ੰ tippi — a dot inside the word, before a consonant.
  • ਂ bindi — a dot at the end of a word.

So Simran keeps a full m in ਸਿਮਰਨ, while Singh is ਸਿੰਘ with tippi. Write the wrong one and it still reads, but it looks foreign to a Punjabi reader immediately.

3. Singh is ਸਿੰਘ — and this is why a name list exists

Take the Hindi सिंह and convert it letter by letter and you get ਸਿਂਹ, which no Punjabi writes. The h in Singh is a gh in Punjabi, and the nasal is tippi because it sits inside the word. No rule applied to individual letters produces that; it is simply the spelling. Surnames are where mechanical transliteration fails hardest, so this page keeps a curated list and says so when it uses it.

Every consonant already contains a vowel

Gurmukhi, like Devanagari, is an abugida — not an alphabet. ਕ is not "k", it is "ka". The short a is built in, which is why a name like Raj needs only ਰ + ਜ, not three separate vowels.

A different vowel is added as a mark called a matra: ਕ + ਿ = ਕਿ (ki), ਕ + ੀ = ਕੀ (kee), ਕ + ੋ = ਕੋ (ko). And a bare consonant with no vowel at all needs the built-in one removed with a stroke called a halant: ਕ੍.

Questions people ask

Why is my name spelled differently from the Hindi version?

Because the scripts have different spelling habits, not because one is wrong. Punjabi avoids stacked consonants and uses tippi for a nasal inside a word; Hindi uses conjuncts and one nasal mark everywhere. The same sounds, written by two different sets of conventions.

Is Singh written ਸਿੰਘ or ਸਿਂਹ?

ਸਿੰਘ. A letter-by-letter conversion of सिंह produces ਸਿਂਹ, which no Punjabi writes. The h is a gh in Punjabi, and the nasal is tippi because it is inside the word. This is exactly the case the curated list exists for.

Do Punjabi names use the same letters as Hindi?

No. Gurmukhi is a separate script with its own letters. Hindi and Punjabi share most vocabulary and grammar, but Devanagari cannot be read as Gurmukhi and Gurmukhi cannot be read as Devanagari. If you want the Hindi version, the Hindi tool is the one to use.

Is there one official spelling of my name?

No. Personal names have no official spelling in any script, and Punjabi families spell the same name two or three ways. What matters is that a Punjabi speaker reading it aloud produces your name. Where a second spelling is common, the curated list keeps it.

Can I write a full name, first and last?

Yes. Type both words. Each word is converted on its own and the space is kept, which is correct in Punjabi too.

Does this work for English names?

Yes, by sound. John is ਜੌਨ and Sarah is ਸਾਰਾ. It is weakest on sounds Punjabi does not have at all — the French u, or the Arabic ع — and it will tell you when the conversion was partial.

Want to read it, not just copy it?

Gurmukhi has 35 consonants and 10 vowel signs, and learning to read them takes about two weeks. A tutor fixes your pronunciation in a way no app can. Find a Punjabi or Hindi tutor — or start free with the guides.
