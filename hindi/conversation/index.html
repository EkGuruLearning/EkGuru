💬Conversation बातचीतChapterन म त

Actually talking to someone

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

What a real conversation needs that a phrasebook cannot give you, and how to survive your first one.

A phrasebook prepares you for the half of a conversation you say. It does nothing for the half that comes back, which arrives fast, in an accent, from someone who is not being careful for you.

The skill that makes conversation possible is not vocabulary. It is the ability to keep going while not understanding. Four sentences do most of that work, and they should be automatic before your first real conversation: मुझे समझ नहीं आया (I did not understand), ज़रा धीरे बोलिए (please speak slowly), फिर से कहिए (say it again), and इसे हिंदी में क्या कहते हैं? (how do you say this in Hindi?). Say them in Hindi. The moment you apologise in English, the conversation switches to English and does not switch back.

The second skill is having something to say. Prepare sixty seconds about yourself — where you are from, what you do, why you are learning — and rehearse it until it comes out without assembly. Then prepare questions, because a conversation you cannot continue is a monologue.

Expect to be answered in English, particularly if you look foreign. It is not rudeness; it is helpfulness. Saying मैं हिंदी सीख रहा हूँ, हिंदी में बोलिए usually works.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.
