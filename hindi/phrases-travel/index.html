The Hindi that actually helps on a trip

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Autos, trains, bargaining and the phrases that change how you are treated.

A traveller needs a small, specific set of Hindi, learned to the point of reflex rather than recognition. Recognition is useless at a ticket counter.

The core is: यह कितने का है (how much is this), … कहाँ है (where is …), मुझे … जाना है (I want to go to …), and the numbers to a hundred so you can catch the answer. Everything else can be pointed at.

For autos and taxis, मीटर से चलिए — go by the meter — said first and calmly, changes the negotiation before it starts. सीधे, बाएँ, दाएँ handle directions. यहीं रोकिए stops the vehicle.

For bargaining, थोड़ा कम कीजिए (make it a bit less) is the polite standard opening and बहुत महँगा है (that is very expensive) the standard response to a first price. Bargaining is expected in markets and not in shops with printed prices; doing it in the wrong place is awkward rather than offensive.

The largest single effect is not any phrase. It is attempting Hindi at all. A visitor who says नमस्ते and तीन चाय instead of pointing is treated measurably differently, and the effort registers long before the grammar is right.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.
