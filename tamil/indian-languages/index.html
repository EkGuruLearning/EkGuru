Tamil

Tamil among Indian languages

Dravidian roots, Sanskrit friends, Hindi neighbours — Tamil's family place.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Tamil spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Tamil anchors the Dravidian south: தெலுங்கு (Telugu), கன்னடம் (Kannada), மலையாளம் (Malayalam) — siblings, not dialects. Shared grammar bones, shared ancient words, four proud literatures. Learn Tamil and Kannada signboards start whispering.

The north speaks Indo-Aryan: இந்தி (Hindi), வங்காளம் (Bengali), மராத்தி (Marathi), குஜராத்தி (Gujarati), பஞ்சாபி (Punjabi) — Sanskrit-descended cousins. Different family, shared nation: இந்தியா (India) holds both, and மொழிபெயர்ப்பு (translation) bridges all.

Sanskrit threads everything: சமஸ்கிருதம் lent Tamil thousands of formal words — மொழி itself debates origins endlessly. Pure-Tamil movements coin Dravidian replacements; daily speech mixes happily. Both registers are real Tamil; learners need both.

Unity crowns diversity: ஒற்றுமை (unity) — 22 official languages, hundreds of mothers tongues, one conversation. Tamils learn Hindi for Delhi, English for globe, Tamil for soul. Every Indian language you meet makes your Tamil richer.

Questions learners ask

Is Tamil related to Hindi?

Dravidian — Tamil, தெலுங்கு, கன்னடம், மலையாளம் share ancient roots. Hindi, Bengali and Marathi are Indo-Aryan cousins-by-neighbourhood, not siblings.

Which language is closest to Tamil?

மலையாளம் — the closest sibling, split ~1000 years ago. Shared words everywhere, shared script origins, mutual smiles. The tamil-vs-malayalam page maps both.

Why so much Sanskrit in Tamil?

Centuries of poetry, prayer and scholarship — சமஸ்கிருதம் words fill formal Tamil while grammar stays purely Dravidian. Sanskrit lends vocabulary; Tamil keeps its soul.
