Formal and informal Tamil

நீங்கள் or நீ? வாருங்கள் or வா? Respect levels, decoded.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Tamil spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Tamil has two yous: நீங்கள் (formal/plural — strangers, elders, officials) and நீ (familiar — friends, children, family). English erased this distinction centuries ago; Tamil keeps it razor-sharp. Every conversation opens with an invisible choice — make it consciously, and make it respectful.

Verbs mirror the split: வாருங்கள் (please come) versus வா (come!), சொல்லுங்கள் (please say) versus சொல் (say!), போங்கள் (please go) versus போ (go!). The -ங்கள் ending is pure respect — same verb, different universe. Stem-alone orders bark; -ங்கள் invites.

Giving and forgiving show the range: தாருங்கள் (please give) versus கொடு (give!), மன்னியுங்கள் (please forgive) versus மன்னித்துவிடு (forgive, familiar). Beggars and kings both hear தாருங்கள் — politeness in Tamil costs nothing and buys everything.

Strangers get titles: ஐயா (sir), அம்மா (madam/mother), அண்ணா (brother), அக்கா (sister). ஐயா, உதவி செய்யுங்கள் (sir, please help) opens any door; barking orders at strangers closes them. Tamil warmth is earned with two syllables of respect.

Questions learners ask

When do I use நீங்கள்?

நீங்கள் for every adult you don't know well — shopkeepers, officials, elders, strangers. நீ waits for close friends, children and family. Wrong-side errors embarrass; learners are forgiven, but respect first is always safest.

How do I make a command polite?

Add -ங்கள்: வா becomes வாருங்கள், போ becomes போங்கள், சொல் becomes சொல்லுங்கள். One ending, instant politeness — the highest-value suffix in Tamil.

How do I address strangers?

ஐயா (aiyaa — sir) and அம்மா (ammaa — madam) address strangers respectfully — ஐயா, நேரம் என்ன? (sir, what time is it?). Better than pointing and shouting in any language.
