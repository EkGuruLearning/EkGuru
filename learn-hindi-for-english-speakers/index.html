Learn Hindi for English speakers

Your first language decides which parts of Hindi are easy and which are slow, and almost no course takes account of it. This page does. It is written for someone who already speaks English and wants to know what that is worth before spending a year finding out.

Where the verb goes

English is subject-verb-object: I eat rice. Hindi is subject-object-verb: मैं चावल खाता हूँ — literally I rice eat am. This is the single biggest adjustment, and it is not a matter of translating and then shuffling. Fluency arrives when you stop composing the English sentence first.

What English gives you for free

A very large shared vocabulary, in both directions. Hindi has absorbed hundreds of English words outright — स्टेशन, डॉक्टर, स्कूल, मोबाइल — and English took shampoo, pyjamas, bungalow, loot, jungle and thug from Hindi. You also have Roman-script transliteration available everywhere, which means you can start speaking before you can read.

What it does not prepare you for

English uses aspiration constantly and never contrastively: the p in pin is aspirated and the p in spin is not, and no English speaker notices, because swapping them never changes a word. In Hindi it always does. प and फ are different letters. Your ear has spent a lifetime filing that difference under 'irrelevant' and it takes deliberate work to un-file it.

Sounds to work on

WhatWhy it matters
The dental t and dHindi त and द are made with the tongue against the upper teeth, like Spanish or Italian. English t and d sit further back on the ridge. Say them the English way and a Hindi speaker hears ट and ड — a different letter, often a different word.
The retroflex seriesट ठ ड ढ ण are made with the tongue curled back towards the roof of the mouth. There is no English equivalent, and this is the sound that makes an accent identifiably foreign.
Aspiration as meaningक/ख, प/फ, त/थ, च/छ, ट/ठ. Five pairs. Hold a sheet of paper in front of your mouth: it should move for the second of each pair and not the first.
No schwaEnglish reduces unstressed vowels to an 'uh'. Hindi does not. Every vowel keeps its full value, so नमस्ते is na-mas-te, not nuh-mus-tuh.

Grammar, compared with English

FeatureHow it compares
Postpositions, not prepositionsEnglish puts it before the noun — to the house. Hindi puts it after — घर को, house-to. Mechanically simple, but it takes weeks before it comes out that way without thinking.
Grammatical genderEvery Hindi noun is masculine or feminine, including objects and abstractions, and the adjective AND the verb agree with it. English has none of this. There is no shortcut: gender is learned with the noun, from day one, or relearned painfully later.
The verb agrees with youमैं बोलता हूँ if you are male, मैं बोलती हूँ if you are female. Your own gender is in your verbs, every sentence.
No 'do' supportEnglish builds questions with do: do you speak Hindi? Hindi puts क्या at the front and leaves the sentence alone. Simpler, and English speakers persistently over-complicate it.

A word worth knowing about

अच्छा (achchha)

Looks like: good. In practice: it is also 'okay', 'oh really?', 'I see', and — with the right rising tone — open disbelief. Learners use it as 'good' and miss three-quarters of what it is doing.

A realistic first thirty days

  1. Days 1–5: sound before script. Drill the five aspirated pairs and the dental/retroflex contrast with a person who will correct you. Nothing else matters as much and nothing else is as easy to skip.
  2. Days 6–12: the alphabet. It is phonetic and logically ordered by where in the mouth each sound is made — far more rational than English spelling, and most people read simple words within two weeks.
  3. Days 13–20: sentence shape. Verb last, postpositions after. Build twenty sentences of your own; reading about the rule does almost nothing.
  4. Days 21–30: gender and the present tense together, because in Hindi they are the same problem. Learn every new noun with its gender attached, never separately.

Notice that the order is not the order a general course would use. That is the whole point of organising this by your first language: the sequence that works depends on what you already have.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.

Questions English speakers ask

Is Hindi hard for English speakers?

It depends entirely on what you are comparing. A very large shared vocabulary, in both directions. Hindi has absorbed hundreds of English words outright — स्टेशन, डॉक्टर, स्कूल, मोबाइल — and English took shampoo, pyjamas, bungalow, loot, jungle and thug from Hindi. You also have Roman-script transliteration available everywhere, which means you can start speaking before you can read.

What is the hardest part of Hindi for a English speaker?

English uses aspiration constantly and never contrastively: the p in pin is aspirated and the p in spin is not, and no English speaker notices, because swapping them never changes a word. In Hindi it always does. प and फ are different letters. Your ear has spent a lifetime filing that difference under 'irrelevant' and it takes deliberate work to un-file it.

Do English speakers need to learn Devanagari?

Not to speak. Many learners work in romanised Hindi for months. If you want to read signs, messages or anything written, the script is phonetic and consistent, and most learners read simple words within a few weeks.

How long does Hindi take from English?

Nobody can give you an honest number, because it depends on hours per week far more than on your first language. What this page can tell you is which parts will be quick for you and which will be slow, which is more useful than a figure.

The part you cannot do alone

Everything above can be read. The sound work in particular cannot be self-assessed — you cannot hear your own errors, which is precisely why they fossilise. A tutor is the only mechanism that fixes that, and an hour a week is enough.
