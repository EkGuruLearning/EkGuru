Devanagari digits — ० to ९

Marathi writes its numerals in Devanagari, and they behave exactly like the digits you already know: they combine left to right, so ४९ is 49 and १०० is 100. Ten symbols, no new idea. You will see these digits in Marathi textbooks, money, dates and road signs as much as the ordinary 0–9, so reading them is worth the few minutes it takes.

Bigger numbers

Marathi uses the lakh and crore system, the same as Hindi — not the thousand-million system English uses. Note that 100 is शंभर, not हज़ार; that word belongs to 1000.

Why the list looks random, and the four things that make it stick

1. Every decade has its own ending

Learn the ending once and ten numbers come almost free. The twenties end in -vees (एकवीस, बावीस), the thirties in -tees, the forties in -chalis (एक्केचाळीस, बेचाळीस), the fifties in -vann (एक्कावन्न, बावन्न), the sixties in -asht (एकसष्ठ, बासष्ठ), the seventies in -hattar, the eighties in -ainshi, the nineties in -annav.

2. The unit is usually the first sound

Numbers with a one start ek- (एकवीस, एकसष्ठ), with a two start ba- (बावीस, बासष्ठ), with a three tre-, with a five panch-. So पंच्याहत्तर decodes itself: panch (five) + -ahattar (seventies) = 75.

3. The five nines are subtraction, not addition

This is the one that catches everybody, and it is the biggest difference from English. Marathi builds 49, 59, 69, 79 and 89 as "one short of the ten above": 49 is एकोणपन्नास — literally "one short of fifty", not "forty-nine". Every one of them starts with एकोण (ekon, "one short"). Once you know that, five numbers stop being exceptions and become a rule.

4. 99 is the one that breaks the rule

99 does not follow the subtraction pattern. It is नव्व्याण्णव (navvyaannav), "ninety-nine" built straight. It is the only nine in the last two decades that does not start with ekon, and it is the one learners trip over precisely because the other nines did.

Questions people ask

How do you count to 10 in Marathi?

1 एक (ek), 2 दोन (don), 3 तीन (teen), 4 चार (chaar), 5 पाच (paach), 6 सहा (sahaa), 7 सात (saat), 8 आठ (aath), 9 नऊ (nau), 10 दहा (dahaa).

Are Marathi numbers the same as Hindi numbers?

Almost, not quite. They share the Devanagari numerals and the first ten words. After that Marathi has its own forms: 40 is चाळीस, 60 is साठ, 80 is ऐंशी, and 100 is शंभर. The differences are consistent, not random, so a Hindi speaker learning Marathi counts can reuse half the list and learn the rest in one sitting.

Is 99 नव्व्याण्णव or एकोणशंभर?

नव्व्याण्णव is the everyday form in every published chart. एकोणशंभर is a literal reading, "one short of a hundred", and it is correct but far less common. The table uses the everyday form first.

Why do 49, 59, 69 and 79 look like the next ten?

Because Marathi builds them from the ten above. 49 is एकोणपन्नास, "one short of fifty". The एकोण prefix does the subtraction for you — once you spot it, five numbers become one rule instead of five exceptions.

Do I need the Devanagari digits to read Marathi?

They are the same ten digits Hindi uses, and you already know how they combine, so there is nothing new to learn. Marathi textbooks, money, dates and road signs print them constantly. Ten symbols, and you are reading any number in Marathi immediately.

Learn the script these numbers are written in

Marathi is written in Devanagari, the same script as Hindi, and numbers are the easiest place to start because there are only a hundred of them and you already know what they mean. A tutor will fix your pronunciation of the tricky nines before it sets — find a Marathi or Hindi tutor.
