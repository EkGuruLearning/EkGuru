Shopping and bargaining

قیمت, رعایت, رنگ — shop in Urdu from first price to final deal.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Prices open every transaction: اس کی قیمت کیا ہے؟ (what's this one's price?), کتنے روپے؟ (how many rupees?). Listen for the number, repeat it back — پچاس؟ (fifty?) — and you are already shopping in Urdu.

Bargaining follows the friendly script: بہت مہنگا ہے (too costly), رعایت دیجیے (give a discount), تھوڑا کم کیجیے (reduce a little). Start walking away slowly — اچھا، دیکھتا ہوں (okay, I'll look around) — and the real price often chases you down the street.

Sizes and colours refine the hunt: بڑا دیجیے (give the big one), چھوٹا دیجیے (give the small one), نیا دکھائیے (show a new one), اور رنگ (another colour). Point, name the change, nod — shopkeepers read gestures fluently and reward attempts instantly.

Colours close the sale: لال (red), نیلا (blue), ہرا (green), پیلا (yellow), سفید (white), کالا (black). لال خوب لگتا ہے (the red looks nice) — then لوں گا (I'll take it). Deal done, in Urdu, with a smile on both sides.

A note on تھوڑا کم کیجیے. تھوڑا کم کیجیے (thodaa kam keejiye — please reduce a little) is the magic sentence of every bazaar. Said smiling, it opens every negotiation; said with لوں گا (I'll take it) right after, it closes most. Bargaining respects the dance, never the demand.

Questions learners ask

How do I ask the price?

اس کی قیمت کیا ہے؟ (what's the price of this?) — then بس کتنے؟ (just how much?) for everything else. Two questions, every shop.

How do I bargain politely?

بہت مہنگا ہے (too costly) with a smile, then تھوڑا کم کیجیے (reduce a little). Bargaining is friendly theatre — play your part warmly and the price moves.

Which colours first?

Start with five: لال (red), نیلا (blue), ہرا (green), پیلا (yellow), سفید (white). اور رنگ دکھائیے (show another colour) reveals the rest.
