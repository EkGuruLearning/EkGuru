Saying hello the Urdu way

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

السلام علیکم and beyond — greet with warmth from your first word.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Greetings open every door: السلام علیکم (respectful hello), آداب (courteous hello), ہیلو (casual hi). Hand on heart, say السلام علیکم, and doors open — bazaars, offices, homes. One phrase, infinite welcome.

Health-questions show you care: آپ کیسے ہیں؟ (how are you, formal), میں ٹھیک ہوں (I'm fine), اور آپ؟ (and you?). Urdu small talk runs on genuine enquiry — ask properly, listen properly, belong quickly.

Time splits the day: صبح بخیر (good morning), شب بخیر (good night). Morning greetings before noon, شب بخیر only at parting — timing your بخیر shows cultural fluency.

Partings promise return: خدا حافظ (goodbye), پھر ملیں گے (we'll meet again), اپنا خیال رکھیے (take care). Rarely goodbye without a next meeting — پھر ملیں گے assumes friendship continues. Say it warmly; mean it always.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on السلام علیکم. This greeting is widely used in Muslim and Urdu-speaking contexts. A conventional response is وعلیکم السلام. Usage and accompanying gestures vary by person and setting.

Transfer practice. Choose a relationship and setting before you speak: a greeting to a close friend need not be the greeting you choose for an elder or a first meeting. Practise opening and replying, then listen for the form your conversation partner uses. Mirror it politely instead of assuming one greeting fits every community. Start from السلام علیکم (assalam-o-alaikum) and decide whether it fits the situation before using it elsewhere.

Context check. For Saying hello the Urdu way, “You are welcome” belongs to a particular setting. A common mistake is to treat an English gloss as universal; read the language-specific note above and ask a fluent speaker when local usage differs.

Questions learners ask

How do I say hello respectfully?

السلام علیکم with hand on heart — respectful everywhere. Casual ہیلو works with friends; السلام علیکم never fails.

How do I ask how someone is?

آپ کیسے ہیں؟ (formal) or تم کیسے ہو؟ (informal) — and answer میں ٹھیک ہوں، شکریہ (I'm fine, thanks). Health-questions open hearts.

What are the time greetings?

صبح بخیر (good morning), شب بخیر (good night), آداب (respectful greeting). Time-greetings show care; use them generously.
