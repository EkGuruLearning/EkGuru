Food phrases

بریانی to کھیر — order, eat and praise food in Urdu.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Meals structure the day: ناشتہ (breakfast), کھانا (lunch/dinner), چائے (tea). چائے پییں گے؟ (shall we have tea?) — life runs on doodh-patti and conversation. Accept every cup; refuse nothing twice.

Ordering is easy bravery: مجھے ایک چائے چاہیے (I want one tea), بل دیجیے (please give the bill). ایک پلیٹ بریانی دیجیے (give one plate of biryani) — order the soul of the cuisine in Urdu. Dhabas reward attempts with extra raita.

Staples fill the dastarkhwan: چاول (rice), روٹی (flatbread), دال (lentils), سبزی (vegetables). سبزی کیا ہے؟ (what's the vegetable dish?) — ask daily, eat variously. Shared eating teaches vocabulary with every bite.

Tastes tune the tongue: تیز (spicy), میٹھا (sweet) — کھیر (rice pudding) crowns feasts. کم تیز کیجیے (make it less spicy) — then بہت خوب! (very nice!). Praise the cook; second helpings follow.

A note on دسترخوان. دسترخوان (dastarkhwan — the spread meal-cloth) is hospitality itself — کھانا تیار ہے (food is ready) gathers everyone. مہمان نوازی (hospitality) is sacred; guests eat first, hosts eat joy. Food is love, served hot.

Questions learners ask

How do I talk about meals?

کھانا (meal), ناشتہ (breakfast), چائے (tea). مجھے بھوک لگی ہے (I'm hungry) opens every food conversation.

How do I order food?

یہ کیا ہے؟ (what is this?) and مجھے بریانی چاہیے (I want biryani). Point, ask, order — menus fear the curious.

How do I describe tastes?

تیز (spicy), میٹھا (sweet), نمکین (salty), کھٹا (sour). کم تیز (less spicy) saves sensitive tongues.
