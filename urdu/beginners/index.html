Urdu for absolute beginners

Zero to first conversation — your first week of Urdu, step by step.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Day one speaks: السلام علیکم (hello), ہاں (yes), نہیں (no) — say them to mirrors, pets, shopkeepers. Pronunciation need not be perfect; attempts earn smiles and corrections. Speaking starts speaking; nothing else does.

Days two to four add survival: شکریہ (thanks), معاف کیجیے (sorry), پانی (water), کتنے؟ (how much?). Four days, seven words, infinite transactions. Survival Urdu fits in a pocket and works everywhere.

Days five to seven build sentences: میں استاد ہوں (I am a teacher), مجھے چائے چاہیے (I want tea), آپ کیسے ہیں؟ (how are you?). Frames plus words equal conversation — slots fill fast when frames stand ready.

Week two opens the script: ا ب پ, then ج چ ح خ — Urdu script through familiar words. Read what you already say; letters become friends, not walls. Speak-first learners read faster — motivation decodes.

A note on آغاز. آغاز (aaghaaz — beginning) is the bravest word in Urdu — آج سے آغاز (starting from today). Every fluent speaker once said their first broken السلام علیکم. Begin badly; continue daily; arrive surely.

Questions learners ask

What are my first seven words?

السلام علیکم, ہاں, نہیں, شکریہ, معاف کیجیے, ٹھیک ہے, میں — seven words that open every door. Speak them from day one.

Script first or speech first?

No — speak first in Roman letters, add Urdu script in week two. Speakers stay motivated; script-drillers quit. Talk today, read tomorrow.

How much daily practice?

Twenty focused minutes: five words, one dialogue, one song line. Daily beats marathon — streaks compound into fluency.
