Urdu alphabet

ا ب پ to ی ے — the Perso-Arabic alphabet, right-to-left, made friendly.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Vowels anchor everything: ا آ (a/aa), ع (i/e carrier), ے (e), و (o/u carrier). Long-short pairs matter — دل (heart) vs دیل differs by dots and shape. Chant letters in order daily; the mouth memorises what the ear repeats.

Consonants march in families: ب پ ت ٹ (lips-teeth), ج چ ح خ (throat-palate), د ڈ ذ ر ڑ (tongue-tip), س ش ص ز (hissers). Family by family, feel where each sound lives — Urdu pronunciation is geography you can touch.

Special letters crown the set: ع غ ق (deep throat), خ (guttural kh), ژ (Persian zh), ں (nasal noon). خبر (news) shows خ in action; عشق (love) shows ع. Special letters, everyday words.

Joining grids it all: ب + ا = با, م + ی + ں = میں — letters melt into words. Write joining drills daily; reading unlocks when hands know the shapes. Script becomes a friend for life.

A note on right-to-left. Urdu flows right-to-left — start at the right edge and glide leftwards. ا ب پ ت reads a-ba-pa-ta. Eyes adjust within days; soon left-to-right scripts feel backwards.

Questions learners ask

How many Urdu letters?

39 letters — Arabic's set plus پ چ ٹ ڈ ڑ ژ ں ے and more. Same right-to-left flow, extra Indo-Persian sounds.

Do letters join up?

Almost all letters join within words — initial, middle, final shapes. Practice joining early; reading flows from writing.

Which letters first?

The vowel-bearers ا آ ع plus ب پ ت ٹ — then ج چ ح خ. Sounds before shapes; reading follows speaking.
