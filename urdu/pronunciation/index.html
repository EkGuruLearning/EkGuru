Urdu pronunciation

ع غ ق خ, retroflexes, poetic rhythm — speak Urdu like a native.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

The deep quartet rules: عشق (love), غم (sorrow), قلم (pen), خبر (news) — throat engaged, sound resonant. Minimal pairs drill them: کال (famine) vs قال (he said) — practise قلم vs کلم daily. Master these and Urdu voices beam.

Retroflexes flap boldly: ٹماٹر (tomato), ڈاک (post), پڑھنا (to read) — tongue back, tap forward. Urdu retroflexes hit harder than Hindi's; lean into them. Bold flaps mark the insider.

Length carries meaning: short and long vowels are different letters, different words. Double the long ones fully; clip the short ones crisp. Ears measure; mouths must comply.

Rhythm ties it together: آہستہ (slowly) first, then natural speed; زور سے (loudly) for clarity, خاموش (quiet) for calm. تلفظ (pronunciation) improves with echoing — shadow newsreaders daily, record yourself weekly.

A note on آہستہ. آہستہ (aahistaa — slowly) is the learner's best friend — آہستہ بولیے (please speak slowly). Say it with a smile and every Urdu speaker shifts to teacher-mode. Slow speech is kind speech; ask for it freely.

Questions learners ask

How do I pronounce ع غ ق خ?

Deep-throat sounds: ع (voiced squeeze), غ (gargled g), ق (deep k), خ (guttural kh). Listen to newsreaders; imitate slowly. Months of music in four letters.

What about retroflexes?

ٹ ڈ ڑ — tongue curls to the roof. ٹماٹر (tomato), ڈاک (post), پڑھنا (to read). Flap them boldly; Urdu retroflexes are proud.

Do vowel lengths matter?

Long-short pairs change words. Hold long vowels fully double — Urdu ears measure length precisely.
