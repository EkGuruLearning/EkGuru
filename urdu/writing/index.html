Writing Urdu

Letters to lists to letters — writing from first strokes to full pages.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Writing starts with flow: Urdu letters join and glide — لکھیے (please write) curves and dots before full words. Trace ا ب پ م ن big and slow; the hand learns shapes the eye already knows.

Tools matter little, mileage matters all: قلم (pen), pencil, finger on steamed mirrors — children trace in notebooks, slates and air. Write everywhere; medium is vanity, motion is learning.

Lists graduate the hand: فہرست (list) — نوٹ (notes), پتہ (addresses), تاریخ (dates). Copy your own نام (name) fifty times, then friends' names, then street names. Familiar words, fresh script: the fastest fluent hand.

Real writing arrives: خط (letters), درخواست (applications), امتحان (exams), گھر کا کام (homework). Write a postcard to yourself in Urdu — short, joyful, posted. First posted Urdu is never forgotten.

Questions learners ask

How do I start writing Urdu?

Flowing strokes first: Urdu letters join right-to-left — ا، ب، م. Trace big, shrink slowly; لکھائی (handwriting) beautifies with mileage.

What should I write first?

فہرست (lists): shopping lists, word lists, daily plans. Lists forgive grammar, demand spelling, and get used — the perfect beginner writing.

When real writing?

خط (letters) to friends, نوٹ (notes) to self, گھر کا کام (homework) for courses. Real writing to real readers beats copied exercises every time.
