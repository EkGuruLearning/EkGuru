Urdu for work and business

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Meetings, customers, contracts — professional Urdu for offices.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Workplaces run on roles: نوکری (job), دفتر (office), باس (boss), گاہک (customer), ساتھی (colleague). میری نوکری (my job is) introduces your profession; دفتر کہاں ہے؟ finds any office. Roles first, tasks second.

Meetings move business: میٹنگ (meeting), معاہدہ (contract/deal), دستخط (signature). میٹنگ کل صبح (meeting tomorrow morning) — confirm with ٹھیک ہے، آؤں گا (okay, I'll come). Meetings open with السلام علیکم and close with شکریہ.

Money talks plainly: منافع (profit), نقصان (loss), تنخواہ (salary), چھٹی (leave). تنخواہ کب ملتی ہے؟ (when is payday?) — asked smiling, everywhere. Business Urdu respects directness wrapped in politeness.

Digital tools connect all: ای میل (email), فون (phone), پیغام (message). ای میل دیکھیے (please check email) — then کامیابی (success) follows effort. Professional Urdu: formal words, warm heart.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on گاہک. گاہک (gaahak — customer) rules business — گاہک ہی بادشاہ (the customer is king). Master it syllable by syllable; clients notice the effort and reward it.

Questions learners ask

How do I introduce myself at work?

السلام علیکم، میں (hello, I'm) — then آپ سے مل کر خوشی ہوئی (pleased to meet you). Business Urdu opens formal and warms slowly.

How do I ask about meetings?

میٹنگ کب ہے؟ (when is the meeting?) and میٹنگ کہاں ہے؟ (where?). Meetings run offices — never miss one for language reasons.

Email and phone in Urdu?

ای میل بھیجوں گا (I'll send an email) and فون پر بات کریں گے (let's talk on the phone). Digital Urdu borrows freely — clarity beats purity in business.
