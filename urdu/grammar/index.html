Urdu grammar, plainly explained

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Urdu’s two grammatical genders, postpositions, and verb-final order become clearer in short examples than in isolated rule lists.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Two genders shape everything: masculine (لڑکا — boy), feminine (لڑکی — girl). Adjectives pair — اچھا/اچھی — and verbs pair too. Gender is not decoration; it is Urdu's skeleton.

Pronouns simplify persons: میں (I), تم (you-informal), آپ (you-formal), وہ (he-she/they), ہم (we). ہم vs ہم سب (we vs all-of-us) shades meaning; آپ is always plural-form, always respectful. Choose your you wisely.

Postpositions replace prepositions: -کو (to), -نے (by/doer), -سے (with/from), -میں (in). گھر کو جاؤ (go home), چمچ سے کھاؤ (eat with a spoon). Suffixes after nouns — think backwards from English.

Verbs anchor sentences last: میں چاول کھاتا ہوں (I rice eat), وہ اسکول جاتی ہے (she to-school goes). Present (کھاتا ہوں), past (کھایا), future (کھاؤں گا) — tense on verbs, time words framing. Verb-final: the one rule that never breaks.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on نے. نے (ne) marks the doer in past tense — رام نے کیا (Ram did), میں نے کھایا (I ate). Ergative, elegant, everywhere in stories. Tiny suffix, enormous workload.

Questions learners ask

How many genders?

Masculine and feminine — nouns, adjectives AND verbs agree. اچھا/اچھی (good), جاتا/جاتی (go). Pairs everywhere; learn them as sets.

What are postpositions?

کو marks direction (لاہور کو — to Lahore), نے marks doers (رام نے کیا — Ram did), سے means with/from. Postpositions follow nouns — learn each with ten examples.

What is Urdu word order?

Subject-Object-Verb: میں چاول کھاتا ہوں (I rice eat). Verb last, always. English order is the classic beginner error.
