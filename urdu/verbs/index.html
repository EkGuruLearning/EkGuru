Urdu verbs

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

ہے, جانا, کرنا — the verbs that carry every Urdu sentence.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Being grounds all: ہوں/ہے (am/is/are) — میں ہوں (I am), ہم ہیں (we are), آپ ہیں (you are), وہ ہیں (they are). ہے anchors identity, place, state — میں یہاں ہوں (I'm here), میں ٹھیک ہوں (I'm fine). Be first.

Going and coming move life: جانا (to go), آنا (to come) — میں جاتا ہوں (I go), میں آتا ہوں (I come). Direction verbs plus کہاں (where) navigate everywhere — میں لاہور جاتا ہوں (I'm going to Lahore).

Doing and eating fill days: کرنا (to do), کھانا (to eat), پینا (to drink) — میں کام کرتا ہوں (I work), میں کھاتا ہوں (I eat). Daily verbs, daily use; conjugate them in your sleep.

Speaking and knowing crown all: بولنا (to speak), پڑھنا (to read), لکھنا (to write) — میں اردو بولتا ہوں (I speak Urdu). Say it now, imperfectly; say it daily until the mirror agrees.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on ہوں/ہے/ہیں. The ہوں family (hun/hai/hain) is Urdu's heartbeat — میں ہوں (I am), وہ ہے (he/she is), آپ ہیں (you are). Every present-tense sentence pulses with ہ. Learn this family cold; the whole tense follows.

Questions learners ask

How do I say 'I am'?

میں ہوں (I am), تم ہو, وہ ہے, ہم ہیں, آپ ہیں, وہ ہیں. Being first, everything else after.

How does the present tense work?

Present uses root + تا/تی/تے + ہے: میں کرتا ہوں (I do, m), میں کرتی ہوں (f). Gender shows on every present verb.

Which five verbs first?

جانا (to go), آنا (to come), کرنا (to do), کھانا (to eat), بولنا (to speak). Five verbs, half of daily speech.
