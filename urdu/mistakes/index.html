Mistakes every learner makes in Urdu

Verb-first sentences, میں چاہیے, نہیں vs نہ — fix them all.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Mistake one: English word order. میں جا (I go — bare stem, wrong) instead of میں جاتا ہوں (I go — conjugated, right); verbs wandering mid-sentence instead of anchoring the end. Urdu is verb-final, always — SOV is not a suggestion, it is the law.

Mistake two: میں چاہیے (I want — wrong) instead of مجھے چاہیے (to-me wanted — right). Urdu wanting flows toward the wanter; English wanting fires from the wanter. Flip the arrow and dozens of sentences — مجھے سمجھ آیا, مجھے معلوم ہے — unlock at once.

Mistake three: one-size no. نہیں denies verbs — پیسے نہیں ہیں (there's no money); نہ forbids and refuses — چائے نہ (no tea for me). Beginners spray نہ everywhere; natives smile and correct. Learn both no's, use each precisely.

Mistake four: rushing sounds — flat long vowels, weak ع غ ق, wrong gender endings. آہستہ (slowly), صحیح (correctly), پھر (again): the holy trinity of repair. کوشش (effort) plus مشق (practice) plus صبر (patience) — and مبارک ہو, mistakes become fluency.

A note on غلطی. غلطی (ghalati — mistake) is the kindest word in this lesson — میری غلطی (my mistake) disarms every situation. Urdu speakers adore learners who err boldly and laugh warmly; the only true غلطی is silence.

Questions learners ask

What is the most common mistake?

English order with Urdu words: میں جا instead of میں جاتا ہوں, verb first, question words misplaced. Cure: every sentence ends with the verb — practise SOV until English order feels foreign.

Why is میں چاہیے wrong?

Wanting flows toward people: مجھے چاہیے (to-me it-is-wanted), never میں چاہیے. One of Urdu's deepest patterns — experiences come TO you — and beginners fight it for weeks.

نہیں or نہ؟

نہیں denies verbs — پیسے نہیں ہیں (there's no money); نہ forbids — شور نہ کرو (don't make noise). Two no's, two jobs, zero overlap.
