Urdu

Flashcards by topic in Urdu

Spaced repetition, topic decks, daily streaks — flashcards that work.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Cards carry memory: کارڈ (card) — one word, one side; meaning, the other. یادداشت (memory) strengthens with every recall; بھولنا (forgetting) is just unreviewed memory. Build decks per topic — greetings, food, verbs — and review each separately.

Spacing schedules success: وقفہ (interval) — review new cards tomorrow, then in three days, seven, thirty. پھر پھر (again and again), but wider each time. Cramming fills hours; spacing fills years.

Rhythms sustain streaks: روز (daily) quick decks, ہفتہ وار (weekly) full reviews, ماہانہ (monthly) championships. Ten daily minutes beat Sunday marathons; streaks turn study into عادت (habit).

Scores gamify growth: نمبر (marks), مقابلہ (contest), کامیابی (achievement) — even ہار (defeat) teaches. Quiz yourself, beat yesterday's score, celebrate loudly. Flashcards are games that pay in fluency.

A note on دہرائی. دہرائی (dohraai — revision, repeating) is Urdu's own word for spaced review — recalling again, and again, at widening intervals. The language itself teaches the method: revisit, widen, remember.

Questions learners ask

How often should I review?

پھر پھر (again and again) — but spaced: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 30 days. وقفہ (spacing) beats cramming; یادداشت (memory) loves intervals.

How many cards per topic?

Five to ten per topic — quality over quantity. Master one کارڈ deck fully before opening the next; half-known decks rot.

Daily or weekly review?

روز (daily) short sessions — ten minutes, every day, no exceptions. Streaks compound; بھولنا (forgetting) loses to routine.
