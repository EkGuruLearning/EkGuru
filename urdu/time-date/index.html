Time and dates in Urdu

کتنے بجے؟ پیر to اتوار — time, days and dates.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Urdu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Clock time strikes: کتنے بجے؟ (what time?), ایک بجا (one o'clock), پانچ بجے (five o'clock). ساڑھے پانچ (5:30), پونے چھ (5:45), سوا چھ (6:15) — halves and quarters sing. Ask time daily; numbers stick.

Day parts flow: صبح (morning), دوپہر (afternoon), شام (evening), رات (night). صبح کو (in the morning), رات کو (at night) — suffixes time the day. Parts first, precision after.

Weeks cycle planet-wise: پیر (Monday) through اتوار (Sunday). آج پیر ہے (today is Monday) — date yourself daily. Weekdays rhythm life.

Dates frame plans: آج (today), کل (tomorrow), گذشتہ کل (yesterday), تاریخ (date). کل ملیں گے؟ (shall we meet tomorrow?) — propose, confirm, arrive. Plans in Urdu keep in Urdu.

A note on بجے. بجے (baje — has struck) makes time musical — کتنے بجے؟ (how much has struck?), پانچ بجے (five has struck). Clocks strike, bells ring, Urdu asks poetically. Time doesn't pass; it chimes.

Questions learners ask

How do I ask the time?

کتنے بجے؟ (how much has struck?) — answer پانچ بجے (five o'clock). Time strikes in Urdu; ask it everywhere.

Days of the week?

پیر منگل بدھ جمعرات جمعہ ہفتہ اتوار — chant the week; planet-days roll musically.

Today, tomorrow, yesterday?

آج (today), کل (tomorrow), گذشتہ کل (yesterday), پرسوں (day after/before). Four words frame every plan.
