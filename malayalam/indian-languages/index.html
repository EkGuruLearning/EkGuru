Malayalam

Malayalam among Indian languages

Dravidian roots, Sanskrit friends, Hindi neighbours — Malayalam's family place.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Malayalam spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Malayalam anchors the Dravidian coast: തമിഴ് (Tamil), തെലുങ്ക് (Telugu), കന്നഡ (Kannada) — siblings, not dialects. Shared grammar bones, shared ancient words, four proud literatures. Learn Malayalam and Tamil signboards start whispering.

The north speaks Indo-Aryan: ഹിന്ദി (Hindi), ബംഗാളി (Bengali), മറാഠി (Marathi), ഗുജറാത്തി (Gujarati), പഞ്ചാബി (Punjabi) — Sanskrit-descended cousins. Different family, shared nation: ഇന്ത്യ (India) holds both, and വിവർത്തനം (translation) bridges all.

Sanskrit threads everything: സംസ്കൃതം lent Malayalam thousands of formal words — manipravalam wove both for centuries. Daily speech mixes happily. Both registers are real Malayalam; learners need both.

Unity crowns diversity: ഐക്യം (unity) — 22 official languages, hundreds of mother tongues, one conversation. Malayalis learn Hindi for Delhi, English for globe, Malayalam for soul. Every Indian language you meet makes your Malayalam richer.

Questions learners ask

Is Malayalam related to Hindi?

Distant cousins — Malayalam is Dravidian; Hindi, Bengali and Marathi are Indo-Aryan. Shared nation, shared Sanskrit loans, different grandmothers.

Which language is closest to Malayalam?

തമിഴ് — the closest sibling, split ~1000 years ago. Shared words everywhere, shared sounds, mutual smiles. The malayalam-vs-tamil page maps both.

Why so much Sanskrit in Malayalam?

Centuries of poetry, prayer and scholarship — സംസ്കൃതം words fill formal Malayalam while grammar stays purely Dravidian. Sanskrit lends vocabulary; Malayalam keeps its soul.
