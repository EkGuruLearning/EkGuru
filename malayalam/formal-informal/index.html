Formal and informal Malayalam

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

നിങ്ങൾ or നീ? വരൂ or വാ? Respect levels, decoded.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Malayalam spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Malayalam has two yous: നിങ്ങൾ (formal/plural — strangers, elders, officials) and നീ (familiar — friends, children, family). English erased this distinction centuries ago; Malayalam keeps it razor-sharp. Every conversation opens with an invisible choice — make it consciously, and make it respectful.

Verbs mirror the split: വരൂ (please come) versus വാ (come!), പറയൂ (please say) versus പറ (say!), പോകൂ (please go) versus പോ (go!). The -ൂ ending is pure respect — same verb, different universe. Stem-alone orders bark; -ൂ invites.

Giving and forgiving show the range: തരൂ (please give) versus താ (give!), ക്ഷമിക്കണം (please forgive) versus ക്ഷമിക്ക് (forgive, familiar). Beggars and kings both hear തരൂ — politeness in Malayalam costs nothing and buys everything.

Strangers get titles: സാർ (sir), ചേട്ടൻ (brother), ചേച്ചി (sister). സാർ, സഹായിക്കൂ (sir, please help) opens any door; barking orders at strangers closes them. Malayali warmth is earned with two syllables of respect.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on -ൂ. -ൂ (oo) is Malayalam's respect rocket — attach it to any verb and watch warmth ignite: വരൂ (do come), നോക്കൂ (please see), പറയൂ (please tell). One sound that upgrades every request instantly.

Questions learners ask

When do I use നിങ്ങൾ?

നിങ്ങൾ for every adult you don't know well — shopkeepers, officials, elders, strangers. നീ waits for close friends, children and family. Wrong-side errors embarrass; learners are forgiven, but respect first is always safest.

How do I make a command polite?

Add -ൂ: വാ becomes വരൂ, പോ becomes പോകൂ, പറ becomes പറയൂ. One ending, instant politeness — the highest-value suffix in Malayalam.

How do I address strangers?

സാർ (saar — sir) and ചേച്ചി/ചേട്ടൻ for slightly-older strangers. സാർ, സമയം എത്ര? (sir, what time is it?). Better than pointing and shouting in any language.
