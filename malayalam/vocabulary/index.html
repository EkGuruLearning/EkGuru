Core Malayalam vocabulary

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Build a practical Malayalam word set around water, money, time, and today, then retrieve each word in a familiar scene.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Malayalam spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Survival runs on nouns: വെള്ളം (vellam — water), ആഹാരം (aahaaram — food), വീട് (veed — home), പണം (panam — money), സമയം (samayam — time). Five words, and you can ask for water, find home, handle money and ask the time — the entire first day in a new city.

Movement words come next: റോഡ് (road — road), ബസ് (bus — bus), കട (kada — shop), സ്കൂൾ (school — school). Kerala moves by bus and foot; knowing ബസ് സ്റ്റാന്റ് എവിടെ? (where is the bus stand?) saves more time than any app.

Opinions need adjectives: നല്ല (nalla — good), വലിയ (valiya — big), ചെറിയ (cheriya — small), പുതിയ (puthiya — new), പഴയ (pazhaya — old). നല്ലത് (nallath — that's good) approves everything from food to plans — the most useful single opinion in Malayalam.

Weather and mood close the kit: മഴ (mazha — rain) matters half the year, വെയിൽ (veyil — sun/heat) the other half. മഴ പെയ്യുന്നു (rain is coming) starts conversations with strangers; in Kerala, weather talk is friendship's front door.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on ഇന്ന്, നാളെ, ഇന്നലെ. Malayalam time words pivot around today: ഇന്ന് (inn — today), നാളെ (naale — tomorrow), ഇന്നലെ (innale — yesterday). Master this trio and half your daily sentences assemble themselves — നാളെ കാണാം (see you tomorrow) alone is worth the lesson.

Questions learners ask

Which words pay off fastest?

Time words first: ഇന്ന് (today), നാളെ (tomorrow), ഇന്നലെ (yesterday), ഇപ്പോൾ (now). You will use them ten times a day — shopping, planning, chatting — while rare nouns gather dust.

Is mixing English okay?

Malayalam happily absorbs English — ബസ് (bus), ടിക്കറ്റ് (ticket), ഓഫീസ് (office). Use the pure Malayalam word when you know it, the loanword when you don't. Fluency first, purity later.

Nouns or verbs first?

Nouns first, verbs second, adjectives third. With fifty nouns and the twelve verbs from the verbs page, you can point, ask and survive — grammar glues them later.
