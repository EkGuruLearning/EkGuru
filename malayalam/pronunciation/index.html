Malayalam pronunciation

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

ഴ, റ-ര, ള — every tricky sound, one by one, with examples.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Malayalam spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

The two r's split soft and hard: ര is a single flap — മരം (maram — tree), വലിയ (valiya — big) — while റ trills hard — കറുത്ത (karutha — black). English ears merge them; Malayalam meaning doesn't. Practise pairs daily until your tongue files them separately.

The liquid ള curls back: പള്ളി (palli — school), വെള്ളം (vellam — water). Contrast ല (dental, tongue on teeth) with ള (retroflex, curled). Glide la-lla daily; the roof-touch becomes reflex.

Dental versus retroflex stops: ത (teeth — തല, head) against ട (roof — കോട്ട, fort). Hold a finger to your lips: dentals puff forward, retroflexes pop upward. Ten minutes of ത-ട alternation retunes the mouth within a week.

The rest behave kindly: ച is cha at word start (ചായ) softening inside words, ണ marks retroflex n (കണ്ണ് — eye), and long vowels simply hold double — ആ, ഈ, ഊ. Malayalam spelling mirrors speech faithfully: pronounce what you see, and you are right nine times in ten.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on ഴ (zha). Malayalam's signature sound, shared with Tamil: curl the tongue back WITHOUT touching the roof, and voice it — പഴം (fruit), വാഴ (plantain), മഴ (rain). Three everyday words, one legendary sound. Master ഴ and Kerala applauds.

Questions learners ask

What is the difference between ര and റ?

ര is a light flap (like the dd in American cuter); റ is a hard trill (rolled r). മരം (maram — tree) versus കറുത്ത (karutha — black). Practise pairs daily until your tongue files them separately.

How do I make the ള sound?

ള is retroflex — tongue curled back: പള്ളി (school), വെള്ളം (water). ല is dental — tongue on teeth. Say la-la sliding tongue back and feel the shift.

How do I pronounce ഴ?

Curl the tongue back toward the roof WITHOUT touching, and voice it — between r, l and a French j. പഴം, വാഴ, മഴ. Children take years to perfect it, so forgive yourself months — then enjoy the applause.
