Food phrases in Malayalam

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Appam, puttu, sadya — order like a local, from breakfast to bill.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Malayalam spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Breakfast is the glory: അപ്പം (appam — lacy hoppers), പുട്ട് (puttu — steamed cylinders), ഇഡ്ഡലി (iddali — steamed cakes), ദോശ (dosha — crisp crepe), all served with സാമ്പാർ (saambaar) and ചമ്മന്തി (chammanthi — coconut chutney). ചായ കുടിച്ചോ? (have you had tea?) is asked with love — answer honestly, then accept seconds.

Meals centre on ഊണ് (oon — the rice meal): ചോറ് (chor — rice), സാമ്പാർ, രസം (rasam — pepper-tamarind soup), മീൻ കറി (meen kari — fish curry), തൈര് (thayir — curd), പപ്പടം (pappadam — crisp papad). Served on banana leaves at weddings, eaten with the right hand — ഊണ് റെഡിയാണോ? (is the meal ready?) is the happiest question.

Taste words steer every order: എരിവ് (eriv — spicy), മധുരം (madhuram — sweet), പുളി (puli — sour), ഉപ്പ് (upp — salt). കുറച്ച് ഉപ്പ് (a little salt), പഞ്ചസാര വേണ്ട (no sugar) — with taste words, your plate obeys you.

Drinks and bills close the meal: കാപ്പി (coffee), ചായ (tea — the famous bed-tea!), മോര് (mor — buttermilk), പഴച്ചാറ് (pazhachaar — juice). ബില്ല് തരൂ (please give the bill) ends it; രുചിയുണ്ടായിരുന്നു (it was delicious) guarantees a smile on your way out.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on സദ്യ. The Onam sadya — rice, sambar, avial, thoran, pachadi, payasam on a banana leaf — is Kerala on a plate. Eat with the right hand, start from the left, finish with payasam, and understand the state in one feast.

Questions learners ask

How do I order with almost no Malayalam?

Point and say ഇത് ഒന്ന് (ith onn — one of these) — it works in every chayakada, mess and bakery in Kerala. Add ദയവായി (please) and a smile; grammar can wait until dessert.

How do I ask for less spicy food?

എരിവ് കുറച്ച് (eriv kurach — less spicy, please). Kerala food honours chilli and coconut deeply; this one sentence saves sensitive stomachs while keeping every flavour.

How do I praise the food?

രുചിയുണ്ട് (ruchiyund — it's delicious). Say it to the cook, the server, the ammama who packed your lunch — it is the highest compliment in Malayali food culture.
