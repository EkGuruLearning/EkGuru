Malayalam for absolute beginners

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Your first Malayalam week: yes, no, I, you, and sentences that survive anything.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Malayalam spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Week one needs eleven words: നമസ്കാരം (hello), അതെ (yes), ശരി (okay), നന്ദി (thanks), ദയവായി (please), ഞാൻ (I), നിങ്ങൾ (you, formal), ഇത് എന്താണ്? (what is this?), മനസ്സിലായി (I understand), അറിയില്ല (I don't know), പോകാം (let's go). Eleven words, and you can greet, agree, thank, ask, confess ignorance and move — the whole beginner game.

Pronouns branch early: ഞാൻ (I), നിങ്ങൾ (you formal/plural), നീ (you familiar), അവൻ/അവൾ (he/she), അവർ (they formal), ഞങ്ങൾ (we exclusive), നമ്മൾ (we inclusive). Two we's where English has one — നമ്മൾ പോകാം (let's go, you included) is Malayalam's warmest invitation.

Quantity words multiply everything: കുറച്ച് (a little), ഒരുപാട് (a lot), മതി (enough), മാത്രം (only). കുറച്ച് മലയാളം സംസാരിക്കും (I speak a little Malayalam) is the perfect learner's confession — humble, charming, instantly rewarded with help.

Action invitations close week one: ഇവിടെ വരൂ (come here), ഇരിക്കൂ (please sit), കഴിക്കൂ (please eat), പോകാം (let's go). These four run hospitality, the beating heart of Malayali culture — learn them and you stop being a guest and start being family.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on അതെ, ശരി and ആ. Malayalam yes comes in three flavours: അതെ (athe — respectful yes), ശരി (shari — okay/agreed), ആ (aa — casual yeah). Learners live on അതെ and ശരി — respectful, warm, never wrong. Save ആ for friends.

Questions learners ask

What are the first five words?

നമസ്കാരം, അതെ, ശരി, നന്ദി, ദയവായി — five words, infinite mileage. Add ഞാൻ and നിങ്ങൾ and you can introduce yourself to anyone in Kerala.

How much should I study daily?

Fifteen focused minutes beat two distracted hours. One topic page, every speaker tapped, five words reviewed — daily beats heroic.

Must I learn the script first?

No — speak first, script second. Roman letters carry you through months of conversation; the alphabet page waits patiently until curiosity pulls you in.
