Punjabi vs Hindi

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Punjabi and Hindi share Indic vocabulary, but Punjabi has its own sounds and script traditions; compare attested forms rather than assuming they match.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Punjabi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

The cousins grew side by side: ਪੰਜਾਬੀ bloomed in Punjab, हिंदी spread across the north. Same Indo-Aryan mother, sister scripts — Gurmukhi rounded and open while Devanagari ruled with its headline. History in every sentence.

Shared words delight learners: ਘਰ/घर (house), ਪਾਣੀ/पानी (water), ਨਾਮ/नाम (name), ਦੋਸਤ/दोस्त (friend) — identical or near. Core vocabulary overlaps enormously; learn one list, own two languages' hearts.

Split sounds mark the border: Punjabi tones against Hindi flatness, ਮੈਨੂੰ (mainun — to-me) against मुझे, ਹਾਂ (haan — yes) against हाँ. Pronouns and yes-no drifted most — precisely the words learners say most. Drill the pairs; cross the border daily.

Greetings show both faces: ਸਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ stands Punjabi-proud while नमस्ते stands Hindi-classic — yet each understands the other's instantly. Cousins recognise cousins; learn either with love and both reward you.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on ਘਰ = घर. Punjabi ਘਰ (house) and Hindi घर are the SAME word in sister scripts — thousands work this way. Learn Punjabi's core well and Hindi signboards start reading themselves.

Questions learners ask

Can Punjabis understand Hindi?

Largely yes for simple speech — ਘਰ, ਪਾਣੀ, ਨਾਮ, ਨਹੀਂ/नहीं are near-identical. Speed and slang divide them; patience reunites them.

Why do they sound similar yet different?

Punjabi kept tones and bold retroflexes; Hindi softened both. Cousins with different habits — precisely the words learners say most.

Which should I learn first?

Punjabi first for Punjab, Hindi structure second for the nation. Each language halves the other's difficulty — learn one, get the second at a discount.
