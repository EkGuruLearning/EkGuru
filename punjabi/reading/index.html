Reading Punjabi

Words to sentences to stories — reading from first signs to full pages.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Punjabi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Reading starts with words: ਸ਼ਬਦ (word) by ਸ਼ਬਦ — ਪੜ੍ਹੋ (please read) signs, labels, names. Punjabi spelling mirrors speech, so sounding out works: see ਦੁਕਾਨ, say dukaan, know shop. Phonetic scripts reward beginners richly.

Sentences assemble next: ਵਾਕ (sentence) — subject, object, verb-last. Read SOV sentences end-first for meaning: find the verb, then ask who-did-what. Backwards parsing feels odd for a week, then automatic forever.

Paragraphs and articles follow: ਪੈਰਾ (paragraph), ਲੇਖ (article), ਕਵਿਤਾ (poem). Children's stories first — short, illustrated, kind — then ਰਸਾਲੇ (magazines), then ਸਾਹਿਤ (literature) itself. Punjabi's written heritage spans five centuries; start at the shallow end.

Dictionaries deepen everything: ਸ਼ਬਦਕੋਸ਼ (dictionary) — five new words per page, written down, reviewed nightly. Reading without recording entertains; reading with recording teaches. Mine every page; keep every gem.

Questions learners ask

What should I read first?

Signboards: ਦੁਕਾਨ (shop), ਸਟੇਸ਼ਨ (station), ਹੋਟਲ (restaurant) — big letters, daily repetition, instant rewards. Streets teach faster than textbooks.

When am I ready for books?

Children's books — short ਵਾਕ (sentences), pictures for context, full conjuncts. Then comics, then ਅਖ਼ਬਾਰ headlines, then articles.

How do I handle unknown words?

Keep a ਸ਼ਬਦਕੋਸ਼ (dictionary) app open and mine unknown words — five per page, no more. Reading with a dictionary beats studying word lists: context glues memory.
