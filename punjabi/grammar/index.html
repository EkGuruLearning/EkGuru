Punjabi grammar, plainly explained

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

Punjabi grammar brings two grammatical genders, postpositions, and verb-final clauses together; use the examples to see how the roles connect.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Punjabi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Two genders shape everything: masculine (ਮੁੰਡਾ — boy), feminine (ਕੁੜੀ — girl). Adjectives pair — ਚੰਗਾ/ਚੰਗੀ — and verbs pair too. Gender is not decoration; it is Punjabi's skeleton.

Pronouns simplify persons: ਮੈਂ (I), ਤੂੰ (you-informal), ਤੁਸੀਂ (you-formal), ਉਹ (he-she/they), ਅਸੀਂ (we). ਅਸੀਂ vs ਆਪਾਂ (exclusive vs inclusive we) — Punjabi distinguishes; English doesn't. Choose your we wisely.

Postpositions replace prepositions: -ਨੂੰ (to), -ਨੇ (by/doer), -ਨਾਲ (with), -ਵਿੱਚ (in). ਘਰ ਨੂੰ ਜਾ (go home), ਚਮਚੇ ਨਾਲ ਖਾ (eat with a spoon). Suffixes after nouns — think backwards from English.

Verbs anchor sentences last: ਮੈਂ ਚਾਵਲ ਖਾਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ (I rice eat), ਉਹ ਸਕੂਲ ਜਾਂਦੀ ਹੈ (she to-school goes). Present (ਖਾਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ), past (ਖਾਧਾ), future (ਖਾਵਾਂਗਾ) — tense on verbs, time words framing. Verb-final: the one rule that never breaks.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on ਨੇ. ਨੇ (ne) marks the doer in past tense — ਰਾਮ ਨੇ ਕੀਤਾ (Ram did), ਮੈਂ ਖਾਧਾ (I ate). Ergative, elegant, everywhere in stories. Tiny suffix, enormous workload.

Questions learners ask

How many genders?

Masculine and feminine — nouns, adjectives AND verbs agree. ਚੰਗਾ/ਚੰਗੀ (good), ਜਾਂਦਾ/ਜਾਂਦੀ (go). Pairs everywhere; learn them as sets.

What are postpositions?

ਨੂੰ marks direction (ਅੰਮ੍ਰਿਤਸਰ ਨੂੰ — to Amritsar), ਨੇ marks doers (ਰਾਮ ਨੇ ਕੀਤਾ — Ram did), ਨਾਲ means with. Postpositions follow nouns — learn each with ten examples.

What is Punjabi word order?

Subject-Object-Verb: ਮੈਂ ਚਾਵਲ ਖਾਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ (I rice eat). Verb last, always. English order is the classic beginner error.
