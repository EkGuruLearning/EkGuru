Learn Hindi for German speakers

Your first language decides which parts of Hindi are easy and which are slow, and almost no course takes account of it. This page does. It is written for someone who already speaks German and wants to know what that is worth before spending a year finding out.

Where the verb goes

This is where German speakers have an advantage nobody tells them about. German already puts the verb at the end — in every subordinate clause, and the participle at the end of the main clause too: ich habe das Buch gelesen. Hindi does it in every clause. You are not learning a new structure; you are extending one you use daily.

What German gives you for free

Case. German's four cases have trained exactly the instinct Hindi's postpositions require — that a noun's role is marked, not just implied by position. When Hindi puts ने after the subject of a completed action, the German speaker's reaction is 'yes, obviously' where the English speaker's is 'why'. Both languages are Indo-European, so there is a scattering of visible cognates too, and German's willingness to build long compounds prepares you for Hindi's Sanskrit-derived ones.

What it does not prepare you for

Retroflex consonants, which German entirely lacks, and the fact that German has aspiration but distributes it differently — you aspirate initial p, t and k automatically, which means you will produce फ where you want प. That auto-aspiration is a habit to suppress, and it is the mirror image of the Spanish speaker's problem.

Sounds to work on

WhatWhy it matters
Suppressing automatic aspirationGerman initial stops are aspirated by default. Hindi's unaspirated क प त need the puff removed, which is harder than adding one.
Retroflexट ठ ड ढ. New, and worth a fortnight.
w vs vGerman w is English v, which happens to be close to Hindi व. This is one of the few places German speakers get a free pass.
The Hindi rA light tap. Not the German uvular r, and not silent at the end of a syllable the way it is in much of Germany.

Grammar, compared with German

FeatureHow it compares
Verb-final: already yoursThe single largest obstacle for English speakers is not an obstacle for you.
Case → postpositionDem Mann is Hindi आदमी को. The concept is identical; only the mechanics differ.
Gender, but only twoGerman has three genders, Hindi has two. This is one of the very few places where Hindi is simpler than the language you already speak.
No verb-second ruleGerman's V2 constraint has no Hindi equivalent. Hindi word order is much freer, and that freedom takes some getting used to in the opposite direction.

A word worth knowing about

बस (bas)

Looks like: the vehicle. In practice: it also means 'enough' or 'that's it' and is used constantly as a standalone word. Both meanings are common and context separates them instantly.

A realistic first thirty days

  1. Days 1–5: aspiration control, in both directions — adding it where Hindi wants it and removing it where German adds it automatically.
  2. Days 6–14: script. German speakers who have learned Latin or Greek will find Devanagari's systematic ordering familiar.
  3. Days 15–22: postpositions, explicitly mapped to the cases you already know. This is the fastest route for you and most courses will not do it this way unless you ask.
  4. Days 23–30: gender and verb agreement, which is less work for you than for anyone starting from English.

Notice that the order is not the order a general course would use. That is the whole point of organising this by your first language: the sequence that works depends on what you already have.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.

Questions German speakers ask

Is Hindi hard for German speakers?

It depends entirely on what you are comparing. Case. German's four cases have trained exactly the instinct Hindi's postpositions require — that a noun's role is marked, not just implied by position. When Hindi puts ने after the subject of a completed action, the German speaker's reaction is 'yes, obviously' where the English speaker's is 'why'. Both languages are Indo-European, so there is a scattering of visible cognates too, and German's willingness to build long compounds prepares you for Hindi's Sanskrit-derived ones.

What is the hardest part of Hindi for a German speaker?

Retroflex consonants, which German entirely lacks, and the fact that German has aspiration but distributes it differently — you aspirate initial p, t and k automatically, which means you will produce फ where you want प. That auto-aspiration is a habit to suppress, and it is the mirror image of the Spanish speaker's problem.

Do German speakers need to learn Devanagari?

Not to speak. Many learners work in romanised Hindi for months. If you want to read signs, messages or anything written, the script is phonetic and consistent, and most learners read simple words within a few weeks.

How long does Hindi take from German?

Nobody can give you an honest number, because it depends on hours per week far more than on your first language. What this page can tell you is which parts will be quick for you and which will be slow, which is more useful than a figure.

The part you cannot do alone

Everything above can be read. The sound work in particular cannot be self-assessed — you cannot hear your own errors, which is precisely why they fossilise. A tutor is the only mechanism that fixes that, and an hour a week is enough.
