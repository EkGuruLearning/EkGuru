Telugu

Telugu grammar, plainly explained

Verb last, respect built in — Telugu grammar in plain English.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Telugu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Telugu sentences end with the verb: నేను పుస్తకం చదువుతున్నాను (I — book — read). Subject first, object middle, verb last — SOV, the order of half the world's languages. Rearrange your English instincts once, and every Telugu sentence parses cleanly.

Small endings do heavy work: -లో (lo — in/on) places things — ఇంట్లో (at home), టేబులు మీద (on the table); -తో (to — with) joins company — స్నేహితుడితో (with a friend); -కు (ku) aims motion and wanting — బడికి (to school), నాకు కావాలి (I want). Endings, not word order, carry Telugu meaning.

Questions rise at the end: మీరు ఎక్కడికి వెళ్తున్నారు? (where are you going?) — ఎక్కడ (where), ఏమిటి (what), ఎవరు (who), ఎందుకు (why), ఎలా (how), ఎప్పుడు (when). Yes-no questions add -ఆ (-aa): భోజనం చేశారా? (have you eaten?). Question words plus rising tone — asking is easy.

Negation splits three ways: లేదు (ledu) denies existence — డబ్బు లేదు (no money); verb endings deny actions — నాకు తెలియదు (I don't know); వద్దు (vaddu) refuses — చక్కెర వద్దు (no sugar, thanks). Three no's for three jobs — Telugu refuses precisely.

A note on -కు (ku). Telugu's dative ending aims verbs at people and places: నాకు (to me), బడికి (to school), ఇంటికి (homeward). Sentences like నాకు అర్థమైంది (to-me it-is-understood = I understand) reveal Telugu's worldview: experiences come TO you.

Questions learners ask

Where does the verb go?

Put the verb last: నేను పుస్తకం చదువుతున్నాను (I book read). Subject-Object-Verb, always. English instincts will fight you for a week; then SOV feels like home.

How do I say I want something?

నాకు (naaku — to me), నీకు (neeku — to you familiar), మీకు (meeku — to you formal). నాకు నీళ్లు కావాలి (I want water) is literally to-me water is-wanted — Telugu wants flow toward people.

How do this and that work?

ఇది (idi — this, near me), అది (adi — that, far). ఇది ఏమిటి? (what is this?) points at anything within reach; mastery takes one shopping trip.
