Flashcards by topic in Telugu

Written by: authorship not independently recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-10-04. Editorial policy and corrections.

A few Telugu topic-card reviews at spaced intervals are easier to keep up than a single weekly cram.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Telugu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Cards carry memory: కార్డు (card) — one word, one side; meaning, the other. జ్ఞాపకశక్తి (memory) strengthens with every recall; మరపు (forgetting) is just unreviewed memory. Build decks per topic — greetings, food, verbs — and review each separately.

Spacing schedules success: విరామం (interval) — review new cards tomorrow, then in three days, seven, thirty. మళ్లీ మళ్లీ (again and again), but wider each time. Cramming fills hours; spacing fills years.

Rhythms sustain streaks: రోజూ (daily) quick decks, వారానికి (weekly) full reviews, నెలకు (monthly) championships. Ten daily minutes beat Sunday marathons; streaks turn study into అలవాటు (habit).

Scores gamify growth: మార్కులు (marks), పోటీ (contest), ఘనత (achievement) — even ఓటమి (defeat) teaches. Quiz yourself, beat yesterday's score, celebrate loudly. Flashcards are games that pay in fluency.

Language, culture, and common traps

A note on పునశ్చరణ. పునశ్చరణ (punascharana — revision, recollection) is Telugu's own word for spaced review — recalling again, and again, at widening intervals. The language itself teaches the method: revisit, widen, remember.

Questions learners ask

How often should I review?

మళ్లీ మళ్లీ (again and again) — but spaced: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 30 days. విరామం (spacing) beats cramming; జ్ఞాపకశక్తి (memory) loves intervals.

How many cards per topic?

Five to ten per topic — quality over quantity. Master one కార్డు deck fully before opening the next; half-known decks rot.

Daily or weekly review?

రోజూ (daily) short sessions — ten minutes, every day, no exceptions. Streaks compound; మరపు (forgetting) loses to routine.
