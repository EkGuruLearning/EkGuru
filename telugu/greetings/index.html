Saying hello the Telugu way

నమస్కారం opens every door in Telugu states — but mornings, welcomes and goodbyes each have their own grace.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Telugu spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

నమస్కారం (namaskaram) is the one greeting that never fails. Folded hands, a small nod, and it works with strangers, elders, shopkeepers and officials — exactly the people a learner meets first. Say it clearly, hold the folded hands half a second, and the Telugu states open to you.

Mornings get their own greeting: శుభోదయం (shubhodayam — good morning), heard in offices, schools and tiffin centres until about eleven. Evenings soften to శుభసాయంత్రం (shubhasaayantram). Time-stamped greetings signal that you live in the language, not just visit it.

Welcomes are grand in Telugu culture: స్వాగతం (svaagatam — welcome) opens homes, shops and hearts. Guests are greeted standing, often with water or coffee before a single question. Return the warmth — వచ్చాను, ధన్యవాదాలు (I've come, thank you) — and you are family within minutes.

Goodbyes promise return, never end: మళ్లీ కలుద్దాం (we'll meet again) is the everyday farewell; జాగ్రత్త (jaagratta — take care) adds affection. Leave every conversation with a నమస్కారం and a smile — in Telugu culture, how you leave is remembered longer than how you came.

A note on బాగున్నారా vs కులాసా. బాగున్నారా? (baagunnaaraa) is the everyday are you well? — warm, quick, everywhere. కులాసాగా ఉన్నారా? is its old-fashioned formal cousin, heard from grandparents. Start with బాగున్నారా; the rest arrives with aunties.

Questions learners ask

Can I just say namaskaram everywhere?

Yes, and nobody will mind — నమస్కారం with folded hands is understood and loved everywhere Telugu is spoken. With close friends a smile and బాగున్నారా? (are you well?) feels warmer.

How do I greet an elder respectfully?

నమస్కారం with folded hands and a small nod, and మీరు (meeru — formal you) until invited closer. Add అన్నయ్య (annayya) or అక్కయ్య (akkayya) for someone slightly older — it warms everything instantly.

How do Telugu people say goodbye?

మళ్లీ కలుద్దాం (mallee kaluddaam — we'll meet again) is the warm standard. సెలవు (selavu — permission to leave) is the classic respectful farewell — receive it with a smile and a nod.
