Two spellings are normal. Punjabi has no academy that fixes how a number is written, and regions differ — 25 is written ਪੰਝੀ in most charts and ਪੱਚੀ by many speakers. Where a second form is genuinely common, this page shows it rather than pretending there is one answer.

Gurmukhi digits — ੦ to ੯

Punjabi has its own numerals, and they behave exactly like the digits you already know: they combine left to right, so ੨੫ is 25 and ੧੦੦ is 100. Ten symbols, no new idea.

Most Punjabi today — newspapers, WhatsApp, road signs with prices — uses the ordinary 0–9 digits. The Gurmukhi forms appear on signboards, in gurdwaras, in children's books and anywhere a writer wants the text to sit fully in the script. You will not be wrong using either, but you cannot read a Punjabi calendar without them.

Bigger numbers

Punjabi uses the lakh and crore system, the same as Hindi — not the thousand-million system English uses.

Why the list looks random, and the three things that make it stick

1. Every decade has its own ending

Learn the ending once and ten numbers come almost free. The twenties end in an -ee sound (ਇੱਕੀ, ਬਾਈ), the fifties in -anjaa (ਇਕਵੰਜਾ, ਬਵੰਜਾ), the sixties in -haṭh (ਇਕਾਹਠ, ਬਾਹਠ), the seventies in -hattar, the eighties in -aasi, the nineties in -aanven.

2. The unit is usually the first sound

Numbers with a one start ik- (ਇਕਤਾਲੀ, ਇਕਵੰਜਾ), with a two start b- (ਬੱਤੀ, ਬਵੰਜਾ), with a seven sat-, with an eight ath-. So ਪਚਾਸੀ decodes itself: pach (five) + aasi (eighties) = 85.

3. The nines look forward, not back

This is the one that catches everybody. 29 is ਉਣੱਤੀ, "one short of thirty" — not "twenty-nine". 39, 49, 59, 69, 79, 89 and 99 all do the same. English does this for 11 to 19 and then stops; Punjabi keeps going to 99, which is exactly why those seven numbers are the ones people forget.

Questions people ask

How do you count to 10 in Punjabi?

1 ਇੱਕ (ikk), 2 ਦੋ (do), 3 ਤਿੰਨ (tinn), 4 ਚਾਰ (chaar), 5 ਪੰਜ (panj), 6 ਛੇ (chhe), 7 ਸੱਤ (satt), 8 ਅੱਠ (atth), 9 ਨੌਂ (naun), 10 ਦਸ (das).

Is 25 ਪੰਝੀ or ਪੱਚੀ?

Both. They are both in everyday use and both are correct — it is regional, not right and wrong. The table shows the more widely published form first.

Why do 29, 39 and 49 look like the next ten?

Because Punjabi builds the nines from the ten above them. 49 is ਉਣਿੰਜਾ, "one short of fifty". Once you know that, seven numbers stop being exceptions and become a rule.

Do I need the Gurmukhi digits to read Punjabi?

No. Prices, dates and phone numbers are written with 0–9 almost everywhere. The Gurmukhi digits matter for calendars, older printed material and anything deliberately written fully in the script. They take about ten minutes to learn — there are only ten.

What is ginti?

ਗਿਣਤੀ (ginti) is the Punjabi word for counting — the set of numbers, especially 1 to 100, that children traditionally learn by heart in the same order every time.

Learn the script these numbers are written in

Gurmukhi has 35 consonants and 10 vowel signs, and numbers are the easiest thing to start with because there are only a hundred of them and you already know what they mean. A tutor will fix your pronunciation before it sets — find a Punjabi or Hindi tutor.
