What a quiz can and cannot tell you

Multiple choice measures recognition, which is the easiest of the four language skills and the least useful. You can score full marks here and still be unable to order tea. That is not a flaw in the quiz; it is what quizzes are. Treat a good score as evidence that your vocabulary revision is working, and nothing more.

The abilities that actually decide whether you can use Hindi — producing a sentence under time pressure, and understanding one said at full speed by someone who is not being careful for you — cannot be tested by a web page, and anyone claiming otherwise is selling something.

Questions

What does the quiz test?

Recognition of common phrases, numbers and letters — the things a beginner should be able to spot. It does not test speaking or listening, which are the two abilities that matter most and neither of which a web page can assess honestly.

Is a good score meaningful?

Only narrowly. Scoring ten out of ten here means you recognise these particular items. It does not mean you can hold a conversation, and any tool that tells you otherwise is flattering you.

Can I retake it?

As often as you like. The questions are drawn at random from the full set each time, so it is different on every run.

The honest next step

If you did well here, the useful thing is not another quiz. It is twenty minutes of being unable to find a word while a patient person waits. That is where the learning happens and it is the one thing this site cannot do for you.
