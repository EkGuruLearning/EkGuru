What is the word order in Hindi?

Hindi is subject-object-verb: the verb goes last. "I eat rice" is मैं चावल खाता हूँ — literally "I rice eat". Adjectives come before nouns as in English, but the little joining words come AFTER the noun, which is why they are called postpositions rather than prepositions. Once the verb-last habit forms, long Hindi sentences stop feeling like puzzles.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

SOV, and why it matters

English: I eat rice. Hindi: मैं चावल खाता हूँ — I rice eat.

Around 45% of the world's languages do it this way, including Japanese, Korean and Turkish. English is the outlier, not Hindi.

The practical consequence: in a long Hindi sentence the verb — the thing that tells you what actually happened — arrives last. Listeners have to hold the whole sentence before it resolves. That is why Hindi feels fast to a beginner even at normal speed.

Postpositions, not prepositions

English puts them before: in the house, to Ram, from Delhi. Hindi puts them after: घर में, राम को, दिल्ली से.

The common ones: में (in), पर (on), से (from/with/by), को (to), का/की/के (of), तक (until), के लिए (for).

Where the rest goes

  • Adjective before noun: लाल किताब, red book — same as English
  • Time and place early: कल मैं दिल्ली जाऊँगा — tomorrow I Delhi will-go
  • Question words sit where the answer would: तुम क्या खा रहे हो? — you what eating are?

That last point is quietly useful. Hindi does not shuffle the sentence to ask a question the way English does.

Flexibility

Word order can move for emphasis, because the postpositions keep the roles clear. चावल मैं खाता हूँ puts the stress on the rice. Beginners should stay with SOV until it is automatic; the freedom becomes available later.

The habit to build

When you form a sentence, decide the verb last. Say the who, then the what, then the doing. Fight the English instinct for a fortnight and it stops being effortful.
