How is Hindi different from Punjabi and Bengali?

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

All three are Indo-Aryan languages descended from Sanskrit, but they are separate languages, not dialects. Hindi uses Devanagari, Punjabi in India uses Gurmukhi, Bengali uses its own script. Grammar is broadly similar — verb last, postpositions, gendered nouns — but vocabulary and sound diverge enough that speakers cannot simply understand one another.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

Mutual intelligibility, honestly

  • Hindi ↔ Punjabi: partial. Shared vocabulary and a Hindi speaker in Delhi hears Punjabi constantly, so exposure does a lot of the work. A Hindi speaker with no exposure catches the gist and misses the detail.
  • Hindi ↔ Bengali: low. Related, clearly, but a Hindi speaker does not follow a Bengali conversation.
  • Hindi ↔ Urdu: essentially the same spoken language in different scripts. Genuinely different from the two above.

Scripts

| Language | Script | |---|---| | Hindi | Devanagari देवनागरी | | Punjabi (India) | Gurmukhi ਗੁਰਮੁਖੀ | | Punjabi (Pakistan) | Shahmukhi, Perso-Arabic | | Bengali | Bengali–Assamese বাংলা |

Devanagari and Bengali script share an ancestor and both hang letters from a horizontal line, which is why they look related at a glance.

Grammar differences worth knowing

Punjabi is tonal — pitch changes meaning, which is unusual in this family and hard for Hindi speakers.

Bengali has no grammatical gender at all. No masculine and feminine nouns, no changing verb endings. For an English speaker that removes the single hardest part of Hindi. Bengali instead has an elaborate system of verb forms for politeness levels.

Which is most useful?

By speakers and reach, Hindi — around 600 million, and it functions as a link language across north India. Bengali is next at roughly 265 million and gives you Bangladesh as well as West Bengal. Punjabi is the most useful of the three in the UK and Canada, where the diaspora is large.

Does one help with another?

Some. Shared Sanskrit vocabulary and a shared grammatical shape mean the second Indo-Aryan language is noticeably easier than the first. But it is a real second language, not a dialect you can guess your way into.
