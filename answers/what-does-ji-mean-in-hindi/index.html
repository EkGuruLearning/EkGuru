What does "ji" mean in Hindi?

जी (ji) is a respect marker with no English equivalent. Added after a name or title it makes it polite — नमस्ते जी, पापा जी, गांधी जी. On its own it means a soft "yes". Before हाँ or नहीं it makes them formal: जी हाँ, जी नहीं. It is the smallest word in Hindi and one of the most important.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

The four jobs it does

1. After a name — respect. शर्मा जी, दादी जी, गांधी जी. Attach it to almost any name and the sentence becomes courteous. Overusing it is not a mistake; omitting it with an elder can be.

2. On its own — a polite yes. Someone calls your name, you answer "जी?" — meaning "yes?" or "you called?". Warmer than हाँ and much more common than learners expect.

3. Before yes and no — formality. जी हाँ (ji haan) and जी नहीं (ji nahin). This is the easiest politeness upgrade in the whole language: two extra letters and a flat answer becomes a respectful one.

4. After a greeting — warmth. नमस्ते जी. Small, and immediately noticeable.

Where it comes from

Probably from जीव, "life" or "soul" — so addressing the person, not the body. Whatever the etymology, it now functions as pure social lubricant.

When not to use it

With close friends and children it sounds oddly stiff. Between peers of the same age it is unnecessary. The rule of thumb: elder, stranger, or anyone in a position of respect — use जी. Friend — do not.

The learner's shortcut

If you are unsure how formal to be, add जी. Being slightly too polite in Hindi costs nothing. Being too casual with an elder is one of the few things that genuinely registers as rude.
