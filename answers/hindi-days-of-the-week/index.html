What are the days of the week in Hindi?

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

सोमवार somvaar (Monday), मंगलवार mangalvaar (Tuesday), बुधवार budhvaar (Wednesday), गुरुवार guruvaar (Thursday), शुक्रवार shukravaar (Friday), शनिवार shanivaar (Saturday), रविवार ravivaar (Sunday). Every one ends in -वार (-vaar), meaning "day", and each is named after a planet — exactly as the English days are, which is why the two sets line up perfectly.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

The pattern that makes them easy

Strip -वार off and what remains is a planet. Som is the Moon, Mangal is Mars, Budh is Mercury, Guru is Jupiter, Shukra is Venus, Shani is Saturn, Ravi is the Sun.

Now compare English: Monday is Moon-day, Saturday is Saturn's day, Sunday is Sun's day. Both systems inherited the same Babylonian week. You are not memorising seven random words — you are matching two lists you half know already.

Saying "on Monday"

Add को (ko): सोमवार को (somvaar ko) means "on Monday". Same particle for every day.

Today, tomorrow, yesterday

  • आज (aaj) — today
  • कल (kal) — both tomorrow and yesterday
  • परसों (parson) — both the day after tomorrow and the day before

This surprises everyone. The verb tense tells you which: kal aaya (kal + past) is yesterday, kal aayega (kal + future) is tomorrow. Hindi treats "one day away" as the unit and lets the sentence supply the direction.

Booking a lesson

"मंगलवार को शाम सात बजे" (mangalvaar ko shaam saat baje) — Tuesday at 7 in the evening. Day + को + time + बजे is the whole formula.
