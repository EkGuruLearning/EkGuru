What are the colours in Hindi?

लाल laal (red), नीला neela (blue), हरा hara (green), पीला peela (yellow), काला kaala (black), सफ़ेद safed (white), नारंगी naarangi (orange), गुलाबी gulaabi (pink), भूरा bhoora (brown), बैंगनी baingani (purple). Most colours ending in -आ change with gender: लाल stays लाल, but हरा becomes हरी for a feminine noun.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

The gender rule, in one line

Colours ending in -आ change: हरा (hara) → हरी (hari) → हरे (hare). Colours ending in anything else — लाल, सफ़ेद, नारंगी, गुलाबी — never change at all.

So: हरा आम (green mango, masculine) but हरी मिर्च (green chilli, feminine). Same colour, different ending, because the noun decided.

Colours named after things

Hindi is lovely about this and it makes them easy to remember:

  • नारंगी (naarangi) — orange, from नारंगी the fruit
  • गुलाबी (gulaabi) — pink, from गुलाब, a rose
  • बैंगनी (baingani) — purple, from बैंगन, an aubergine
  • सुनहरा (sunahra) — golden, from सोना, gold

Once you know the noun, the colour is free.

Light and dark

हल्का (halka) for light, गहरा (gehra) for dark, placed before: हल्का नीला, light blue. Both follow the same -आ gender rule.

Asking

"यह किस रंग का है?" (yeh kis rang ka hai?) — what colour is this? रंग (rang) is the word for colour, and it is the same रंग in Holi, the festival of colours.
