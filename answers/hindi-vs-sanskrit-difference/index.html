What is the difference between Hindi and Sanskrit?

Sanskrit is the ancient classical language of India; Hindi is a modern language descended from it, roughly as Italian descends from Latin. They share the Devanagari script and a great deal of vocabulary, which makes them look closer than they are. Sanskrit has eight noun cases and a famously complex grammar; Hindi has simplified almost all of it. Learning Sanskrit will not let you speak Hindi.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

The relationship, plainly

Sanskrit → Prakrits → Apabhramsha → Hindi. Roughly two and a half thousand years of ordinary speech drifting away from a literary standard.

A useful comparison: Latin and Italian. An Italian speaker recognises a great deal of Latin and cannot read Cicero. That is about where a Hindi speaker stands with Sanskrit.

Same script, different languages

Both are written in Devanagari, which is why they look so similar on the page. But English and French share an alphabet too.

Grammar is where they part

| | Sanskrit | Hindi | |---|---|---| | Noun cases | eight | effectively two, plus postpositions | | Verb forms | enormous | manageable | | Word order | very free | subject-object-verb | | Dual number | yes | no |

Sanskrit encodes relationships in word endings. Hindi uses small separate words — का, को, से, में — placed after the noun. That single shift is most of what makes Hindi learnable.

Shared vocabulary

Words borrowed straight from Sanskrit are called तत्सम (tatsam) — धन्यवाद, विद्यालय, राष्ट्र. Words worn down over time are तद्भव (tadbhav) — दूध from दुग्ध, हाथ from हस्त.

Formal Hindi leans tatsam; conversational Hindi leans tadbhav and Persian. A news broadcast and a market conversation can feel like different registers of the same language, because in vocabulary terms they nearly are.

Which should you learn?

To talk to people, travel, watch films, work in north India: Hindi. To read scripture, study philology or chant: Sanskrit. They are different projects, and Hindi is by a wide margin the easier of the two.
