How do you order food in Hindi?

…दीजिए (…deejiye) is the polite "please give me": एक चाय दीजिए, one tea please. For the bill, बिल दीजिए. To ask the price, कितने का है? (kitne ka hai?). And the sentence worth learning before any other if you have a dietary restriction: मुझे … नहीं चाहिए (mujhe … nahin chahiye) — I don't want …
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

Ordering

  • एक चाय दीजिए — one tea, please
  • दो रोटी और दाल दीजिए — two rotis and dal, please
  • मेन्यू दिखाइए (menu dikhaiye) — show me the menu
  • बिल दीजिए (bill deejiye) — the bill, please
  • पानी (paani) — water. बोतल बंद पानी — bottled water.

दीजिए is the polite imperative and it works with any noun. It is the single highest-value word on this page.

Saying how spicy

  • तीखा (teekha) — spicy hot
  • कम तीखा (kam teekha) — less spicy
  • तीखा नहीं (teekha nahin) — not spicy

Say it when you order, not after. And know that "not spicy" is a relative measurement.

Dietary needs — say these clearly

  • मैं शाकाहारी हूँ (main shaakaahaari hoon) — I am vegetarian
  • मुझे प्याज़ लहसुन नहीं चाहिए — no onion or garlic
  • मुझे … से एलर्जी है (mujhe … se allergy hai) — I am allergic to …

शाकाहारी is widely understood and respected. If it is an allergy rather than a preference, use the allergy sentence — it is taken more seriously.

Prices

कितने का है? — how much is it? Answers come in numbers, so the numbers page is the natural next one.

Afterwards

बहुत स्वादिष्ट था (bahut swaadisht tha) — it was delicious. Say it to the person who cooked it and watch what happens.
