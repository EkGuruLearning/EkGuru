Learn Hindi for Swahili speakers

Your first language decides which parts of Hindi are easy and which are slow, and almost no course takes account of it. This page does. It is written for someone who already speaks Swahili and wants to know what that is worth before spending a year finding out.

Where the verb goes

Subject-verb-object, so Hindi's verb-final order is new. Swahili's heavy verbal morphology does mean you are used to a verb carrying a lot of information, which helps when Hindi's verbs start agreeing with gender and number.

What Swahili gives you for free

A shared vocabulary layer through Arabic and Persian that most learners never notice: duka, safari, hisabu, lakini, karatasi, wakati, kitabu — all of these have Hindi-Urdu counterparts from the same source. Swahili's five clean vowels also line up almost exactly with Hindi's short vowels, so your pronunciation of the vowel system is right from the start. And Swahili's noun-class system, while very different from gender, means the idea that nouns belong to categories which force agreement elsewhere in the sentence is entirely familiar — Hindi's two genders will seem simple by comparison.

What it does not prepare you for

Aspiration and the retroflex series, both absent from standard Swahili. And Hindi's gender is arbitrary where Swahili's noun classes are semi-semantic, so the categories cannot be reasoned about — they must be memorised with each word.

Stress movesSwahili stresses the penultimate syllable consistently; Hindi stress is flatter and less predictable.

Grammar, compared with Swahili

FeatureHow it compares
Noun classes → genderThe concept of a noun class forcing agreement elsewhere is already yours. Hindi's version has two classes instead of many, but is arbitrary rather than semantic.
PostpositionsSwahili uses prepositions and locative suffixes; Hindi puts them after the noun.
Shared Arabic and Persian vocabularykitabu/किताब, wakati/वक़्त, duka/दुकान. A real head start.
Verb agreementSwahili verbs already agree with the subject class. Hindi's gender agreement is a smaller version of a habit you have.

A word worth knowing about

kitabu / किताब

Looks like: book in both. In practice: a true friend — both from Arabic. So are safari, wakati, hisabu and lakini. This layer is worth harvesting early.

A realistic first thirty days

  1. Days 1–5: harvest the shared Arabic and Persian vocabulary. You know more Hindi words than you think.
  2. Days 6–13: aspiration and retroflex — your two sound gaps.
  3. Days 14–21: Devanagari.
  4. Days 22–30: gender, learned with each noun rather than reasoned about.

Notice that the order is not the order a general course would use. That is the whole point of organising this by your first language: the sequence that works depends on what you already have.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.

Questions Swahili speakers ask

Is Hindi hard for Swahili speakers?

It depends entirely on what you are comparing. A shared vocabulary layer through Arabic and Persian that most learners never notice: duka, safari, hisabu, lakini, karatasi, wakati, kitabu — all of these have Hindi-Urdu counterparts from the same source. Swahili's five clean vowels also line up almost exactly with Hindi's short vowels, so your pronunciation of the vowel system is right from the start. And Swahili's noun-class system, while very different from gender, means the idea that nouns belong to categories which force agreement elsewhere in the sentence is entirely familiar — Hindi's two genders will seem simple by comparison.

What is the hardest part of Hindi for a Swahili speaker?

Aspiration and the retroflex series, both absent from standard Swahili. And Hindi's gender is arbitrary where Swahili's noun classes are semi-semantic, so the categories cannot be reasoned about — they must be memorised with each word.

Do Swahili speakers need to learn Devanagari?

Not to speak. Many learners work in romanised Hindi for months. If you want to read signs, messages or anything written, the script is phonetic and consistent, and most learners read simple words within a few weeks.

How long does Hindi take from Swahili?

Nobody can give you an honest number, because it depends on hours per week far more than on your first language. What this page can tell you is which parts will be quick for you and which will be slow, which is more useful than a figure.

The part you cannot do alone

Everything above can be read. The sound work in particular cannot be self-assessed — you cannot hear your own errors, which is precisely why they fossilise. A tutor is the only mechanism that fixes that, and an hour a week is enough.
