How do you say I am tired in Hindi?

Author: not separately recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-11-21. Editorial policy and corrections.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

मैं थरा गया हूँ (main tharra gaa ya hoon) or मैं थक गया हूँ (main thaak gaa ya hoon) are the two most common — "I have become tired." Thaak gaa ya hoon is the more standard form; tharra gaa ya hoon carries a touch more weariness.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

When to use it

SituationSay
Standard, all-purposeमैं थक गया हूँ main thaak gayaa hoon
More weary, worn outमैं थरा गया हूँ main tharra gaa ya hoon
Female speaker (standard)मैं थक गई हूँ main thaak gaii hoon
I am very tiredमैं बहुत थक गया हूँ main bahut thaak gayaa hoon

Thaak gayaa hoon — the standard form

Thaak means "tired" and gayaa means "became/gone." Main thaak gayaa hoon literally means "I have become tired." It is the standard, all-purpose way to say you are tired and works in every context — after work, after a journey, after exercise. It is neutral in register and universally understood.

Tharrnaa gaa ya hoon — more weary

Tharrnaa is a slightly stronger, more colloquial verb than thaaknaa, carrying a sense of being worn out, exhausted, or drained. Main tharra gaa ya hoon means "I am worn out," and it is the natural choice when you want to convey deeper fatigue — after a long day, a hard journey, or a stretch of being on your feet.

Frequently asked

What is the difference between thaak gayaa hoon and tharra gaa ya hoon?

Thaak gayaa hoon is the standard, all-purpose 'I am tired.' Tharra gaa ya hoon is slightly stronger, meaning 'I am worn out/exhausted.' Both are correct; thaak gayaa hoon is the more common default.

How do you say I am tired in Hindi — for a female speaker?

For a female speaker, the verb forms change: मैं थक गई हूँ (Main thaak gaii hoon) — 'I have become tired.' The past tense participle changes from gayaa (masc) to gaii (fem).

How do you say I am very tired in Hindi?

मैं बहुत थक गया हूँ (Main bahut thaak gayaa hoon) — 'I am very tired.' Bahut means 'very/much,' so adding it to the standard phrase gives you the emphasis.

How do you say I am exhausted in Hindi?

मैं बहुत थरा गया हूँ (Main bahut tharra gaa ya hoon) or ฉัน exhaustion — the stronger tharra form with bahut (very) conveys 'I am exhausted.' You can also say मैं खटखटा गया हूँ for a very colloquial, worn-out feeling.
