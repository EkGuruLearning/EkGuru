Is Hindi actually useful to learn?

For most people yes — around 600 million speakers, the working language of north India, and it unlocks a country of 1.4 billion. It is less useful if your interest in India is limited to the south, or to IT work where English dominates.

Where Hindi genuinely helps

  • Travel in north and central India — outside hotels and airports English thins out fast. Hindi is the difference between being a tourist and being a guest.
  • Family and marriage — the most common reason our students learn. Grandparents often speak no English at all.
  • Business — a factory floor, a supplier, a driver, a market. Boardrooms run in English; almost nothing else does.
  • Film and music — an enormous body of work, most of it never subtitled well.

Where it helps less — honestly

  • South India. Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh have their own languages and a real history of resisting Hindi.
  • Indian IT and corporate work. English is the working language. Hindi builds rapport; it is not required.
  • As a "useful second language" in the abstract. With no connection to India, Spanish or French will be used more often.

The honest summary

Hindi is worth learning if you have a reason — people, a place, a plan. It is one of the world's largest languages, but unlike Spanish it is concentrated in one region, so its usefulness depends almost entirely on whether you go there or know people from there.

If you do have that reason it repays effort quickly: Hindi speakers are unusually welcoming to learners, and even a hundred words visibly changes how you are treated.
