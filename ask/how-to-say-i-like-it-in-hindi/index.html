How do you say I like it in Hindi?

Author: not separately recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-11-21. Editorial policy and corrections.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

मुझे यह पसंद है (mujhe yah pasand hai) is the direct, natural translation — "to me this is liked." मैं इसे पसंद करता हूँ (main ise pasand kartaa hoon) is the alternative verb form — "I it like." Both are correct and commonly used.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

Mujhe yah pasand hai — the default

This is the most common, natural way to say "I like it." Mujhe means "to me," yah means "this," and pasand hai means "is liked." The structure — "to me this is liked" — is the standard Hindi way to express liking, and it is the phrase you will hear most often in everyday conversation.

Pasand kartaa hoon — the verb form

Main ise pasand kartaa hoon uses the verb karnaa (to do) — literally "I do like it." It is equally correct but slightly more deliberate. The mujhe pasand hai form is more common and more natural in most contexts. Both are fine; the first is the default.

Frequently asked

What is the most natural way to say I like it in Hindi?

मुझे यह पसंद है (Mujhe yah pasand hai) is the most natural and common. It literally means 'to me this is liked' and is the standard way to express liking something.

What is the difference between mujhe yah pasand hai and main ise pasand kartaa hoon?

Mujhe yah pasand hai is the more common, natural form — 'to me this is liked.' Main ise pasand kartaa hoon is the verb form — 'I like it.' Both are correct, but the first is the everyday default.

How do you say I like it very much in Hindi?

मुझे यह बहुत पसंद है (Mujhe yah bahut pasand hai) — add bahut (very/much) to the standard phrase. For even more emphasis, आप much: मुझे यह बहुत-बहुत पसंद है.

How do you say I don't like it in Hindi?

मुझे यह पसंद नहीं है (Mujhe yah pasand nahin hai) — add nahin (not) after pasand. The structure is identical, just with the negation inserted between pasand and hai.
