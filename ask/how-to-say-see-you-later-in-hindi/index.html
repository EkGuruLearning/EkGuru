How do you say see you later in Hindi?

Author: not separately recorded. Maintained by Prakash. Reviewed by: not recorded. Updated: 2026-11-21. Editorial policy and corrections.

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

बाद में मिलते हैं (baad meN milte hain) is the most natural and widely used — "we meet later." तुमसे later (tumse baad meN) is a casual alternative, and फलाने is the colloquial way to fill in a specific time.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

When to use it

SituationSay
Polite, all-purposeबाद में मिलते हैं baad meN milte hain
Casual, between friendsबाद में मिलता है baad meN milta hai
With a specific time PM मिलते हैं PM milte hain
A more final goodbyeफिर मिलते हैं phir milte hain — we meet again

Baad meN milte hain — the standard farewell

This is the phrase you will hear most often when parting with someone you will see again. Literally "later we meet," it is warm, natural, and works with friends, colleagues, and acquaintances. The plural milte hain is the polite form; with a close friend you might hear the singular milta hai.

Phir milte hain — "we meet again"

When the goodbye carries a sense of finality but you still expect to see the person again, phir milte hain is the right choice. Phir means "again," so it is slightly warmer than baad meN milte hain and implies a more meaningful parting.

Frequently asked

What is the difference between baad meN milte hain and phir milte hain?

Baad meN milte hain means 'we meet later' — a standard, neutral farewell. Phir milte hain means 'we meet again' — slightly warmer, used when the parting feels more meaningful or when you are not sure when you will next see the person.

How do you say goodbye in Hindi?

The most common spoken goodbye is अच्छா (acchā) or achha, which means 'okay/good.' Namaste also works as a formal goodbye. See the how to say goodbye in Hindi page for the full picture.

How do you say see you tomorrow in Hindi?

कल मिलते हैं (kal milte hain) — 'we meet tomorrow.' Kal means tomorrow, and milte hain is 'we meet.' It is the most natural way to say see you tomorrow.

How do you say take care in Hindi?

दिल से (dil se) is not the phrase. The common spoken form is anywhere from dhiyan rakhe (ध्यान रखें — 'keep attention/take care') to the simpler aap apna dhyan rakhein (आप अपना ध्यान रखें) — 'take care of yourself.'
