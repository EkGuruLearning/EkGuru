Will Hindi speakers mind if I make mistakes?

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

Almost universally no — the reaction to a foreigner attempting Hindi is warmth, not correction. The bigger risk is being answered in English out of helpfulness.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

Most learners worry about the wrong thing. Nobody is scoring your grammar. The common experience of trying Hindi in India is that people are visibly pleased and become noticeably more helpful.

The actual problem: being switched to English

If you look foreign, many people will answer in English — not to correct you, but to be kind and to save you effort. It is the single biggest obstacle to practising in India, and it is well-meant.

What works: मैं हिंदी सीख रहा हूँ, हिंदी में बोलिए — I am learning Hindi, please speak in Hindi. Said with a smile, it works almost every time, and it is worth memorising before you arrive.

The one mistake worth avoiding

Not a grammar mistake — a register one. Using तू with a stranger, a waiter or anyone senior is genuinely rude, where a wrong verb ending is simply a wrong verb ending. Default to आप with everybody and the risk disappears.

With family

Heritage learners often get more correction, not less, and it can sting more because it comes from people whose opinion matters. That is affection expressed awkwardly, and it is usually worth saying out loud that you would rather be encouraged than corrected mid-sentence.
