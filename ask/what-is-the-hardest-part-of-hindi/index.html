What is the hardest part of learning Hindi?

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

For most learners it is grammatical gender — every noun is masculine or feminine, there is no reliable rule, and the verb agrees too. The pronunciation gets more attention but is a smaller problem.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

Learners expect the script to be the wall and it is not: Devanagari is phonetic, finite and usually readable within six weeks. The real difficulties are two, and they are both grammar.

Gender, and why postponing it is expensive

Every Hindi noun carries a gender, adjectives agree with it, and — unlike Spanish or French — so does the verb. Your own gender appears in your verbs in every sentence you speak. There is no dependable rule for guessing, so gender has to be learned with each noun from the very first day. Learners who decide to "do gender later" end up relearning several hundred words, which takes roughly ten times as long as learning them correctly once.

The ने construction

In the completed form of a transitive verb, the subject takes ने and the verb agrees with the object instead. मैंने किताब पढ़ी — the verb is feminine because किताब is, regardless of who is speaking. This is called split ergativity, the rule is one sentence long, and the exceptions are the entire subject. It is the single thing self-taught learners most reliably get wrong and then fossilise.

What is easier than expected

No articles. No ser/estar distinction. Almost no irregular verbs. Spelling that matches pronunciation. Hindi gives back more than it is given credit for.
