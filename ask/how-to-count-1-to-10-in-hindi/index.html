How do you count from 1 to 10 in Hindi?

एक ek (1), दो do (2), तीन teen (3), चार chaar (4), पाँच paanch (5), छह chhah (6), सात saat (7), आठ aath (8), नौ nau (9), दस das (10).
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

The bad news about 11 to 100

English builds numbers from parts: twenty-one, twenty-two. Hindi does not. Every number from 1 to 100 is its own word, and you have to learn all hundred. 21 is ikkis, 22 is baais, 31 is ikattis — the pattern rhymes but never quite repeats.

This is genuinely the most tedious part of learning Hindi, and there is no shortcut. The good news is that it is pure memorisation with no grammar attached, so it is the one thing an app is perfect for.

What to learn first

1 to 20, then the tens (20, 30, 40…). That covers prices, time and most conversations. Fill in the gaps as you meet them.

A trap worth knowing now

Indians count large numbers in lakhs (100,000) and crores (10,000,000), not millions. A price written "₹5,00,000" is not a typo — the digit grouping is different too.
