Is Hindi hard to learn for English speakers?

Moderately. The US Foreign Service Institute rates Hindi Category III — about 1,100 hours to professional proficiency, harder than Spanish or French but easier than Arabic, Chinese or Japanese. Speaking comes faster than most people expect; the script and the gender system are the real work.
A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard.

What is genuinely easy

  • The script is phonetic. Every letter makes one sound and always the same sound. English cannot claim that — though, through, tough.
  • No articles. No a, an or the to worry about.
  • Shared roots. Hindi and English are both Indo-European. Naam/name, maata/mother, do/two, teen/three are not coincidences.
  • You already know hundreds of words. Everyday Hindi is full of English: station, ticket, phone, doctor, hospital, computer.

What is genuinely hard

  • Gender. Every noun is masculine or feminine, and verbs and adjectives must agree. Worse, the verb agrees with the speaker's gender in the present tense. This is the single biggest source of errors.
  • Word order. Verb last. "I bread eat" instead of "I eat bread." It takes a few months to stop translating in your head.
  • Retroflex sounds. त versus ट, द versus ड. English has nothing like the distinction, and you will not hear the difference at first — which is exactly why an app cannot teach it.
  • Postpositions. Hindi puts them after the noun, and they change the noun's form.

A realistic timeline

GoalTime
Greetings and basic politeness2–4 weeks
Reading Devanagari aloud4–8 weeks
Simple conversation4–6 months
Comfortable conversation1–2 years

Anyone promising fluency in three months is selling something.
