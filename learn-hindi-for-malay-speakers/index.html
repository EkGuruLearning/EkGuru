Learn Hindi for Malay speakers

Your first language decides which parts of Hindi are easy and which are slow, and almost no course takes account of it. This page does. It is written for someone who already speaks Malay and wants to know what that is worth before spending a year finding out.

Where the verb goes

Subject-verb-object, so Hindi's verb-final order is new. Malay's flexibility about topic placement helps a little.

What Malay gives you for free

The Sanskrit layer, which is substantial and largely unrecognised by learners: bahasa, guru, dunia, raja, cinta, kepala, negara, harta, warna, sederhana all have Hindi counterparts from the same source. Malay grammar is also famously light — no gender to unlearn, no tense conjugation to unlearn — so you are building on a clean base rather than transferring a conflicting system. The five-vowel inventory maps well onto Hindi's short vowels.

What it does not prepare you for

The whole consonant system, essentially. Malay has neither aspirated nor retroflex consonants, so ten of Hindi's thirty-three are new. And inflection: Hindi marks gender, number and formality on nouns, adjectives and verbs at once, where Malay marks almost nothing.

A word worth knowing about

guru

Looks like: teacher in both. In practice: a true friend from Sanskrit. So are bahasa, dunia, cinta and kepala. Most Malay learners never find out how many Hindi words they half-know.

A realistic first thirty days

  1. Days 1–8: consonants — aspiration and retroflex together. Ten new sounds is your real workload.
  2. Days 9–14: harvest the Sanskrit layer.
  3. Days 15–22: Devanagari.
  4. Days 23–30: inflection and gender, continuously.

Notice that the order is not the order a general course would use. That is the whole point of organising this by your first language: the sequence that works depends on what you already have.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.

Questions Malay speakers ask

Is Hindi hard for Malay speakers?

It depends entirely on what you are comparing. The Sanskrit layer, which is substantial and largely unrecognised by learners: bahasa, guru, dunia, raja, cinta, kepala, negara, harta, warna, sederhana all have Hindi counterparts from the same source. Malay grammar is also famously light — no gender to unlearn, no tense conjugation to unlearn — so you are building on a clean base rather than transferring a conflicting system. The five-vowel inventory maps well onto Hindi's short vowels.

What is the hardest part of Hindi for a Malay speaker?

The whole consonant system, essentially. Malay has neither aspirated nor retroflex consonants, so ten of Hindi's thirty-three are new. And inflection: Hindi marks gender, number and formality on nouns, adjectives and verbs at once, where Malay marks almost nothing.

Do Malay speakers need to learn Devanagari?

Not to speak. Many learners work in romanised Hindi for months. If you want to read signs, messages or anything written, the script is phonetic and consistent, and most learners read simple words within a few weeks.

How long does Hindi take from Malay?

Nobody can give you an honest number, because it depends on hours per week far more than on your first language. What this page can tell you is which parts will be quick for you and which will be slow, which is more useful than a figure.

The part you cannot do alone

Everything above can be read. The sound work in particular cannot be self-assessed — you cannot hear your own errors, which is precisely why they fossilise. A tutor is the only mechanism that fixes that, and an hour a week is enough.
