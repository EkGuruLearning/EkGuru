The Mistakes Every Learner Makes — A Checklist

A free EkGuru material · updated 2026-09-12

High beginner
What this is for

Revision is most useful when it targets the specific errors you actually make. This checklist names the ten most common learner mistakes, why each happens, and the fix — tick the ones you have beaten and work the rest.

What you will learn

  • The ten errors that most reliably mark a learner
  • Why each one happens (English interference, mostly)
  • The fix for each, with an example
  • A way to self-audit before a trip or lesson

Quick reference

#MistakeFix
1Word order: putting the verb in the middleSubject–Object–Verb: मैं किताब पढ़ता हूँ
2One word for 'you'Use aap with strangers, tum with friends, tu only when invited
3Ignoring noun genderLearn each noun with its gender; adjectives and verbs agree
4Forgetting the aspirated lettersक and ख are different letters — practise the puff of air
5Using 'of' as one wordका/की/के agree with the following noun
6Translating 'have' directlyमेरे पास — 'near me', not a have-verb
7Retroflex flattened to dentalद/ड are different — curl the tongue for ड
8Verb ending not matching the speakermale -ता / female -ती with हूँ
9Stretching short vowelsइ/उ stay short; length changes meaning
10Reading the vowel sign before the consonantThe ि sign is said after the consonant it precedes

How to use this checklist

  1. Read the ten items and tick the ones you are confident you get right every time.
  2. For the rest, work them one at a time — the linked lesson or material per row is the fastest route.
  3. Re-run this audit before a trip or a lesson; the goal is to move items from 'working on' to 'beaten'.

The pattern underneath

Almost all ten trace to one root: English habits imported into Hindi. Word order, 'you', 'have', 'of', vowel length — every one is a place where English structure is the wrong default. Noticing that is half the cure; the other half is the deliberate practice the fixes prescribe.

Practice

  1. Audit yourself: read each row aloud, produce the correct form, and only then tick it.
  2. Pick the one row you got wrong and spend a week on its linked lesson and practice lab.
  3. Record a short self-introduction and check it against rows 1, 2, 3 and 8.

Mini quiz

1. What is the root cause behind most of these mistakes?

Importing English habits — word order, pronouns, 'have', vowel length — into Hindi, where the structure is different.

2. How do you say 'I have a book' without a 'have' verb?

मेरे पास एक किताब है — 'near me is a book'.

3. Which two letters do learners flatten into one?

द (dental) and ड (retroflex) — the tongue position is the whole difference.
