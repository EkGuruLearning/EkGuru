The consonants, in Hindi's own order

Hindi groups its consonants by where the tongue makes them — back of the mouth to the lips. Each group runs unaspirated, aspirated, voiced, then voiced-aspirated. You do not need the Sanskrit names; you need to notice that क and ख are a pair, प and फ are a pair, and so on.

Matras — the vowel signs

A consonant by itself carries an implied short 'a'. To change the vowel you hang a sign (matra) on the consonant. क (ka) becomes:

One habit that saves you hours

Read a word right-to-left in sound terms: the sign after a consonant belongs to the consonant before it. किताब is k+i = ki, then t+ā = tā, then b = ba → ki-tā-b, 'book'.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Reading कि as 'kai'कि is 'ki' (short i)The ि sign sits before the consonant in writing but is spoken after it.
Ignoring the aspirated pairsक (ka) and ख (kha) are different lettersख has a puff of air; they change meaning (काल era vs खाल skin).
Forgetting the implied 'a'कम is 'kam', not 'km'A bare consonant carries a short 'a'.

Practice

  1. Sound out, then check: कमल (ka-mal, lotus), नमस्ते (na-mas-te), धन्यवाद (dhan-ya-vād).
  2. Write your first name in Devanagari using the chart, one sound at a time.
  3. Cover the Romanised column and read the chart aloud once a day for a week.

Mini quiz

1. Which sound does आ (ā) make?

A long 'a' like the 'a' in 'father' — distinct from अ, the short 'u'-like 'a'.

2. What is the difference between क and ख?

ख (kha) is aspirated — a puff of air after the k. English speakers hear them as the same sound, but they are different letters.

3. How do you write 'ki'?

कि — the ि sign is written before the consonant but pronounced after it: k + i.
