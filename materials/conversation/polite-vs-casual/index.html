Polite vs Casual — Aap, Tum, Tu in Practice

A free EkGuru material · updated 2026-09-12

Beginner
What this is for

English has one 'you'; Hindi has three, and the choice signals respect or closeness. Using tu with a stranger is genuinely rude; using aap with your own child is stiff. This sheet gives you the safe default and the rules for each.

What you will learn

  • The three levels of 'you' and who gets which
  • How the verb changes with each level
  • The safe default when you are unsure
  • What happens when you pick wrong (and how to fix it)

Quick reference

LevelFormUse forExample
आप (aap)most politestrangers, elders, anyone you respect, service situationsआप कैसे हैं? (how are you?)
तुम (tum)casualfriends, peers, family you are close to, childrenतुम कैसे हो? (how are you?)
तू (tu)intimate/roughvery close friends, children, God, or deliberate insultतू कैसा है? (how are you?)

The verb changes with the pronoun

Each 'you' takes its own verb form — you cannot just swap the word:

Englishaaptumtu
you areआप हैं (haiṃ)तुम हो (ho)तू है (hai)
you goआप जाते हैंतुम जाते होतू जाता है
come (command)आइए (āie)आओ (āo)आ (ā)

The safe default

When unsure, use aap. It is always safe — at worst you sound a little formal. The only cost of aap is a slight distance; the cost of a wrong tu can be real offence. Start formal, let the other person invite you down.

If someone says 'tum bolo' to you

They are inviting you to the casual level. You may mirror it, but you are never wrong to stay with aap.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Using tu with a waiter or shopkeeperUse aap in any service situationtu to a stranger reads as contempt.
Using tum with eldersDefault to aap for anyone clearly olderAge matters more than English speakers expect.
Swapping the pronoun but not the verbChange both together'aap hai' or 'tum haiṃ' is broken — the verb form belongs to its pronoun.

Practice

  1. Practise the three 'how are you' forms until you can produce all three on cue.
  2. Role-play greeting (a) a shopkeeper, (b) a friend, (c) a small child — with the right form each time.
  3. Listen for aap/tum/tu in any Hindi media and note who uses which with whom.

Mini quiz

1. Which 'you' should you use with a stranger?

आप (aap) — it is always safe, and the polite default.

2. What is the polite command form of 'come'?

आइए (āie) — the aap-level imperative.

3. Why must the verb change with the pronoun?

Each level of 'you' carries its own verb forms — हैं/हो/है — so they move together.
