Restaurant, Shopping & Transport Phrases

What this is for

These are the phrases you will actually say on a trip — ordering without pointing, asking a price without confusion, and getting a rickshaw where you want it. Grouped by situation so you can rehearse each one.

What you will learn

  • Ordering food and asking what things are
  • Asking prices and bargaining politely
  • Directions and transport phrases

Ordering food without pointing

मुझे … चाहिए (mujhe … chāhie, I want …) is the workhorse. Add कृपया (kripayā, please) or the aap-level verb to stay polite, and ask यह क्या है? for anything you do not recognise.

Bargaining is expected — and polite is better

In markets a quoted price is often an opening, not a final number. थोड़ा कम कीजिए (make it a little less) said with a smile works far better than an aggressive बहुत महँगा है. Bargain in good humour and walk away politely if it does not work.

Transport: the rickshaw script

The full rickshaw exchange: state the place (मुझे … जाना है, I want to go to …), agree the price first (कितना? how much?), and say यहाँ रोकिए (stop here) when you arrive. Agreeing the price before getting in avoids the whole classic misunderstanding.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Shouting 'cheaper!' in Englishथोड़ा कम कीजिए, with a smileTone is half of bargaining — good humour gets better prices.
Not agreeing the rickshaw fare firstAsk कितना? before getting inThe fare is agreed up front, not on the meter.
Using tum with a driver or vendorUse aap in every service exchangeaap is the safe, respectful default with strangers.

Practice

  1. Rehearse the full restaurant script: enter, order tea, ask what a dish is, ask for the bill.
  2. Practise the bargaining exchange aloud until थोड़ा कम कीजिए rolls off the tongue.
  3. Learn the four direction words with gestures so you can navigate without a map.

Mini quiz

1. How do you ask 'how much is this?'

यह कितने का है? (yah kitne kā hai?).

2. What should you agree before getting into a rickshaw?

The fare — ask कितना? (how much?) and agree it first.

3. Which politeness level do you use with vendors?

आप (aap) — the polite form, in every service exchange.
