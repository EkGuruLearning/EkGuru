Writing Practice — Form the Letters Yourself

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

A free EkGuru material · updated 2026-09-12

Beginner
What this is for

Writing is how the letter shapes move from your eyes to your hands — and handwriting fixes them in memory faster than reading alone. This gives you the order to learn the strokes, sentences to copy, and an honest way to check yourself.

What you will learn

  • Why writing beats reading for memorising letters
  • The stroke order for the letters learners find hardest
  • Copywork sentences that practise whole words
  • How to self-check without a teacher

Quick reference

Hard letterStart withThen
क (ka)the vertical stroke, then the hook around itadd the horizontal bar last
म (ma)the loop at the left, then the middle strokefinish with the right curve
र (ra)a short hook that sits above the line in some joinswatch it become a small stroke in conjuncts
ट (ṭa)a loop on the left, then the top barnote the dot below that marks it as retroflex
ड (ḍa)like ट but with a left-side curvethe dot below again marks the retroflex series

Why write by hand (not just type)

Typing is fast but it does not build the hand-memory that makes letters stick. Writing each letter by hand, while saying its sound, links three senses at once — and the letters you write by hand are the ones you recognise fastest later.

Copywork: whole words, not single letters

Once the letters are formed, copy whole words — the word is the unit you will actually read. Copy each of these three times, saying the sound as you write:

  • नमस्ते (namaste) · धन्यवाद (dhanyavād) · पानी (pānī) · किताब (kitāb) · दोस्त (dost)

Self-check: compare, do not guess

Without a teacher, check your writing against the printed form letter by letter — does your व have its open left, does your ब have its two bumps? Then cover the model and write the word from memory. If it matches the model, it is right; if not, that is the letter to practise again.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Writing without looking at a modelCopy a model, then write from memoryCopying fixes the form; freehand before the form is set just practises the wrong shape.
Skipping the horizontal barAdd the top bar lastThe bar joins letters into words — missing it is the commonest early slip.
Typing onlyWrite by hand daily, even brieflyHandwriting builds recognition that typing cannot.

Practice

  1. Write the five hard letters ten times each, in the order above.
  2. Copy the five copywork words three times each, saying the sound.
  3. Write your name and one full sentence from the reading material in Devanagari.

Mini quiz

1. Why does handwriting help more than typing?

It builds hand-memory and links sight, sound and motion — the letters you write by hand are the ones you recognise fastest.

2. What do the dots under ट and ड mark?

The retroflex series — the tongue curls back for these sounds.

3. What is the correct self-check method?

Compare against the printed model letter by letter, then write from memory — not freehand guessing.
