Reading Devanagari in a Week — A Practical Plan

A free EkGuru material · updated 2026-09-12

Beginner
What this is for

Reading the script is the single biggest unlock in Hindi — everything you look at stops being decoration. This is a one-week plan, not a promise: a week gets you sounding words out, not reading novels.

What you will learn

  • Why Devanagari is easier than it looks (it is phonetic)
  • A seven-day order that respects how the letters actually build words
  • The two habits that stop learners getting stuck
  • Ten practice words with the sounding-out shown

Quick reference

5श ष स ह + the matrasRead the matra table; write ka, kā, ki, kī, ku, kū, ke, ko

Two habits that do the work

  • Sound, never memorise. Devanagari is phonetic — every letter is one sound, every time. You are not memorising 600 spellings, you are learning 45 sounds and combining them.
  • Read the vowel after the consonant. In किताब the ि comes before the क in writing but is said after it: ki-tā-b. This one habit fixes most early misreadings.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Waiting to 'know' the alphabet before readingStart sounding out words on day oneYou learn letters by meeting them in words, not by memorising the chart cold.
Reading स्कूल as 'sa-ka-ula'The halant ् joins the s to the k: 'skūl'The halant strips a letter's vowel and glues it to the next.
Expecting silent lettersEvery letter sounds, every timeThere is no English-style silent 'e' — that is the point of a phonetic script.

Practice

  1. Day 1–3: copy each day's letters five times while saying the sound.
  2. Day 4–6: sound out the ten words daily, timed; aim under two minutes by day seven.
  3. Day 7: write your name and three words you see around you (signs, packaging, your phone).

Mini quiz

1. Why is Devanagari easier to read than English spelling?

It is phonetic — one letter, one sound, every time. English spelling is historical and irregular; Devanagari spelling follows speech.

2. What does the halant (्) do?

It removes a consonant's implied 'a' and joins it to the next letter, as in स्कूल (skūl).
