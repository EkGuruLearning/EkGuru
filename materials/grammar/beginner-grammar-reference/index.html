The verb goes last

English says I read a book; Hindi says I book read — मैं किताब पढ़ता हूँ. Almost every sentence you build is Subject, then Object, then Verb. Once this feels natural, the rest of the grammar slots into place around it.

Gender: the two endings that matter

Most masculine nouns end in आ (-ā): लड़का. Most feminine nouns end in ई (-ī): लड़की. Words ending in a consonant are usually masculine, but there are common exceptions (किताब, book, is feminine) — learn those as you meet them.

Postpositions come after

Where English puts a word before the noun (in the house), Hindi puts a short word after it: घर में (ghar meṃ, house-in). They are called postpositions for exactly that reason, and का/की/के (of) is the one that changes to agree with the noun it belongs to.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Copying English word orderPut the verb lastSOV is the frame; English SVO sentences sound wrong even when every word is right.
Guessing gender from meaningLearn gender from the ending, then the exceptionsThere is no logic to why किताब is feminine — you just learn it.
Using 'of' as one wordका / की / के agree with the following nounलड़के की किताब (the boy's book) — की because किताब is feminine.

Practice

  1. Write five sentences about your day in SOV order, then check each verb is last.
  2. Take ten nouns from the vocabulary list and write their plurals.
  3. Make each adjective agree: अच्छा घर, अच्छी किताब, अच्छे लड़के.

Mini quiz

1. Put in Hindi order: 'I drink water'.

मैं पानी पीता हूँ (main pānī pītā hū̃) — Subject, Object, Verb.

2. What is the plural of लड़का?

लड़के (laṛke) — masculine nouns in -ā change to -e.

3. Why is it 'ladke kī kitāb' and not 'ladke kā kitāb'?

Because का/की/के agrees with the noun that follows — किताब is feminine, so की.
