Sentence Patterns That Cover Most of What You Say

Beginner
What this is for

Grammar is easier when it arrives as whole usable frames rather than rules. Learn these ten patterns and you can build hundreds of sentences by swapping one word — the fastest honest route to speaking.

What you will learn

  • Ten high-frequency sentence frames
  • The verb forms each frame needs (hū̃/hai, chāhie, pasand, saknā)
  • How to swap in your own words
  • The male/female endings that change with the speaker

Three frames that need 'mujhe' (to me)

Hindi expresses wanting, liking and needing as things that happen to you: मुझे चाहिए (to me … wanted). You do not say 'I want' with the verb 'to want' the way English does — you use चाहिए (chāhie) or पसंद (pasand) with मुझे. This one frame covers want, like, need and feel.

Gender follows the speaker

In मैं सीख रहा हूँ the ending रहा (rahā) is for a male speaker; a female speaker says रही (rahī): मैं सीख रही हूँ. The verb's ending tracks the speaker's own gender — a feature English has no equivalent for, so it needs a moment of attention.

Questions need no reordering

Hindi questions keep the same word order as statements — you add a question word (कहाँ where, कितना how much, क्या whether) or just say it with rising intonation. No do-support, no inversion.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Saying 'main chāhtā hū̃ chāy'मुझे चाय चाहिएWanting uses the 'to me' frame, not a direct verb.
Keeping one verb ending for everyoneChange rahā/rahī with the speakerA woman saying रहा sounds off; agreement tracks the speaker.
Inverting for questionsKeep statement order, add the question wordHindi questions are statements with a question word or a rising tone.

Practice

  1. Take the ten frames and build three sentences from each, swapping in words from the vocabulary material.
  2. Say the 'mujhe' frames about real things: मुझे चाय चाहिए, मुझे किताब चाहिए, मुझे पानी चाहिए.
  3. Ask three real questions aloud using कहाँ and कितने का.

Mini quiz

1. Why do we say 'mujhe chāhie' instead of a direct 'I want' verb?

Wanting, liking and needing are expressed as things that happen 'to me' — the मुझे frame. It is the natural Hindi pattern.

2. A woman says 'I am learning'. What ending does the verb take?

सीख रही हूँ (sīkh rahī hū̃) — the -rahī ending agrees with the female speaker.

3. How do you form 'Can I sit here?'

क्या मैं यहाँ बैठ सकता हूँ? — using सकना (to be able to).
