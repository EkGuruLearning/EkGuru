Hindi Postpositions — ka, ko, se, meṃ, par

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

A free EkGuru material · updated 2026-09-12

Beginner
What this is for

English puts little words before nouns (in, on, for, with); Hindi puts them after. These postpositions hold most sentences together, and getting them right removes a whole class of early errors.

What you will learn

  • The ten postpositions you will use constantly
  • How का/के/की changes to agree
  • Which postpositions pair with which verbs
  • Common English-to-Hindi traps (like 'to have')

Quick reference

PostpositionMeaningExample
का / के / की (kā/ke/kī)of / 'sलड़के की किताब — the boy's book
को (ko)to (a person); marks some objectsमुझे चाय चाहिए — I want tea
से (se)from, by, with (instrument)दिल्ली से — from Delhi
में (meṃ)in, insideघर में — in the house
पर (par)on, atमेज पर — on the table
के लिए (ke liye)forआपके लिए — for you
के साथ (ke sāth)with (a person)दोस्त के साथ — with a friend
के बारे में (ke bāre meṃ)aboutहिन्दी के बारे में — about Hindi
तक (tak)until, up toकल तक — until tomorrow
के पास (ke pās)near; possession ('have')मेरे पास — I have (with me)

The one that agrees: kā / ke / kī

का/के/की means 'of', but which form you use depends on the gender and number of the noun that follows it — not the one before it:

Following nounFormExample
masculine singularकालड़के का घर (the boy's house)
feminineकीलड़के की किताब (the boy's book)
masculine pluralकेलड़कों के घर (the boys' house)

'To have' does not exist as a verb

Hindi has no single verb 'to have'. Instead it says ke pās (near/with): मेरे पास एक किताब है — 'near me is a book' = I have a book. This is the single most useful postposition habit to internalise.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Saying 'mujhe' for 'I have'मेरे पास for possessionमुझे is 'to me' (I feel/want); मेरे पास is 'I have'.
Using one kā for everythingAgree का/की/के with the next nounThe form follows the noun that comes after it.
Translating 'on' and 'in' word for wordLearn the verb+postposition pair'Think about' is के बारे में सोचना — the pairing, not the word, is what to learn.

Practice

  1. Write 'the boy's book', 'the girl's house', 'the boys' books' using the right form of kā.
  2. Say five true things about your room using में and पर (the book is on the table, the phone is in the bag).
  3. Translate 'I have a question' without using any word for 'have'.

Mini quiz

1. Why is it 'ladke kī kitāb'?

Because का/की/के agrees with the following noun — किताब is feminine.

2. How do you say 'I have a pen'?

मेरे पास एक कलम है (mere pās ek kalam hai) — 'near me is a pen'.
