Hindi Sounds English Speakers Get Wrong

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

A free EkGuru material · updated 2026-09-12

Beginner
What this is for

Hindi has a handful of sounds English does not. Get these six pairs right and your Hindi stops sounding like an accent and starts sounding like Hindi — this is the highest-value pronunciation work there is.

What you will learn

  • The aspirated vs plain consonant pairs
  • The retroflex vs dental split (ट/त, ड/द)
  • Why the short/long vowel length matters
  • Minimal pairs to practise each contrast aloud

Aspirated consonants: the puff of air

Hold a hand in front of your mouth and say pin, then spin. The 'p' in pin has a puff of air; the one in spin does not. Hindi treats those two as different letters across k, c, t, p. English speakers usually produce the plain sound and lose the distinction — which changes words.

Retroflex: the tongue curls back

For ट ठ ड ढ ण the tongue tip curls up and back to touch the roof of the mouth. For त थ द ध न it stays at the teeth, like the English 't' in 'ten'. English has no retroflex series, so every learner flattens them at first — दाल (lentils) and डाल (branch) must sound different.

Why it matters

पानी is water, but a retroflex-flavoured पाणी is a completely different word. A small tongue movement changes what you said.

Vowel length is real

Hindi distinguishes short and long vowels: इ/ई, उ/ऊ, and the short 'a' in कम (kam, less) versus the long in काम (kām, work). Lengthening or shortening a vowel can change the word, so do not borrow English's habit of stretching vowels for emphasis.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Saying 'kam' and 'khaam' interchangeablyKeep क plain and ख aspiratedThe puff of air is the whole difference between two letters.
Flattening ड into दCurl the tongue back for डEnglish has no retroflex sounds, so this needs deliberate practice, not just listening.
Stretching short vowels for emphasisKeep इ/उ short even when excitedVowel length is phonemic in Hindi; stretching it changes the word.

Practice

  1. Say each minimal pair aloud, hand in front of your mouth for the aspirated sounds — you should feel the puff on the second word.
  2. Record yourself on your phone saying the six pairs; listen back the next day and spot the flattened ones.
  3. Practise the retroflex series ट ठ ड ढ with your tongue curled back until it stops feeling strange.

Mini quiz

1. What is the difference between प and फ?

फ (pha) is aspirated — it carries a puff of air that प (pa) does not. पल means 'moment', फल means 'fruit'.

2. Where does the tongue go for a retroflex consonant?

The tip curls up and back to the roof of the mouth — not at the teeth. That is what makes ड different from द.

3. Is vowel length important in Hindi?

Yes — it is phonemic. कम (kam, less) and काम (kām, work) differ only in vowel length.
