Family Words Quick Reference

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear Hindi spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

A free EkGuru material · updated 2026-09-12

Beginner
What this is for

Hindi family vocabulary encodes information English collapses — which side of the family, and older or younger. This table is the fastest way to learn the words you will actually need with relatives, and to see the logic behind them.

What you will learn

  • The core family terms with Romanisation
  • Why Hindi distinguishes father's side from mother's side
  • The older/younger distinction for siblings
  • The polite terms you will use with elders

Quick reference

RelationHindiNote

fatherपिता pitā (or पापा pāpā, informal)pitā is formal, pāpā affectionate
brother (older)बड़ा भाई baṛā bhāīolder brother — order matters in Hindi
brother (younger)छोटा भाई chhoṭā bhāīyounger brother
sister (older)बड़ी बहन baṛī bahanolder sister

grandfather (father's)दादा dādāpaternal grandfather

Why so many words for cousin?

English has one word, 'cousin'. Hindi marks which parent the cousin comes through and often the gender and age — चचेरा भाई (father's brother's son) is not ममेरा भाई (mother's brother's son). The family tree in Hindi carries information the English tree leaves out.

Respect is built into the vocabulary

You address elders with the respectful forms — दादा जी, चाचा जी, adding जी (jī) as a mark of respect — and you refer to them with aap, never tum or tu. The grammar and the family vocabulary run on the same respect system.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Using one word for all unclesLearn chāchā vs māmāThe father's side and mother's side use different words.
Forgetting older/younger for siblingsbaṛā/baṛī = older, chhoṭā/chhoṭī = youngerAge order is part of the word, not an extra adjective.
Using tum with eldersUse aap and add jīFamily elders get the polite forms.

Practice

  1. Draw your own family tree and label each person in Hindi.
  2. Say each term aloud, then test yourself from the English column.
  3. Describe your family in five sentences using the terms and the sentence patterns material.

Mini quiz

1. What is the difference between chāchā and māmā?

चाचा is your father's brother; मामा is your mother's brother — Hindi marks the side of the family.

2. How do you say 'older brother'?

बड़ा भाई (baṛā bhāī).

3. Why does Hindi have twelve words for cousin?

Because it marks which parent the cousin comes through, plus gender and often age — information English collapses into one word.
