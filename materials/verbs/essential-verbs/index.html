25 Essential Hindi Verbs — With Their Present Forms

🔊 Tap any speaker button to hear the words on this page spoken. Too fast or slow? Use the speed button at the bottom-right of the page.

A free EkGuru material · updated 2026-09-12

Beginner
What this is for

A handful of verbs carries an enormous amount of everyday Hindi. Learn these 25 in the present tense and you can talk about your day, your plans, and what you are doing right now.

What you will learn

  • 25 core verbs with meaning
  • The present 'I' form for each (masculine and feminine)
  • The pattern -tā/-tī that most present verbs follow
  • Which verbs pair with postpositions like ko and se

The pattern behind the table

Almost every present-tense 'I' form is the verb stem plus -tā hū̃ for a male speaker and -tī hū̃ for a female speaker. खाना → खा + ता/ती + हूँ. Learn the pattern once and the whole table folds into it.

Two verbs that break the pattern — and why

honā (to be) is irregular: मैं हूँ, वह है, वे हैं, तुम हो. And pasand (like) is not a verb at all but an adjective used with मुझे: मुझे हिन्दी पसंद है. These two deserve to be learned as their own small facts.

Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Using one ending for both genders-tā for male, -tī for female speakersThe verb ending tracks the speaker's gender, not the listener's.
Treating 'pasand' as a normal verbUse मुझे … पसंद हैLiking is an adjective frame, not a conjugated verb.
Forgetting that milnā means 'to be found' tooमुझे मिला (I got/found it)milnā works both ways — meeting and receiving.

Practice

  1. Say what you do each day using five of these verbs in the present tense.
  2. Run the verbs practice lab until the -tā/-tī pattern is automatic.
  3. Write three sentences about a friend using the he/she forms (वह खाता है / वह खाती है).

Mini quiz

1. A woman says 'I drink water'. How does the verb end?

मैं पानी पीती हूँ — -tī hū̃ for a female speaker.

2. Why is 'pasand karnā' listed differently from other verbs?

It is used as an adjective frame — मुझे … पसंद है — not conjugated like a normal verb.

3. What is the present 'I' form of जाना (to go) for a man?

मैं जाता हूँ (main jātā hū̃).
