Cookie Policy

Last updated 18 September 2026 · Covers https://ekguru.shop/ and every page on it.

What a cookie is

A cookie is a small text file a website asks your browser to keep and send back with later requests to the same site. It is how a site remembers something between one page and the next: that you chose dark mode, that you are logged in, that a shopping cart has two items in it.

Two things about them matter here, because most of the fear about cookies belongs to the second one:

  • First-party cookies are set by the site you are visiting. They are read only by that site.
  • Third-party cookies are set by other companies whose code runs inside the page — advertising and analytics networks, mostly. These are the ones that can follow you from site to site, and these are the ones that need your consent in the EU and the UK.

Local storage is the modern cousin of the first kind: same idea, bigger drawer, and it is never sent to a server automatically. EkGuru uses local storage, not cookies.

The short version

  • EkGuru sets no cookies at all — not one, on any page.
  • Preferences and progress are stored in your browser's local storage and stay on your device. They are not transmitted to EkGuru or to anyone else.
  • There is no account, no login, no mailing list and no database of visitors on this website.
  • Advertising is served by Google AdSense. Google and its partners set advertising cookies, which is why this page exists.
  • Where consent is legally required, it is collected through Google Privacy & messaging or another Google-certified platform — a real consent tool, not a decorative banner.

Advertising cookies

EkGuru is free to use and may display advertising to keep it that way. When advertising is enabled, third-party vendors — including Google — use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this or other websites. Those cookies are set by the advertising network, not by EkGuru, and we do not receive the advertising profiles Google builds from them.

  • Google (AdSense / Ad Manager) — sets cookies such as _gcl_au, __gads, __gpi and IDE/DSID to cap how often you see an ad, to measure ad performance, and (with consent) to select ads based on your interests.
  • Other ad vendors — a network may serve some inventory through other partners, who set their own cookies under their own policies.
  • Consent platform — where Google Privacy & messaging or another certified consent tool is active, it stores your consent choices (a cookie or local storage entry) so the site does not ask you again on every page.

You can opt out of personalised advertising at Google Ads Settings, read how Google uses advertising data at Google's Ads & Privacy page, and opt out of many vendors at once at aboutads.info/choices or, in Europe, at youronlinechoices.eu. These are independent services; opting out does not remove ads, it stops them being personalised.

Consent, and what changes when you allow

In the EU, the UK and Switzerland, advertising and analytics cookies may only be set after you consent. Before that, Google runs in a consent-aware mode: non-essential storage is withheld and only a limited, non-personalised form of ad serving runs, if any.

  • If you consent — ad personalisation, and the measurement that goes with it, is switched on. You can withdraw at any time from the consent tool's link on the page, and the withdrawal is stored the same way the consent was.
  • If you do nothing — nothing non-essential is stored. The guides, tools, lessons and worksheets all still work, because none of them depend on a cookie.
  • If you refuse — you keep everything on the site; you simply get ads that are not based on your interests.

The small notice that points at this page only acknowledges the policy: it is not a consent tool, it does not claim to be one, and it does not replace a certified consent platform. Consent for advertising is collected — where the law requires it — by Google Privacy & messaging or another Google-certified platform. Account-side activation and geographic testing of that platform are still marked review required in our monetization configuration.

Analytics

EkGuru counts visits with a privacy-first, cookieless provider (GoatCounter or Plausible). Neither sets a cookie and neither stores personal data, so neither requires a consent banner. If no provider is configured — which is the shipped default — nothing at all is loaded and no request is made. The do-not-track switch stops even that.

Booking, forms and other services

A few things on this site talk to services outside it, and they can set their own cookies when you use them, under their own policies:

  • Lesson booking — a booking calendar opens from cal.com only when you click to schedule a lesson, and it sets its own cookies for that session.
  • Contact form — the form posts through formsubmit.co, which delivers the message to our inbox. It is a form submission, not a tracker, and it does not follow you around the web.

How to control cookies

  • Your browser — every major browser can block third-party cookies, and can clear site data per site. Blocking them does not break anything here; the only things that stop working are the ads' personalisation and, on some pages, the booking calendar.
  • Your consent choices — change or withdraw them in the consent tool when it is shown, or through the privacy settings of your Google account for Google-served ads.
  • Your local storage — "clear site data" in your browser removes every entry in the table above, including your progress. There is nothing to delete on our side, because nothing was ever sent to us.

Changes

If what this site stores changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. A new purpose is never added quietly: it appears here, in plain language, before it is switched on.
