Learn Hindi for Portuguese speakers

Your first language decides which parts of Hindi are easy and which are slow, and almost no course takes account of it. This page does. It is written for someone who already speaks Portuguese and wants to know what that is worth before spending a year finding out.

Where the verb goes

Subject-verb-object, so Hindi's verb-final order is new. Portuguese's flexible clitic placement is mild preparation, nothing more.

What Portuguese gives you for free

Nasal vowels — Portuguese has the richest nasal system of any major European language, and Hindi's ं and ँ will present no difficulty at all. Add the Spanish-family advantages: clean vowels, a tapped r, grammatical gender, and verbs that already agree with their subject.

What it does not prepare you for

Aspiration, absent in Portuguese as in Spanish. And one habit specific to Brazilian Portuguese: palatalising t and d before i, so dia becomes jia. Carry that into Hindi and दिन becomes something a Hindi speaker hears as a different word. European Portuguese has a different problem — reducing unstressed vowels almost to nothing, where Hindi keeps every vowel full.

A word worth knowing about

चाबी (chaabi)

Looks like: sounds like chave. In practice: it IS chave — Portuguese gave Hindi this word during five centuries of contact in Goa. Along with तौलिया from toalha and several more. A rare case of a true friend.

A realistic first thirty days

  1. Days 1–4: confirm your vowels and nasals are already right, then move on.
  2. Days 5–12: aspiration, plus the anti-palatalisation drill if you speak Brazilian Portuguese.
  3. Days 13–22: script.
  4. Days 23–30: verb-final order and the ने construction.

Notice that the order is not the order a general course would use. That is the whole point of organising this by your first language: the sequence that works depends on what you already have.

A note on the Hindi spellings

Devanagari is easy to get slightly wrong when it is typed by hand — a missing matra, the wrong nukta. If something here looks off to you, trust a native speaker over this page and please tell us so it gets fixed — it takes a moment and a real person reads it. Romanised spellings are a guide to the sound, not a standard: there is no single official way to write Hindi in Latin letters.

Questions Portuguese speakers ask

Is Hindi hard for Portuguese speakers?

It depends entirely on what you are comparing. Nasal vowels — Portuguese has the richest nasal system of any major European language, and Hindi's ं and ँ will present no difficulty at all. Add the Spanish-family advantages: clean vowels, a tapped r, grammatical gender, and verbs that already agree with their subject.

What is the hardest part of Hindi for a Portuguese speaker?

Aspiration, absent in Portuguese as in Spanish. And one habit specific to Brazilian Portuguese: palatalising t and d before i, so dia becomes jia. Carry that into Hindi and दिन becomes something a Hindi speaker hears as a different word. European Portuguese has a different problem — reducing unstressed vowels almost to nothing, where Hindi keeps every vowel full.

Do Portuguese speakers need to learn Devanagari?

Not to speak. Many learners work in romanised Hindi for months. If you want to read signs, messages or anything written, the script is phonetic and consistent, and most learners read simple words within a few weeks.

How long does Hindi take from Portuguese?

Nobody can give you an honest number, because it depends on hours per week far more than on your first language. What this page can tell you is which parts will be quick for you and which will be slow, which is more useful than a figure.

The part you cannot do alone

Everything above can be read. The sound work in particular cannot be self-assessed — you cannot hear your own errors, which is precisely why they fossilise. A tutor is the only mechanism that fixes that, and an hour a week is enough.
